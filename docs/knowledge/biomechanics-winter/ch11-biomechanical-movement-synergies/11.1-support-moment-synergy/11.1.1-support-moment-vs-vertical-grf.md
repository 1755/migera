# 11.1.1 Relationship between Ms and the vertical ground reaction force

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §11.1.1, pp. 285–286 ·
> [open PDF at p. 285](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=298) ·
> Up: [11.1 The support moment synergy](./INDEX.md)

The support moment is the summed extensor effort of the stance limb, so it
measures how hard the limb pushes away from the ground. Its averaged profile
matches the vertical ground reaction force almost exactly (r = 0.97). The
limb's extensor synergy is effectively what produces the vertical-force
double hump.

## Key ideas

- Ms (hip + knee + ankle extensor moments, see [11.1](./INDEX.md)) has the
  same **double-hump** shape as the vertical GRF $F_y$. The first peak is
  weight acceptance after heel contact, then a mid-stance trough, then the
  second peak at push-off.
- Winter (1991) correlated averaged Ms and $F_y$ profiles for three cadence
  groups (natural, natural + 20 and natural − 20 steps/min). The match
  survives cadence changes and even abnormal joint-moment patterns in
  pathological gait.
- Because the individual joint moments are highly variable
  ([11.1](./INDEX.md)) while Ms tracks $F_y$, the whole-limb synergy, not any
  one joint, is what is regulated against the ground load.

## Equations and numbers

Correlations between averaged Ms and $F_y$ profiles:

| Group | n | r |
|---|---|---|
| Natural cadence | 19 | **0.97** |
| Fast cadence (natural + 20) | 19 | 0.95 |
| Slow cadence (natural − 20) | 17 | 0.90 |
| 69-year-old female, total knee replacement | 2 trials | 0.96 |
| 69-year-old male, knee replacement | 2 trials | 0.92 |

Read off **Fig. 11.3** (natural cadence, N = 19, stance = 0–~60% stride),
approximately:

- $F_y$ (left axis, N/kg): rises from ~0.5 at heel contact to a first peak
  of **~10.8 N/kg (≈ 1.1 × body weight)** near 14% of stride. It dips to
  **~7.1 N/kg (≈ 0.72 BW)** near 29%, reaches a second peak of ~10.8 N/kg
  near 47%, and falls to ~0 by ~62%.
- Ms (right axis, N·m/kg, scaled 1:10 against $F_y$): first peak ~1.1 near
  15%, trough ~0.68 near 30–35%, second peak ~1.08 near 50%. It dips
  slightly negative (≈ −0.15) just before toe-off at ~60%.

(The book gives only the r values in text. The peak and trough values are
read off the figure to about ±0.3 N/kg and ±2% stride.)

## Relevance to migera

- **Vertical bob / stance-load shape.** Vertical GRF is body weight plus
  mass × vertical COM acceleration. The double hump means the COM
  *decelerates downward* hard after heel strike and again during push-off,
  and is "light" (≈ 0.72 BW, accelerating down) at mid-stance while it vaults
  over the stance leg. Any procedural pelvis-height curve, and the head bob in
  `phase.rs`, should produce this pattern: highest COM at mid-stance, lowest
  in double support. The head bob already runs at twice stride rate, which
  matches two humps per stride. What should be checked is its *phase*: the
  low point belongs at the footfall/double support, the high point at
  mid-stance. The COM-height interpretation is standard mechanics
  applied to the book's curve, not a claim the section makes.
- **Ragdoll support check.** For an unpinned, physics-driven stance leg, the
  extensor-summed PD torque should rise and fall with the stance leg's
  contact force. A cheap live check over BRP would read the leg's contact
  impulse and the summed extensor torque and verify they correlate. A near-zero
  or negative correlation means the leg is not actually supporting the body.
  Today the ragdoll root is kinematically pinned and gravity is scaled by
  strength (`ragdoll_plugin.rs`), so this only matters once the root is
  released.
- **Test fixture:** Appendix A's walking-trial moments and force-plate data
  can reproduce Ms and $F_y$ for one subject, giving known-input,
  known-shape data to check a sign-converting torque summer.
