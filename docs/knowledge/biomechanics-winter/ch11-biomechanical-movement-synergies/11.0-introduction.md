# 11.0 Introduction

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §11.0, pp. 281–282 ·
> [open PDF at p. 281](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=294) ·
> Up: [Chapter 11 — Biomechanical movement synergies](./INDEX.md)

A single joint's moment profile cannot be interpreted on its own: its purpose
only shows up when the whole body's contribution to a task goal is examined.
Winter defines a **synergy** as muscles collaborating toward a common goal,
and insists that the goal *and* the time window of collaboration be stated
before a synergy can be identified.

## Key ideas

- **Why whole-body analysis.** The neuro-musculo-skeletal system is so
  integrated that one variable at one joint is nearly uninterpretable. The
  first three "levels of integration" of §1.2 produce a joint's moment
  profile; the reason for that profile only appears at the task level.
- **One muscle group, several subtasks.** During a movement a muscle group
  can serve more than one goal at once. In gait the subtasks are
  complementary or competitive: muscles generate and absorb energy while also
  controlling balance and preventing vertical collapse (Winter, 1991).
- **Three quick examples the chapter cites:**
  1. *Frontal-plane balance in walking* (MacKinnon & Winter, 1993): hip
     abductors act **proactively** against gravitational and inertial loads on
     the HAT (head-arms-trunk) to keep it nearly vertical during single support.
  2. *Anticipatory responses to arm movements* (Eng et al., 1992): hip, knee
     and ankle moments respond **in anticipation**, with polarity matched to the
     shoulder moment. A shoulder **flexor** moment brings a *posterior*
     postural response (hip extensors, knee flexors, ankle plantarflexors); a
     shoulder **extensor** moment brings an *anterior* one (hip flexors, knee
     extensors, ankle dorsiflexors).
  3. *M/L perturbations of the upper body in standing* (Rietdyk et al., 1999):
     identifies whole-body recovery mechanisms.
- The chapter's method is to use kinetic (moment) and EMG profiles together
  to reveal these patterns. The worked cases are the support moment (§11.1),
  standing balance (§11.2) and dynamic balance in walking (§11.3).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 281 (PDF 294): the argument for whole-body analysis, and examples 1–2.
- p. 282 (PDF 295): example 2's polarity rule, example 3, gait subtasks,
  the synergy definition. §11.1 starts on the same page.

## Relevance to migera

- The anticipatory-response rule (example 2) is a ready recipe for secondary
  motion in `src/character/anim`. When an arm gesture or reach applies a
  shoulder flexion moment, the legs and trunk should shift the body
  *backward* a little, before or with the arm. An extension moment gets the
  opposite shift. The kinematic stack has no torques, so this would be
  authored as a small whole-body lean opposite to the arm's reaction.
  Starting the lean slightly *before* the arm move is what makes it read as
  anticipatory.
- The "state the goal and the window" rule is also a verification rule for
  the active ragdoll (`ragdoll.rs`). A per-joint tracking error can look
  erratic while the task-level quantity (for example total leg extension
  support, §11.1) is steady. Assert on the task-level quantity.
