# 11.3.2 Initiation of gait

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §11.3.2, pp. 290–293 ·
> [open PDF at p. 290](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=303) ·
> Up: [11.3 Dynamic balance during walking](./INDEX.md)

To start walking, the body must first get the COM moving forward and over
toward the stance leg. The inverted pendulum only allows that one way: move
the COP the **opposite** way first. So the first visible event of gait
initiation is a small shift of the COP *backward and toward the swing leg*,
before any foot lifts. This is the **release phase**. The COP then sweeps
quickly to the stance foot, the swing foot lifts, and within about one step
the COM is on its steady-walking track.

## Key ideas

Sequence (Jian et al., 1993; Fig. 11.8). The right leg swings first, the left
is the stance leg, and times are % of the first gait cycle, 0% = right
toe-off (RTO):

1. **Quiet standing** (−69%). COP and COM are together between the feet.
2. **Release phase** (−69% → −20%). The COP moves **posteriorly and toward
   the swing (right) limb**.
   - *Posterior* shift: a sudden **decrease in plantarflexor activity**.
   - *Lateral* shift toward the swing limb: the swing limb is momentarily
     **loaded**, with swing-limb abductors up and stance-limb abductors down.
   - By Eq. 11.3 this COP offset accelerates the COM **forward and toward the
     stance limb**, which is the goal.
   - Herman et al. (1973) had already plotted the brief swing-limb force
     increase without commenting on it.
3. **Weight transfer** (−20% → 0%). The COP moves **rapidly laterally to the
   stance foot**. Stance-limb abductors turn on and swing-limb abductors turn
   off. Swing-limb hip flexors and knee extensors accelerate the swing leg up
   and forward.
4. **First single support** (0% at RTO → 35%). The COP is under the left foot,
   controlled by its plantarflexors. The COM has moved **~6 cm** forward by
   RTO and is still accelerating forward, now *away* from the stance foot.
   Stance plantarflexor activity rises, driving the COP forward to an initial
   push-off.
5. **First right heel contact (RHC1) at 35%**, then double support. The COP
   moves very rapidly forward to the right foot. The COM is now **~25 cm**
   forward and heading along the medial border of the right foot, which is
   the steady-state pattern of Fig. 11.7, reached **by the end of the first
   step**.

- **A/P and M/L muscles must fully collaborate**: plantar/dorsiflexors for
  A/P, hip abductors/adductors for M/L. The goal is to reach the steady COP/COM
  pattern in as short a time as possible (about two steps).
- **Model validity:** the COP − COM vs COM acceleration correlation averaged
  **−0.94** in both A/P and M/L during initiation.
- **Scaling with speed** (Halliday et al., 1998): young, fit elderly and
  Parkinson's subjects show the *same temporal pattern*, scaled by their final
  walking speed (young fastest, Parkinson's slowest). All the significant
  group differences vanished after dividing by walking velocity. The synergy
  is intact, and a "tonic gain" falls with age and disease.

## Equations and numbers

| Event | COP time (% first cycle) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet standing | −69% | start of analysis |
| End of release (max posterior/lateral COP) | −20% | COP farthest back and toward the swing limb |
| Right toe-off (RTO) | 0% | COP under stance (left) foot, COM ≈ 6 cm forward |
| Right heel contact (RHC1) | 35% | COM ≈ 25 cm forward |

- The inverted-pendulum relation used is Eq. 11.3,
  $\mathrm{COP}-\mathrm{COM}=-K\ddot{x}$. The arrows in Fig. 11.8 are the
  COP→COM vectors, i.e. COM acceleration directions.
- Release amplitude, **read off Fig. 11.8** (not stated in the text,
  ±1 cm): the COP moves about **4 cm backward** and about **2 cm toward the
  swing foot** from its quiet-standing position. It then crosses about
  **9–10 cm laterally** to the stance foot by RTO. The figure's COM tick marks
  suggest smaller forward COM travel than the text's 6 cm and 25 cm; trust
  the text.
- The −69% → 0% span is almost 70% of a gait cycle, roughly 0.7 s at a
  ~1 s stride. The release is a slow, visible preparation, not a one-frame
  event. The stride-time conversion is an estimate; the book gives only %.

## Relevance to migera

- **Implemented (2026-09-29) in `transition.rs`**: a 0.5 s release
  (`Transition::release`, posed by `apply_release`: `stance::shift_weight`
  onto the stance leg plus a `RELEASE_LEAN` of 4 cm forward, scaled by
  speed), then the gait fades in from the swinging leg's mid-swing.
  Measured live: pelvis 4.5 cm onto the stance foot and 2.9 cm forward with
  the feet still, and the pelvis ~23 cm forward by the first heel contact
  (Winter: ~25 cm). The first step's slide and the skimming first swing
  were fixed afterwards: see
  [fade a gait only through single support](../../../character-animation/ik-and-locomotion/fade-a-gait-only-through-single-support.md)
  and [foot locks need the body's travel](../../../character-animation/ik-and-locomotion/foot-locks-need-the-bodys-travel.md).
  The release first exposed [a lagging pelvis rotation slides planted feet](../../../character-animation/ik-and-locomotion/a-lagging-pelvis-rotation-slides-planted-feet.md).
- **Starting to walk needs an anticipatory postural adjustment.**
  Before the change above, `transition.rs` started a walk by ramping the gait
  weight in over `blend_seconds` (0.25 s) with no preparatory phase. The
  book says a natural start has one, and it runs in this order:
  1. The pelvis/COM shifts **toward the stance leg** and the body begins
     tipping **forward** *before* the swing foot lifts. In kinematic terms: a
     short lateral pelvis translation onto the stance leg and a slight
     forward lean from the ankles, over a few tenths of a second.
  2. Only then does the swing foot lift. The first step is taken by the leg
     that was *unloaded* during the shift.
  3. The first step reaches normal walking within about one step, so the
     gait weight can ramp to 1 across the first step.
  A start without step 1 looks like the foot "pulls" the body along.
- **Choose the first swing leg** from the current weight distribution. The
  leg carrying less weight after the release swings first. With a standing
  idle that shifts weight (`phase.rs`), starting with the currently
  unweighted leg skips most of the release.
- **Scale by target speed.** The same timing pattern, with amplitudes scaled
  by the final walking speed, is the book's own evidence that one
  parameterized start transition can serve all speeds.
- **Physics ragdoll:** the release shows you cannot start forward motion by
  pushing the COM forward directly. The support must first move *behind* the
  COM (reduce ankle plantarflexor torque) so gravity tips the body. Any
  balance controller for an unpinned ragdoll must allow this counterintuitive
  first move, i.e. a temporary COP–COM error of the "wrong" sign.
