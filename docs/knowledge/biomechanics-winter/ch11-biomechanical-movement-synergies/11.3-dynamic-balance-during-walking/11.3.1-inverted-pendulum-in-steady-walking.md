# 11.3.1 The human inverted pendulum in steady state walking

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §11.3.1, pp. 289–291 ·
> [open PDF at p. 289](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=302) ·
> Up: [11.3 Dynamic balance during walking](./INDEX.md)

Walking is statically unstable all the time. The COM never passes over the
base of support of either foot: it runs just medial of each stance foot.
During each single-support phase the body is an inverted pendulum that is
falling toward where the other foot *will* land. Balance is not held by
keeping the COM over a foot. It is restored every step by **placing the
swing foot**, and a person is never more than about 400 ms from falling.

## Key ideas

- **Phases:** two single-support periods of about **40%** of the gait cycle
  each, and two short double-support periods. In double support neither foot
  is flat. The leading heel is about **20°** off horizontal and plantarflexing
  rapidly toward foot-flat. The trailing heel is well up, with weight on the
  metatarsals and toes.
- **COM path vs COP path (Fig. 11.7, top view, two steps).** The COP runs
  along each foot, heel to toe, and jumps across between feet in double
  support. The COM runs a gently weaving line along the centre of
  progression, passing **just medial of the inside border of each foot**.
- **M/L:** from left toe-off (LTO) the COM heads toward the right (stance)
  foot. The curvature of its path shows it is already being accelerated
  medially, toward the *future* position of the left foot.
- **A/P:** the COM *decelerates* while it is behind the COP (first half of
  stance) and *accelerates* once it passes ahead of the COP, moving forward
  along the foot. So COM forward velocity falls in the first half of stance
  and rises in the second half.
- The COP → COM vector sets the COM's horizontal acceleration (§5.2.9,
  Eq. 11.3), exactly as in standing, but now the COM is outside the base of
  support.
- **Control consequence:** since the stance foot cannot pull the COM back
  inside itself, the CNS's lever is the **trajectory of the swing foot**. Its
  landing position sets the next single-support period's COP and therefore
  that period's stability.

## Equations and numbers

- Single support ≈ 40% of the cycle per side. Double support is short.
- Heel-contact foot angle ≈ 20° from horizontal.
- Time margin: "never more than about **400 ms** away from falling". That is
  roughly one single-support duration at normal cadence.
- The relation is the inverted-pendulum equation of [11.2.1](../11.2-standing-balance-ml-and-ap/11.2.1-quiet-standing.md) (Eq. 11.3):
  $\mathrm{COP}-\mathrm{COM} = -K\ddot{x}$, now per stance foot.

## Relevance to migera

- **Implemented (2026-09-29): step width and sideways sway from this
  constraint.** The walk steps 13 cm wide, the narrowest at which the
  pendulum's COM still passes medial of the stance foot's inner border.
  The pelvis sways ±1.5–2.3 cm by Eq. 11.3 (`phase::WalkSway`), and the
  real-rig test asserts the COM stays 0–3 cm medial of that border. See
  [the walk's step width and sideways sway](../../../character-animation/ik-and-locomotion/walk-step-width-and-sideways-sway.md).
  Before this the feet tracked under the hip sockets (22.9 cm) and the
  pelvis did not move sideways.
- **Forward speed is not constant within a step.** The COM slows in early
  stance and speeds up in late stance. `locomotion.rs` publishes body
  velocity as the negation of the stance foot's body-relative motion, so the
  within-step speed profile is whatever the stance-leg sweep in `gait.rs`
  produces. The book gives a shape check for it: forward speed should dip
  through the first half of stance and recover in the second half, not be
  flat and not peak at mid-stance.
- **Foot placement is the balance strategy** for any physics-driven walker.
  If the active ragdoll ever walks without a pinned root, its step target
  must be computed from COM position and velocity each step, not from a fixed
  cycle. The swing foot lands where it will put the next COP on the correct
  side of the COM. This is the idea later formalized as the "capture point";
  that term and formula are outside this book.
- **Heel-strike foot pitch (~20°)** and the toe-supported trailing foot in
  double support are concrete targets for foot IK and foot-lock ranges. A
  foot forced flat at contact is anatomically wrong.
