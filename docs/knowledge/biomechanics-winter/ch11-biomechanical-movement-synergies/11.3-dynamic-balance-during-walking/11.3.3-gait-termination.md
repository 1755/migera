# 11.3.3 Gait termination

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §11.3.3, pp. 293–294 ·
> [open PDF at p. 293](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=306) ·
> Up: [11.3 Dynamic balance during walking](./INDEX.md)

Stopping is harder than starting. All forward momentum must go within the
last two steps, and the COP must end just *ahead* of the COM as the COM comes
to rest. The body brakes mostly with the plantarflexors of the
second-to-last stance leg, then takes a **short final step** (about half
length). The hip abductors steer the COP to a point directly ahead of the
COM's path.

## Key ideas

Sequence (Fig. 11.9). The stop command, a flashing light, came at a left
heel contact. The last cycle runs LHC → LHC = 0–100%. The right foot is on
plate #2 and the left on plate #1.

1. **56–64%, double support.** The COP moves rapidly forward from the left
   foot to the right foot. The COP→COM vectors point backward: rapid **COM
   deceleration** starts here.
2. **64–100%, right single support (from LTO1).** Right **plantarflexor
   activity increases dramatically**, pushing the COP further forward and
   enlarging the braking vector. By 100% the COM forward velocity is down
   about **70%**. Meanwhile the left swing leg is actively **decelerated** by
   its hip extensors and knee flexors, so the final step is **about half the
   normal length**.
3. **100–120%, left foot loads (after LHC).** The COP shifts rapidly lateral
   and forward, stopping at 120% **directly ahead of the COM's path**. Left
   hip abductors increase and right abductors decrease, the load/unload
   synergy.
4. **120–151%, final settle.** The COP moves **back** (both legs'
   plantarflexors reduce) and slightly right (right abductor up, left down),
   and the COM comes to rest in quiet standing.

- **Key synergy:** *proactive* control by both hip abductors to stop the
  COP's lateral travel exactly ahead of the COM trajectory.
- **Braking split:** about **85%** of forward velocity was removed by the
  right (second-to-last stance) plantarflexors, **15%** by the left.
- **Sensory feedback matters here.** In two unpublished trials, one patient
  with peripheral sensory loss and one subject with an ischemic leg block,
  the COP in the final double-support stage was erratic and **overshot** the
  COM in both M/L and A/P. Peripheral sensation monitors COP and COM position
  for this final placement.

## Equations and numbers

| Phase (% of last cycle) | Support | What the COP does | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 56–64 | double | races forward, left → right foot | COM deceleration begins |
| 64–100 | right single (LTO1 at 64) | pushed forward by right plantarflexors | COM velocity −70% by 100%; final step ≈ ½ length |
| 100–120 | double (LHC at 100) | lateral + forward to left foot, stops ahead of COM at 120 | lateral travel arrested by hip abductors |
| 120–151 | double | back and slightly right | COM at rest; quiet standing at 151% |

- Braking share: right plantarflexors 85%, left 15%.
- The whole stop takes the last **two steps**, and the full settle ends about
  half a cycle (51%) after the last heel contact.

## Relevance to migera

- **Implemented (2026-09-29) in `transition.rs`**: from a footfall the
  gait fades out over the time to the other leg's mid-swing (half the duty
  factor), with the legs kept at the walk's cadence (`stride_speed`) until
  the gait is gone. The last foot to move comes from behind to beside the
  planted one: live, it swung 41 cm (about half a walking swing) and landed
  4 mm beside it, and the planted foot slid 0 mm; the body stopped 0.16 s
  after the command. The old stop coasted 2.4 s on a stalled clock and slid
  the planted feet 73–77 mm. Winter's last foot lands half a step AHEAD;
  ours lands beside, so the character ends in its standing pose.
- **`transition.rs` stops on a footfall, which is the right instinct; the
  book adds the shape.** The stop before the change walked the last step out, then faded
  the gait weight to zero over `blend_seconds` on a footfall, with the cadence
  spring slowing the clock. A natural planned stop has three parts:
  1. Braking starts one step *before* the last footfall: the penultimate
     stance is the strong braking step.
  2. The **final step is about half length**, so the stop shortens the stride
     amplitude over the last step. Cadence alone is not the lever.
  3. The feet end roughly side by side. Over about half a cycle the COM
     drifts the last few centimetres forward and comes to rest, while the
     weight (COP) eases back from the forefeet toward mid-foot. The COM does
     not simply freeze where the last step left it.
  Concretely, stride length should scale toward ~0.5 on the final swing,
  followed by a short damped forward settle of the pelvis after the last
  contact.
- **Lateral end position.** The final COP lands straight ahead of the COM
  path, between the feet. A stop that leaves the pelvis over one foot, as a
  continuing hip sway would at the moment of stopping, is the wrong final
  posture. The pelvis sway amplitude should fade to zero within the final
  step.
- **Physics ragdoll:** braking is done by stance-leg ankle torque that pushes
  the COP forward, and the swing leg is actively slowed. A physics stop
  without both overshoots, which is the sensory-loss failure the book reports.
