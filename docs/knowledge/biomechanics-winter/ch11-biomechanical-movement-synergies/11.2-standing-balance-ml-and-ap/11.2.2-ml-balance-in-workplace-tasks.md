# 11.2.2 Medial lateral balance control during workplace tasks

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §11.2.2, pp. 288–289 ·
> [open PDF at p. 288](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=301) ·
> Up: [11.2 M/L and A/P balance in standing](./INDEX.md)

The hip-abductor load/unload synergy also runs while people stand and work
with their hands. In an efficient stander, left and right gluteus medius
activity alternates: one rises while the other falls. In an inefficient one
the two co-contract and fight each other. The co-contractors developed far
more low-back pain over two hours.

## Key ideas

- **Mechanism restated.** Increased abductor force on one side, with
  decreased abductor force on the other, tries to lift the pelvis/HAT on
  that side. This *loads* the ipsilateral leg and unloads the contralateral
  one. The COP moves toward the ipsilateral foot, and so the COM accelerates
  toward the **contralateral** side (the COP − COM error of Eq. 11.3).
- **Study** (Nelson-Wong et al., 2008): 23 subjects performed manual tasks at
  a table for 2 h. Surface EMG was taken from the right and left gluteus
  medius, linear-enveloped, then cross-correlated per 15-minute block
  (see §2.1).
  - A **negative** peak $R_{xy}$ means the synergy is present: one side up
    while the other is down.
  - A **positive** peak means co-contraction: both sides rise and fall
    together, an inefficient mode in which the abductors oppose each other.
- The $R_{xy}(\tau)$ curves are fairly flat over ±1 s because the gluteal
  bursts last a long time. The small lag at the peak (0.06–0.16 s) says the
  two sides are essentially in phase (co-contraction) or 180° out of phase
  (synergy).

## Equations and numbers

| Pattern (typical subject, one 15-min block) | Peak $R_{xy}$ | at $\tau$ | Figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Load/unload synergy (reciprocal) | **−0.677** | 0.16 s | 11.5 |
| Co-contraction (in phase) | **+0.766** | 0.06 s | 11.6 |

- EMG amplitudes in Figs. 11.5–11.6 are about 2–16% MVC, i.e. low-level
  sustained tone.
- Pain (visual analog scale, 0–40): 15 co-contractors went from an average
  of **4** in the first task to **32** in the last. 8 reciprocal subjects went
  from **1** to **8**. The group main effect was p < 0.0005.

## Relevance to migera

- Low for the kinematic stack; its one lesson is visual. A standing idle that
  shifts weight should *transfer* weight from leg to leg (one hip up, the
  other down, pelvis translating) rather than hold the body symmetric and
  tense. This supports the alternating weight shift in `phase.rs`'s
  standing idle.
- For the active ragdoll: a balance controller that stiffens *both* hip
  abductors at once (high symmetric PD gains) is the co-contraction mode. It
  holds the pose by fighting itself and saturates `max_torque` for no net
  moment. Driving the left and right hips' frontal-plane targets in opposite
  directions is the efficient pattern.
- Methodologically: a sign-aware cross-correlation of two actuator signals is
  a cheap automated test that tells "alternating" from "fighting". The
  unsigned alternative is blind to exactly that difference.
