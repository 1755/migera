# 4.3.2 Change in muscle length during movement

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.3.2, p. 102 ·
> [open PDF at p. 102](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=115) ·
> Up: [4.3 Muscle anthropometry](./INDEX.md)

Few studies map muscle length against joint angle. Grieve et al. (1978),
working on eight cadavers, found the gastrocnemius length nearly **linear**
in both ankle and knee angle. Muscle length can therefore be approximated
as a sum of per-joint linear terms.

## Key ideas and numbers

- Reference (resting) length: knee flexed 90°, ankle neutral.
- **Ankle:** −8.5% at 40° plantarflexion, rising linearly to +4% at 20°
  dorsiflexion. That is a slope of ≈ 12.5% / 60° ≈ **0.21% per degree**.
- **Knee:** +6.5% at full extension, falling almost linearly to −3% at 150°
  flexion. That is ≈ 9.5% / 150° ≈ **0.063% per degree**.
- The slopes are derived from the book's endpoints and are not printed.

## Where to read in the book
- p. 102 (PDF 115): the whole section, one paragraph.

## Relevance to migera

Low today, because migera has no muscle model; its `muscle` module was
deleted. If a muscle-length-based stiffness or a two-joint coupling term is
ever added to the ragdoll or IK, these slopes are the calibration.
