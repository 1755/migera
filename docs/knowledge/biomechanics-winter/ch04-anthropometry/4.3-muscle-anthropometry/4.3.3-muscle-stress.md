# 4.3.3 Force per unit cross-sectional area (stress)

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.3.3, p. 102 ·
> [open PDF at p. 102](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=115) ·
> Up: [4.3 Muscle anthropometry](./INDEX.md)

Maximum muscle force ≈ stress × PCA. Reported stresses span a wide range,
**20–100 N/cm²** (0.2–1.0 MPa), mostly measured isometrically. Much of the
spread comes from pennate muscles, whose true fiber area exceeds the
anatomical area used in the calculation.

## Key ideas and numbers

| Value | Condition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 20–100 N/cm² | range of reported values, mostly isometric; high end in pennate muscles | Haxton 1944; Alexander & Vernon 1975; Maughan et al. 1983 |
| up to 38 N/cm² | gastrocnemius and soleus (pennate) | Haxton 1944 |
| ≈ 70 N/cm² | quadriceps, dynamic, running and jumping; from a peak knee extensor moment of 210 N·m in adult males | stated in the same sentence as the Maughan et al. MVC value; attribution ambiguous |
| ≈ 100 N/cm² | quadriceps, isometric maximum voluntary contraction (MVC) | Maughan et al. 1983 |

- Pennate muscles give the high values because the pennate orientation
  effectively increases the cross-sectional area above the value measured
  and used in the stress calculation.
- 1 N/cm² = 10 kPa.

## Where to read in the book
- p. 102 (PDF 115): the whole section, one paragraph.

## Relevance to migera

Gives absolute torque scales for sanity-checking ragdoll strength.
Example, derived: knee extensor PCA from Table 4.3 is 30 + 26 + 25 + 12.5
= 93.5 cm². At 70 N/cm² that is ≈ 6.5 kN of muscle force. With the
book's 210 N·m peak knee moment, the effective moment arm comes out near
3 cm. migera's `max_torque` values in `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs` are
acceleration-shaped rad/s², not N·m. To compare, divide such a torque by
the segment chain's inertia about the joint
([4.1.6](../4.1-density-mass-inertial-properties/4.1.6-parallel-axis-theorem.md)).
For example, 210 N·m over a shank-plus-foot I_knee of roughly 0.3–0.4 kg·m²
is about 500–700 rad/s².
