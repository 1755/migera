# 4.3.4 Mechanical advantage of muscle

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.3.4, p. 102 ·
> [open PDF at p. 102](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=115) ·
> Up: [4.3 Muscle anthropometry](./INDEX.md)

Origin and insertion fix a tendon's angle of pull, and so its leverage.
The **moment arm** is the length of the line from the joint center
perpendicular to the muscle's line of action. It changes with joint angle,
so the same muscle force yields different joint moments at different poses.

## Key ideas and numbers

- Joint moment = muscle force × moment arm.
- Underlying structures (bone, other tendons) can redirect a tendon and
  change its pull angle.
- Smidt (1973, 26 subjects): the moment arms of both the **knee extensors
  and the hamstrings increase with knee flexion, peak at ≈ 45°**, and then
  fall again toward 90°.
- Wilkie (1950) documented moment arms and lengths for the elbow flexors.
- Specific moment-arm lengths for the biarticular leg muscles are in
  [4.3.5](./4.3.5-multijoint-muscles.md) (Fig. 4.10).

## Where to read in the book
- p. 102 (PDF 115): the whole section, one paragraph (no figure).

## Relevance to migera

Low to moderate. It justifies making a joint's torque ceiling
angle-dependent, strongest near mid-range, for example the knee near 45°.
It also explains why a straight-knee stance is mechanically weak. Nothing
in `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs` varies the ceiling with angle today;
each joint has one constant `max_torque`.
