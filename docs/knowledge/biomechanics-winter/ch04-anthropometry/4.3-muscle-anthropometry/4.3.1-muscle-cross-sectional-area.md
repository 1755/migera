# 4.3.1 Cross-sectional area of muscles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.3.1, pp. 100–102 ·
> [open PDF at p. 100](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=113) ·
> Up: [4.3 Muscle anthropometry](./INDEX.md)

A muscle's force capacity scales with its **physiological cross-sectional
area** (PCA, often written PCSA): the number of sarcomeres in parallel along
the line of pull. That is fiber volume divided by fiber length, reduced by
cos(pennation). Muscles in an agonist group probably share load in
proportion to their PCA. Table 4.4 therefore gives each muscle's share of
the total PCA crossing a joint.

## Key ideas

- **Pennation angle θ:** the angle between fibers and the muscle's long
  axis. Pennate fibers pull less effectively, but many short fibers pack a
  large PCA into a small volume, as in the soleus. θ grows as the muscle
  shortens.
- Long parallel fibers (sartorius, 38 cm) give tiny PCA and large
  excursion. Short pennate fibers (soleus, 3 cm) give huge PCA and little
  excursion.
- A biarticular muscle has a *different* %PCA at each joint it crosses,
  because each joint's total differs. For example, gastrocnemius is 22% at
  the ankle and 19% at the knee.

## Equations and numbers

Parallel-fibered muscle:

$$\text{PCA} = \frac{m}{d\,l}\ \ \text{cm}^2 \tag{4.17}$$

Pennate muscle:

$$\text{PCA} = \frac{m\cos\theta}{d\,l}\ \ \text{cm}^2 \tag{4.18}$$

m = fiber mass (g), d = muscle density = **1.056 g/cm³**, l = fiber length
(cm), θ = pennation angle.

**Table 4.3 — Mass, length, and PCA of some muscles** (Wickiewicz et al.
1983, three cadavers; verified against the rendered page):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sartorius | 75 | 38 | 1.9 | 0 |
| Biceps femoris (long) | 150 | 9 | 15.8 | 0 |
| Semitendinosus | 75 | 16 | 4.4 | 0 |
| Soleus | 215 | 3.0 | 58 | 30 |
| Gastrocnemius | 158 | 4.8 | 30 | 15 |
| Tibialis posterior | 55 | 2.4 | 21 | 15 |
| Tibialis anterior | 70 | 7.3 | 9.1 | 5 |
| Rectus femoris | 90 | 6.8 | 12.5 | 5 |
| Vastus lateralis | 210 | 6.7 | 30 | 5 |
| Vastus medialis | 200 | 7.2 | 26 | 5 |
| Vastus intermedius | 180 | 6.8 | 25 | 5 |

Eq. 4.18 reproduces the table, which is a useful fixture. Soleus:
215·cos30°/(1.056·3.0) = 58.8 cm², printed as 58. Biceps femoris:
150/(1.056·9) = 15.8. Sartorius: 75/(1.056·38) = 1.87, printed as 1.9.

Derived groupings, not printed in the book. At the ankle, the
plantarflexors (soleus, gastrocnemius, FHL, FDL, tibialis posterior,
peroneus brevis) hold 91% of the PCA and the dorsiflexors (TA, EDL, EHL)
9%, about **10 : 1**. At the knee, the extensors (the vasti and rectus
femoris) hold 56% and the flexors (gastrocnemius, hamstrings, sartorius,
gracilis) 44%.

## Relevance to migera

Per-joint strength *ratios* for the ragdoll's PD torque ceilings. Those
ceilings are `default_joint_params` in `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`:
`max_torque` there is acceleration-shaped, scaled by `CEILING_SCALE`, and
hand-authored per bone. Real capacity ∝ PCA × stress × moment arm (N·m).
Dividing by the segment's inertia about the joint
([Table 4.1](../4.1-density-mass-inertial-properties/4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#table-41-anthropometric-data))
turns it into the acceleration units the controller uses. The strong
**asymmetry** between plantar- and dorsiflexion (~10 : 1 PCA), and between
knee extension and flexion, cannot be represented today. The controller has
one symmetric ceiling per joint. It is a candidate refinement if a
standing or pushed ragdoll's ankle behaves wrong.
