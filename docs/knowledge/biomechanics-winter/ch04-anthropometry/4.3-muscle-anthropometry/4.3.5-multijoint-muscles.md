# 4.3.5 Multijoint muscles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.3.5, pp. 102–104 ·
> [open PDF at p. 102](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=115) ·
> Up: [4.3 Muscle anthropometry](./INDEX.md)

Many muscles span two joints. Their fibers are often too short to allow full
range at both joints at once. Normal movements therefore lengthen them at one
joint while shortening them at the other, which keeps the muscle nearly
isometric and **transfers energy between segments** instead of wasting
positive and negative work. In stance, the moment arms of the three big
biarticular leg muscles make each one a net *extensor* of the limb, so they
help the single-joint extensors resist collapse under gravity.

## Key ideas

- **Pairs:** hamstrings extend the hip and flex the knee. Rectus femoris
  flexes the hip and extends the knee. Gastrocnemius flexes the knee and
  plantarflexes the ankle.
- **Energy transfer (Elftman 1966), running examples:**
  - *Early swing:* rectus femoris shortens at the hip as it flexes, and
    lengthens at the knee as the leg swings back. Its tension does positive
    work at the hip and negative work at the knee: it decelerates the
    backward leg swing, then starts it forward. It may be isometric overall,
    moving energy from the leg to the pelvis.
  - *Push-off:* plantarflexors generate energy fast while the knee keeps
    extending. The gastrocnemius shortens distally and lengthens
    proximally, so it is roughly isometric.
  - *Late swing:* the knee extends fast while the hip starts extending.
    The hamstrings lengthen distally and shorten proximally, so they
    lengthen more slowly than a single-joint muscle would.
- **Stance roles via moment arms (Fig. 4.10):**
  - *Gastrocnemius*: 5 cm at the ankle vs 3.5 cm at the knee. Its ankle
    extensor (plantarflexor) contribution is ~50% larger than its knee
    flexor one. Net effect: rotates the leg posteriorly and prevents knee
    collapse.
  - *Hamstrings* (except the short head of biceps femoris): 6–7 cm at the
    hip vs 3.5 cm at the knee. Their hip extension is about twice their
    knee flexion. Net effect: rotates the thigh posteriorly and prevents
    knee collapse.
  - *Rectus femoris*: 5 cm at the hip, 4 cm at the knee, slightly larger at
    the hip. It fires with the whole quadriceps, whose uniarticular vasti
    are 84% of quadriceps PCA, so the dominant action is knee extension.
  - **Net:** the major biarticular muscles produce extension at all three
    joints in stance. The summed extensor moment is the **support moment**
    (Winter 1984; §5.2.6).

## Equations and numbers

Moment arms from Fig. 4.10 (rendered, PDF p. 117):

| Muscle | Proximal joint | Distal joint |
|---|---|---|
| Gastrocnemius | knee 3.5 cm | ankle 5 cm |
| Hamstrings | hip 6–7 cm | knee 3.5 cm |
| Rectus femoris | hip 5 cm | knee 4 cm |

**Book erratum:** the text on p. 103 says "The *hamstrings* have a 5-cm
moment-arm at the ankle and 3.5-cm moment-arm at the knee". The hamstrings
do not cross the ankle. The figure and the following sentence show that
this describes the **gastrocnemius**.

The 84% figure: Table 4.3 gives vasti 30 + 26 + 25 = 81 of 93.5 cm², which
is 87%. Table 4.4's knee column gives 48 of 56, which is 86%. The book's
84% is close to both but reproduces neither exactly.

## Relevance to migera

- **Leg controllers:** stance legs should resist collapse as a coupled
  hip–knee–ankle *extensor* synergy, not three independent joints. That
  suggests a support-moment-style term for a future balance or active-ragdoll
  stance controller. migera's PD joints in `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`
  are independent per joint today.
- **Procedural gait:** in late swing the knee extends while the hip begins
  to extend. That phase relation is what a two-joint coupling enforces, and
  it is a plausibility check for authored or oscillator-driven swing
  curves.
