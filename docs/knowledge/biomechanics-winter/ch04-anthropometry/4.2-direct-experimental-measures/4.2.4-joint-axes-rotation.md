# 4.2.4 Joint axes of rotation

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.2.4, pp. 98–100 ·
> [open PDF at p. 98](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=111) ·
> Up: [4.2 Direct experimental measures](./INDEX.md)

Surface markers placed "at" a joint are only estimates of its axis, and
some are off by centimetres. The true instantaneous axis can be computed from
two markers on each adjacent segment. Express the motion with one segment
held fixed, then use the moving marker's velocity and the joint angular
velocity to find the point it rotates about.

## Key ideas

- **Ankle:** the tibia and fibula roll in a small arc over the talus. The
  true axis is a few cm **distal** to the lateral-malleolus marker.
- **Hip:** the greater-trochanter marker sits **lateral** to the hip center,
  so thigh internal/external rotation and ab/adduction cause large errors.
- **Method (Fig. 4.9):** rotate and translate the data so segment A is fixed
  in space. Segment B's markers then move only by the relative joint motion.
  Marker (x₁, y₁) on B has velocity V and lies at radius R from the axis.
  The angular velocity ω_z comes from the line through B's two markers.
  Solve for R, then (x_c, y_c) = marker − R, and express it relative to A's
  anatomical markers (x₃, y₃), (x₄, y₄).
- **Degeneracy:** as ω_z → 0 or changes sign, R is indeterminate or blows
  up. Errors become significant below **0.5 rad/s**.

## Equations and numbers

$$\mathbf{V} = \boldsymbol{\omega}_z \times \mathbf{R} \tag{4.16a}$$

$$V_x\hat{\imath} + V_y\hat{\jmath} = R_y\omega_z\hat{\imath} - R_x\omega_z\hat{\jmath}
\;\Rightarrow\; V_x = R_y\omega_z,\quad V_y = -R_x\omega_z \tag{4.16b}$$

Sign caution: with **ω** = ω_z k̂ and **R** from the axis to the marker,
**ω** × **R** = (−R_yω_z, +R_xω_z). Eq. 4.16b has the opposite sign, so it
holds only if **R** points from the marker to the axis. Re-derive the sign
for your own convention before coding it.

## Relevance to migera

- **Rig joint placement:** Mixamo-style rigs, `puppet_base.gltf` included,
  put the ankle and hip joints near surface landmarks. The anatomical axis
  is a few cm off. That matters for foot-roll pivots and hip IK roots.
- **Fitting pivots from motion capture or a retargeted clip:** the
  instantaneous-axis method, with its low-ω cut-off, is the recipe. Gate
  on |ω| > 0.5 rad/s.
