# 4.2.2 Calculation of the mass of a distal segment

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.2.2, pp. 96–97 ·
> [open PDF at p. 96](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=109) ·
> Up: [4.2 Direct experimental measures](./INDEX.md)

On the same balance board, raising a distal segment (e.g. leg and foot) to
vertical moves its COM over the joint. The change in the scale reading then
gives the segment's weight.

## Key ideas

- Before lifting, the segment COM is x₄ from the pivot and the scale reads S.
  After lifting, the COM is x₅ from the pivot and the scale reads S′.
- The lost moment of the segment equals the gained moment of the scale.
- **Main error source:** x₄ usually comes from anthropometric tables.
- Whole-limb mass: repeat supine with the limb flexed to 90°. The thigh is
  then total limb minus leg-and-foot.

## Equations and numbers

$$w_4(x_4 - x_5) = (S' - S)\,x_3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad w_4 = \frac{(S' - S)\,x_3}{x_4 - x_5} \tag{4.14}$$

## Where to read in the book
- pp. 96–97 (PDF 109–110): method, Eq. 4.14, Fig. 4.7(b).

## Relevance to migera

Low. It is an experimental technique. The only takeaway is that measured
segment masses still inherit table error through the COM location.
