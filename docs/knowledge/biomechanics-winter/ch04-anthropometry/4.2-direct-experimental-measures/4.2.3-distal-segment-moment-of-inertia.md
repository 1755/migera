# 4.2.3 Moment of inertia of a distal segment

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.2.3, pp. 97–98 ·
> [open PDF at p. 97](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=110) ·
> Up: [4.2 Direct experimental measures](./INDEX.md)

I about a joint can be measured directly from I = M/α. A limb is held
still by two balanced forces. One is suddenly released, and the
instantaneous angular acceleration under the remaining known moment gives I.
This assumes the proximal segment is fixed.

## Key ideas

- A horizontal force F pulls on a cable at y₁ from the joint and is balanced
  by a release mechanism. An accelerometer sits at y₂ from the joint, so its
  tangential acceleration is a = y₂α.
- On release the net moment is F·y₁. Capture the peak acceleration burst,
  which needs a storage oscilloscope because pen recorders are too slow.
  The accelerometer burst can trigger the sweep. F then drops after the
  peak as the limb moves and the cable slackens.
- Release by cutting the rope.
- Hatze (1975) measured I, COM location and a damping coefficient together.

## Equations and numbers

$$I = \frac{M}{\alpha} = \frac{F\,y_1\,y_2}{a} \tag{4.15}$$

## Relevance to migera

Moderate as a **test pattern**. A physics body's effective inertia about its
joint can be measured the same way in a unit test: pin the parent, apply a
known torque for one step, read the angular acceleration, and compare it
with m(ρ_prox·L)² from
[Table 4.1](../4.1-density-mass-inertial-properties/4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#table-41-anthropometric-data).
