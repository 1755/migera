# 4.2.1 Location of the anatomical center of mass of the body

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.2.1, p. 96 ·
> [open PDF at p. 96](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=109) ·
> Up: [4.2 Direct experimental measures](./INDEX.md)

The whole-body COM (the "anatomical center of mass") is found by laying the
subject on a rigid board supported by a pivot at one end and a scale at the
other. The scale reading and a moment balance about the pivot locate the COM.

## Key ideas

- The board's weight w₁ at x₁ from the pivot and the body weight w₂ are
  known. The scale reads S at distance x₃ from the pivot. Unknown: the body
  COM's distance x₂ from the pivot.
- Put the pivot close to the expected COM: the scale load is then small, so
  a sensitive 0–5 kg scale can replace a 50–100 kg one, which is more
  accurate.
- The section intro warns that direct measures have limited capability and
  sometimes do little better than tables.

## Equations and numbers

Moments about the pivot:

$$w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 = Sx_3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x_2 = \frac{Sx_3 - w_1x_1}{w_2} \tag{4.13}$$

## Where to read in the book
- p. 96 (PDF 109): method and Eq. 4.13; Fig. 4.7(a) is on p. 97 (PDF 110).

## Relevance to migera

Low for runtime code. As a test idea it is a static equilibrium check: a
limp ragdoll lying on two supports must load them in the ratio set by its
COM.
