# 4.1.5 Mass moment of inertia and radius of gyration

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.1.5, pp. 89–90 ·
> [open PDF at p. 89](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=102) ·
> Up: [4.1 Density, mass, and inertial properties](./INDEX.md)

Moment of inertia I is the rotational analogue of mass (M = Iα). It depends
on the axis, is smallest about the COM, and grows with the square of each
mass element's distance. It is tabulated compactly as a **radius of
gyration** ρ: the distance at which the segment's whole mass would give the
same I.

## Key ideas

- M (N·m) = I (kg·m²) × α (rad/s²): I measures resistance to change of
  angular velocity.
- Mass near the axis barely matters; mass far from it dominates (the
  flywheel principle). Hence a limb's inertia about its proximal joint is
  dominated by its distal part.
- ρ₀ picture (Fig. 4.5): split the segment into two equal point masses m/2
  at ±ρ₀ from the COM. They keep the same COM and the same I₀ in the plane
  of rotation.

## Equations and numbers

About the left end, segment sliced into masses mᵢ at distance xᵢ:

$$I = m_1x_1^2 + m_2x_2^2 + \dots + m_nx_n^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i x_i^2 \tag{4.8}$$

About the COM, with radius of gyration ρ₀:

$$I_0 = m\rho_0^2 \tag{4.9}$$

Table 4.1 gives ρ₀ / L (and ρ about each end / L), so I₀ = m(ρ₀·L)² with
m = fraction × body mass. For reference, a thin uniform rod has
ρ₀ = L/√12 = 0.289 L. The table's long limb segments (thigh, shank, upper
arm, forearm) sit just above that, at 0.302–0.323 L, so a thin rod is a
good first approximation for them. The foot (0.475 L) and forearm-and-hand
(0.468 L) are not rod-like.

## Relevance to migera

**Built (2026-09-29):** `ragdoll_plugin::limb_mass_properties` gives each
limb body (upper arm, forearm-and-hand, thigh, shank, foot; the
`anthropometry::limb_segment` table) Table 4.1's mass, COM fraction and
transverse inertia m(ρL)², with avian's auto mass properties off. Test
`a_limb_body_carries_winters_mass_distribution`: the real rig's thigh has
COM within 5 mm of 43.3% hip→knee and inertia within 1%. With the
properties disabled the COM lands 2.9 cm off. The trunk, head and hands
still use avian's capsules. The text below describes the state before this
change.

Gives the target inertia for each ragdoll body: I⊥ = m(ρ₀L)². Before the
change, avian computed each body's inertia from a uniform capsule. For migera's radius
of 0.2–0.22 L that is ρ₀ ≈ 0.38–0.39 L, about 20% above the table's ρ and
45–65% above its I for long limb segments (see the mapping in
[4.1.3](./4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#relevance-to-migera)).
In migera's acceleration-shaped PD loop inertia does not change tracking.
It does change how an off-centre hit splits into spin versus travel, and how
constraints share corrections.
([apply_angular_acceleration](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-apply-angular-acceleration-not-torque.md)).
Also useful for any torque-based controller budget: a joint moment
M = I_joint·α for a desired swing.
