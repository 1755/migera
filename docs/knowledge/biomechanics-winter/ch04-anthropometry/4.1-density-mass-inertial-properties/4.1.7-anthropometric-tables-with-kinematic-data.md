# 4.1.7 Use of anthropometric tables and kinematic data

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.1.7, pp. 91–96 ·
> [open PDF at p. 91](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=104) ·
> Up: [4.1 Density, mass, and inertial properties](./INDEX.md)

This section turns
[Table 4.1](./4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#table-41-anthropometric-data)
into numbers from marker positions. It covers segment masses and COM
locations, whole-body COM as a mass-fraction-weighted sum, and segment
inertia from radius of gyration. Along the way it gives Winter's own
**14-segment whole-body COM model** and a physics check that any COM
estimate must pass.

## Key ideas

**4.1.7.1 Segment masses and COMs** (Example 4.4). An 80 kg subject with
measured lengths foot 0.195 m, leg (shank) 0.435 m, thigh 0.410 m and HAT
0.295 m (trochanter→mid rib):

| Segment | Mass | COM location |
|---|---|---|
| Foot | 0.0145×80 = 1.16 kg | 0.50×0.195 = 0.098 m between ankle and metatarsal markers |
| Leg | 0.0465×80 = 3.72 kg | 0.433×0.435 = 0.188 m below the femoral condyle |
| Thigh | 0.10×80 = 8.0 kg | 0.433×0.410 = 0.178 m below the greater trochanter |
| HAT | 0.678×80 = 54.24 kg | 1.142×0.295 = 0.337 m above the greater trochanter |

**4.1.7.2 Total-body COM.** With mass fractions fᵢ, the body mass cancels
(Eq. 4.12). Only the fractions and the segment COMs are needed.

- **Symmetric-gait trick:** if only one side is filmed, use that side's
  data half a stride later in time, shifted back by one step length, as the
  other side. In Example 4.5 the stride is 68 frames, so frame 15's left
  side is frame 49's right side, shifted back by the step of 0.707 m (half
  the 1.414 m stride). Using fractions foot 0.0145, leg 0.0465, thigh 0.10
  and ½HAT 0.339 per side, and Table 4.2's coordinates, the body COM is
  **(0.724, 0.937) m**. HAT dominates the COM position, but the leg
  segments dominate the walking *energy* changes (Chapter 6).
- **The pelvis is not the COM.** Some standing studies track a
  pelvis-mounted rod as a COM proxy. In a **hip strategy**, where the whole
  body flexes at the hip to defend against a forward fall, the pelvis moves
  backward much more than the COM (Horak & Nashner 1986).
- **3D COM needs every segment tracked.** MacKinnon & Winter (1993) and
  Jian et al. (1993) used 7-segment estimates for frontal-plane balance and
  gait initiation. The most complete is Winter et al. (1998): **21 markers,
  14 segments** (Fig. 4.6). Its trunk needs **four** segments because
  breathing and heartbeat shift mass inside it.
- **Validity check for any COM estimate**, from the inverted-pendulum
  model:
  $$\text{COP} - \text{COM} = -K\,\ddot{\text{COM}},\qquad K = \frac{I}{W h}$$
  COP is from the force plate and COM̈ is the horizontal (A/P or M/L)
  COM acceleration. I is the whole-body moment of inertia about the ankles,
  W the body weight and h the COM height above the ankles.

**Winter's 14-segment model** (Fig. 4.6, from the rendered page; marker
numbers as in the figure: 1/2 R/L ankle, 3/4 R/L knee, 5/6 R/L hip, 7/10 R/L
wrist, 8/11 R/L elbow, 9/12 R/L shoulder, 13/14 R/L ear, 15/16 R/L ASIS,
17/18 R/L iliac crest, 19/20 R/L lower rib, 21 xiphoid):

| Segment | Mass fraction | Segment COM |
|---|---|---|
| Head | 0.081 | (13 + 14)/2 |
| Trunk 4 | 0.136 | (9 + 12 + 21)/3 |
| Trunk 3 | 0.078 | ((19 + 20)/2 + 21)/2 |
| Trunk 2 | 0.065 | (17 + 18 + 19 + 20)/4 |
| Trunk 1 | 0.078 | (17 + 18 + 15 + 16)/4 |
| Pelvis | 0.142 | (15 + 16)/2 |
| Thighs | 0.100 (×2) | 0.433·3 + 0.567·5 and 0.433·4 + 0.567·6 |
| Legs & feet | 0.060 (×2) | 0.606·1 + 0.394·3 and 0.606·2 + 0.394·4 |
| Upper arms | 0.028 (×2) | 0.436·8 + 0.564·9 and 0.436·11 + 0.564·12 |
| Lower arms | 0.022 (×2) | 0.682·7 + 0.318·8 and 0.682·10 + 0.318·11 |
| Total | 1.00 | |

Each limb weight multiplies the *distal* marker by the COM-from-proximal
fraction: thigh COM = 0.567·hip + 0.433·knee, which is 0.433 of the way
from hip to knee. The fractions sum to 1.000. The four trunk parts
(0.357) plus the pelvis equal Table 4.1's thorax + abdomen (0.355) plus
pelvis to within 0.002. "Legs & feet" is 0.060 here against 0.061 in
Table 4.1.

**4.1.7.3 Moment of inertia.** Example 4.6, shank with m = 3.72 kg and
L = 0.435 m:
I₀ = 3.72(0.435·0.302)² = **0.064**, I_p = 3.72(0.435·0.528)² = **0.196**,
I_d = 3.72(0.435·0.643)² = **0.291 kg·m²**. By parallel axis,
0.064 + 3.72·0.188² = 0.196, which agrees.
Example 4.7, HAT with m = 54.24 kg and L = 0.295 m:
I_p = 54.24(0.295·1.456)² = **10.01 kg·m²**. Then
I₀ = 10.01 − 54.24(0.295·1.142)² = **3.85**, which equals
54.24(0.295·0.903)² = 3.85 kg·m².

## Equations and numbers

$$\bar{x} = \frac{m_1x_1 + \dots + m_nx_n}{m_1 + \dots + m_n} \tag{4.11}$$

$$\bar{x} = \frac{f_1Mx_1 + \dots + f_nMx_n}{M} = f_1x_1 + f_2x_2 + \dots + f_nx_n \tag{4.12}$$

Table 4.2 (Example 4.5, frame 15, metres): right foot/leg/thigh/½HAT
X = 0.791/0.814/0.787/0.721 and Y = 0.101/0.374/0.708/1.124. The left side,
from frame 49 with the step subtracted, is X = 0.646/0.648/0.695/0.717 and
Y = 0.067/0.334/0.691/1.122. The book's own sum uses 0.645 for the left
foot X, a typo that does not change the result.

## Relevance to migera

- **The 14-segment model is a template beside migera's 14-body ragdoll.**
  Winter splits the trunk into 4 parts plus the pelvis and merges each
  shank with its foot. migera
  (`default_body_layout` in `src/character/anim/ragdoll_plugin.rs`) uses a
  pelvis, 2 torso bodies and a separate foot. Both use the same Dempster
  fractions. For a *COM estimate* from the animated skeleton, Winter's
  marker recipe maps onto rig joints almost one to one.
- **COM for balance code:** Eq. 4.12 over the rig's joints is cheap, a
  dozen multiply-adds. It is the right input for pelvis/stance adaptation,
  foot placement and a future balance controller. The hip-strategy remark
  warns against using the hips as a COM proxy.
- **Test oracle:** COP − COM = −K·COM̈ gives a physics consistency test for
  a ragdoll or balance sim: the support-point estimate must lead or lag the
  COM by an amount proportional to its acceleration. Examples 4.4–4.7 are
  fixed numeric fixtures.
