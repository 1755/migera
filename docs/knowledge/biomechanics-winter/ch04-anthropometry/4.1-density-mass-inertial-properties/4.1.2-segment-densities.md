# 4.1.2 Segment densities

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.1.2, pp. 84–85 ·
> [open PDF at p. 84](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=97) ·
> Up: [4.1 Density, mass, and inertial properties](./INDEX.md)

Density is not uniform across (or within) segments: distal segments carry
more bone per volume and are denser, and every segment's density rises with
the whole-body density. Fig. 4.2 plots six limb segments as straight lines
against body density.

## Key ideas

- Distal > proximal: hands densest, thigh least dense.
- Upper-extremity lines are steeper than lower-extremity lines.
- Body density comes from [Eq. 4.1/4.2](./4.1.1-whole-body-density.md) or is measured directly.

## Equations and numbers

Approximate readings from Fig. 4.2 (read off the rendered plot, ±0.005 kg/l),
over body density 1.03–1.10 kg/l:

| Segment | at body 1.03 | at body 1.066 (dashed line in fig.) | at body 1.10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hands | ≈1.09 | ≈1.16 | ≈1.215 |
| Forearms | ≈1.085 | ≈1.135 | ≈1.18 |
| Foot | ≈1.075 | ≈1.105 | ≈1.135 |
| Shank | ≈1.065 | ≈1.10 | ≈1.12 |
| Upper arms | ≈1.06 | ≈1.10 | ≈1.13 |
| Thigh | ≈1.04 | ≈1.065 | ≈1.09 |

These agree with the density column of
[Table 4.1](./4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#table-41-anthropometric-data)
(hand 1.16, forearm 1.13, upper arm 1.07, foot 1.10, leg 1.09, thigh 1.05).
§4.4 Problem 1(b) reads forearm density 1.13 kg/l for a body of 1.059 kg/l.

## Relevance to migera

Low. The ragdoll sets masses directly, so density only matters if a limb
collider's volume is used to derive its mass. The 10% spread between
segments is smaller than the error of approximating a limb as a capsule.
