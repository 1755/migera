# 4.1.6 Parallel-axis theorem

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.1.6, pp. 90–91 ·
> [open PDF at p. 90](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=103) ·
> Up: [4.1 Density, mass, and inertial properties](./INDEX.md)

Segments rotate about a joint, not their COM, and in vivo inertia can only be
measured about a joint. The parallel-axis theorem connects the two: the
moment of inertia about any parallel axis is the COM value plus mass times
the squared offset.

## Key ideas

- Proof via the two-point-mass model of Fig. 4.5: masses m/2 at x − ρ₀ and
  x + ρ₀ from the new axis.
- x may be in either direction from the COM, as long as the axes are
  parallel to the one I₀ was computed about.
- Consequence: I about a distant joint dwarfs I₀. A limb swung from the hip
  with a locked knee has ~20× its own centroidal inertia.

## Equations and numbers

$$I = \frac{m}{2}(x-\rho_0)^2 + \frac{m}{2}(x+\rho_0)^2 = m\rho_0^2 + mx^2 = I_0 + mx^2 \tag{4.10}$$

I₀ = inertia about the COM, x = COM-to-axis distance, m = segment mass.

In fractions of segment length (a self-consistency check on Table 4.1):
ρ_prox² = ρ₀² + f_prox², ρ_dist² = ρ₀² + f_dist². For the thigh,
0.323² + 0.433² = 0.540², matching the table. Checking every row this way
exposes the head-and-neck misprint (0.116 → 1.116) noted in
[4.1.3](./4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#table-41-anthropometric-data).

**Example 4.3** (p. 91), a prosthetic leg with m = 3 kg, COM 20 cm from the
knee, ρ₀ = 14.1 cm:
- (a) I₀ = 3·0.141² = 0.06 kg·m². About the knee: I = 0.06 + 3·0.2² = **0.18 kg·m²**.
- (b) Knee locked, hip 42 cm above the knee, so x = 0.62 m:
  I_hip = 0.06 + 3·0.62² = **1.21 kg·m²**, about 20× I₀.

## Relevance to migera

- Build the inertia of a ragdoll body whose origin is not its COM, or of a
  body that stands for several segments. migera's forearm body carries the
  forearm-and-hand mass (0.022), and its lower-torso body spans both
  `Spine` and `Spine1` (`default_body_layout` in `ragdoll_plugin.rs`). Combine the parts' I₀ about their joint COM
  using this theorem.
- Swing-time and torque budgets for procedural leg swing: the hip must
  supply I_hip·α, where I_hip is for the whole leg about the hip.
- Test oracle: Example 4.3 is a fixed input→answer pair for any inertia
  helper in `src/math`.
