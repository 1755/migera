# 4.1.4 Center of mass of a multisegment system

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.1.4, pp. 88–89 ·
> [open PDF at p. 88](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=101) ·
> Up: [4.1 Density, mass, and inertial properties](./INDEX.md)

The body's COM moves whenever any segment moves, so it is recomputed every
frame as the mass-weighted average of the segment COMs. It is essential for
balance and jumping analyses but **wrong as an energy measure**: opposite
limb swings cancel in the COM while each costs real energy.

## Key ideas

- Per-axis weighted average; each coordinate computed separately (Fig. 4.4,
  three segments).
- **Limited use for energetics.** Forward motion of one leg and backward
  motion of the other leave the COM nearly unchanged, so COM-based energy
  misses the reciprocal-limb energy (developed in Chapter 6).
- **Where it is essential:** jumping (the flight trajectory is fixed at
  takeoff) and posture/balance studies.

## Equations and numbers

For segments with masses m₁, m₂, m₃ at COMs (xᵢ, yᵢ), M = m₁ + m₂ + m₃:

$$x_0 = \frac{m_1x_1 + m_2x_2 + m_3x_3}{M} \tag{4.6}$$

$$y_0 = \frac{m_1y_1 + m_2y_2 + m_3y_3}{M} \tag{4.7}$$

Generalises to n segments and 3D; with mass fractions it becomes
x₀ = Σ fᵢxᵢ ([Eq. 4.12](./4.1.7-anthropometric-tables-with-kinematic-data.md)).

## Relevance to migera

- **Balance and pelvis:** the whole-body COM of the animated pose, with
  [Table 4.1](./4.1.3-segment-mass-and-center-of-mass.md#table-41-anthropometric-data)
  fractions, is the quantity to keep over the support base. Pelvis sway or
  stance logic in `src/character/anim/pelvis.rs` and `stance.rs` can use it
  as the target or the test oracle, instead of treating the hips as the COM.
  The hips are not the COM: in a hip strategy the pelvis moves far more than
  the COM ([4.1.7](./4.1.7-anthropometric-tables-with-kinematic-data.md)).
- **Ragdoll verification:** the same sum over the 14 ragdoll bodies'
  positions gives a physical COM to compare against the animated COM.
- **Energy caveat:** do not use COM kinetic/potential energy as an effort
  or "naturalness" metric for a gait. Sum per-segment energies instead
  (Chapter 6).
