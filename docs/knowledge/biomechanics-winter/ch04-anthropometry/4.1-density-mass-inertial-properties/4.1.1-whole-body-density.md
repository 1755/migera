# 4.1.1 Whole-body density

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.1.1, pp. 83–84 ·
> [open PDF at p. 83](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=96) ·
> Up: [4.1 Density, mass, and inertial properties](./INDEX.md)

The body averages a little above water density — about 1.06 kg/l — and the
exact value depends on build: a linear formula in the ponderal index
(height over cube root of weight) predicts it, with short, heavy people less
dense than tall, thin ones.

## Key ideas

- Tissue specific gravities: cortical bone > 1.8, muscle just over 1.0,
  fat < 1.0, lungs hold gas. The mix (somatotype) sets the average.
- **Ponderal index** c = h / w^(1/3): larger for tall slim people, smaller
  for short heavy ones; density rises with c.

## Equations and numbers

Imperial (h in inches, w in pounds):

$$d = 0.69 + 0.0297\,c \quad \text{kg/l},\qquad c = h/w^{1/3} \tag{4.1}$$

Metric (h in metres, mass in kg):

$$d = 0.69 + 0.9\,c \quad \text{kg/l} \tag{4.2}$$

(kg/l = g/cm³; ×1000 for kg/m³.)

Example 4.1 (p. 84): 5′10″, 170 lb → c = 70/170^(1/3) = 12.64,
d = 1.065 kg/l. Metric: h = 1.78 m, w = 77.3 kg, c = 0.418, d = 1.066 kg/l.
Problem 1(a) of §4.4: 1.68 m, 68.5 kg → d = 1.059 kg/l.

## Relevance to migera

Moderate. Gives the physically right density scale, ~1060 kg/m³, for
converting a body's volume to mass. migera's ragdoll does not use a
physical density: `density_for` in `src/character/anim/ragdoll_plugin.rs`
back-solves a per-capsule density that makes each capsule hit its Table 4.1
mass, which is the better choice because capsules are not body-shaped. Use
this density for a prop, a severed limb, or a buoyancy test (the whole body
floats near neutral in water).
