# 4.4 Problems based on anthropometric data

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §4.4, pp. 104–106 ·
> [open PDF at p. 104](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=117) ·
> Up: [Chapter 4 — Anthropometry](./INDEX.md)

Four problem sets exercise every formula in the chapter, and each comes with
a printed answer. Problem 1 is fully self-contained, because all inputs are
in the text. Problems 2–4 use the walking subject of Appendix A (Tables A.3
and A.4 and the subject's anthropometrics). The answers make them ready-made
known-input → known-output fixtures.

## Key ideas

| Problem | Exercises | Inputs | Printed answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1(a) | Eq. 4.2 body density | h = 1.68 m, m = 68.5 kg | **1.059 kg/l** |
| 1(b) | Fig. 4.2 segment density; slice-volume integration (Eq. 4.4) | forearm 24.0 cm (ulnar styloid → elbow axis); circumferences at 1-cm steps from the wrist, circular sections: 20.1, 20.3, 20.5, 20.7, 20.9, 21.2, 21.5, 21.9, 22.5, 23.2, 23.9, 24.6, 25.1, 25.7, 26.4, 27.0, 27.5, 27.9, 28.2, 28.4, 28.4, 28.3, 28.2, 28.0 | density **1.13 kg/l**, volume **1.174 l**, mass **1.33 kg**; Table 4.1 gives **1.10 kg** |
| 1(c) | Eq. 4.5 slice COM | same slices | COM **10.34 cm** from the elbow; Table 4.1 gives **10.32 cm** |
| 1(d) | Eq. 4.8 slice inertia; ρ = √(I/m) | same slices | I_elbow = **0.0201 kg·m²**, ρ = **12.27 cm**; Table 4.1 gives **12.62 cm** |
| 2(a) | lower-limb COM (Eq. 4.12) | Appendix A Table A.3, frame 70 | x = **1.755 m**, y = **0.522 m** |
| 2(b) | whole-body COM with the symmetric-gait trick | stride length from Problem 3.6-2(f), stride 68 frames, Table A.4, frame 30 | x ≈ **1.025 m**, y = **0.904 m** |
| 3(a) | I₀ of HAT (Eq. 4.9) | Appendix A subject | **1.96 kg·m²** |
| 3(b) | parallel axis, feet together standing | HAT about hip, knee, ankle | I_h = **5.09**, I_k = **15.78**, I_a = **42.31 kg·m²** |
| 3(c) | point-mass contribution | head COM 1.65 m above the ankle | I_head,ankle = **12.50 kg·m²**, **29.6%** of I_HAT,ankle |
| 4(a) | lower-limb I about the hip | knee straight; foot as a point mass 6 cm distal to the ankle | **1.39 kg·m²** |
| 4(b) | added point mass | 1.8 kg ski boot, 1 cm distal to the ankle | increase **1.01 kg·m²** |

Notes:
- Problem 1 was recomputed in Python on 2026-09-28, with each 1-cm slice
  taken as a cylinder centred at its midpoint and ρ = 1.13 kg/l. It gives
  V = 1.174 l, m = 1.327 kg, COM = 10.34 cm, I = 0.0201 kg·m² and
  ρ = 12.31 cm. Everything matches the book except ρ, which the book gives
  as 12.27 cm. The 0.3% gap probably comes from the slice convention, so
  test ρ with a small tolerance.
- Problem 1(b)–(d) shows how much one real person departs from the table:
  mass is 21% above it, COM agrees to 0.2 mm, ρ is 3% off. Averaged mass
  fractions are the weakest column.
- In 1(b), the mass from Table 4.1 (1.10 kg) is 0.016 × 68.5, which uses
  the forearm-only fraction.
- Problem 2(b) points to "Problem 2(f) in Section 3.8" for the stride
  length. In the manifest the kinematics problems are §3.6; the book's
  cross-reference number appears stale.
- Problem 3(b): HAT inertia grows ~8× from hip to ankle. The ankle
  controls by far the largest inertial load in standing.

## Where to read in the book
- p. 104 (PDF 117): Problem 1(a)–(b).
- p. 105 (PDF 118): Problems 1(c)–4(b).

## Relevance to migera

Direct test fixtures. Problems 1(c)–(d) test a numerical COM/inertia
integrator. Problems 3–4 are parallel-axis and point-mass fixtures for any
inertia helper. Problem 3(b)'s ratios (hip : knee : ankle ≈ 1 : 3.1 : 8.3)
are an order-of-magnitude reference for how much more authority an
ankle-strategy balance controller needs than a hip strategy.
