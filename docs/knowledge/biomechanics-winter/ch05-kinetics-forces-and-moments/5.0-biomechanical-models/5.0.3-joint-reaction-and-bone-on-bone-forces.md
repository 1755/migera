# 5.0.3 Joint reaction forces and bone-on-bone forces

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.0.3, pp. 110–112 ·
> [open PDF at p. 110](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=123) ·
> Up: [5.0 Biomechanical models](./INDEX.md)

Cutting the link-segment model at each joint (the free-body diagram, Fig. 5.3)
exposes an equal and opposite **joint reaction force** on the two segments.
That is not the force the bones press together with: the **bone-on-bone
force** also includes the compression that active muscles add across the
joint.

## Key ideas

- **Free-body diagram:** break the model at the joints, draw the reaction
  force and moment at each cut, equal and opposite on the two sides (Newton's
  third law). A foot held off the ground pulls down on the leg with its own
  weight, and the leg pulls up on the foot equally.
- **Bone-on-bone force** = actual contact force on the articular surfaces;
  in the simplest case = muscle compressive force + joint reaction force.
  Active muscles pull the surfaces together (compression, sometimes shear).
- **Fig. 5.4 example** (lower segment weighs 100 N, hangs from muscles):
  - Case 1, muscles passive (with ligaments) pulling up 100 N:
    reaction 100 N, bone-on-bone force **0** (no compression or tension).
  - Case 2, muscles actively pull up 170 N: bone-on-bone force **70 N**
    compression; the lower segment still sees a net 100 N up
    (170 up − 70 down), so the free-body diagram is unchanged.
- Real joints have several muscles each side, unknown tendon angles and
  articular geometry, so the full analysis needs the more detailed model of
  [5.3](../5.3-dynamic-bone-on-bone-forces/INDEX.md). Representative paper:
  Paul (1966).

In magnitudes for Fig. 5.4 case 2: total muscle pull 170 N minus the 100 N
the reaction must carry leaves 70 N pressing the surfaces together.

## Relevance to migera

Low–moderate. A physics engine's joint constraint force is the *reaction*
force (the torque-motor model), never a bone-on-bone load, so it is useless
for anything like injury/impact-severity heuristics without the muscle term.
Useful mainly as a vocabulary guard when reading ragdoll constraint forces.
