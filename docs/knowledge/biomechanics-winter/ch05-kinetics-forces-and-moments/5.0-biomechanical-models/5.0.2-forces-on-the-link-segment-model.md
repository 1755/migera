# 5.0.2 Forces acting on the link-segment model

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.0.2, pp. 109–110 ·
> [open PDF at p. 109](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=122) ·
> Up: [5.0 Biomechanical models](./INDEX.md)

Three kinds of load act on the whole-body model: gravity, external (ground)
reactions, and muscle/ligament action. The last is only ever recovered as a
**net** moment per joint, which cannot separate co-contraction, friction or
passive ligament contributions.

## Key ideas

1. **Gravity** acts downward through each segment COM, magnitude $m g$
   ($g \approx 9.8$ m/s²).
2. **Ground reaction / external forces** must be measured (force transducer,
   force plate). A distributed load such as the pressure under a foot is
   represented as one vector acting at the **centre of pressure (COP)**.
3. **Muscle and ligament forces** appear as a net muscle moment per joint:
   - Co-contraction: only agonist minus antagonist is visible.
   - Joint/muscle friction lowers the effective moment; the contractile
     elements actually produce more than computed, but at low–moderate speeds
     the error is only a few percent.
   - Near the end of range, ligaments and other passive structures add or
     subtract moment; unless the muscles are silent their share is unknowable.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 109 (PDF 122): the three force types, with the co-contraction and
  friction caveats.
- p. 110 (PDF 123): passive-structure caveat.

## Relevance to migera

Moderate. It sets expectations for an inverse-dynamics oracle: a small or
zero computed moment does not mean "no muscle effort" (co-contraction is
invisible), so such an oracle can flag implausibly *large* moments but cannot
prove a pose is relaxed. The "passive structures at end of range" point maps
onto the ragdoll's joint limits — avian's limit constraint plays the
ligament's role, and its contribution shows up in the net joint torque just as
the book warns.
