# 5.3.2 Example problem (Scott and Winter, 1990)

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.3.2, pp. 132–136 ·
> [open PDF at p. 132](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=145) ·
> Up: [5.3 Bone-on-bone forces during dynamic conditions](./INDEX.md)

For a middle-distance runner's stance, the plantarflexor moment is shared
among five muscles by assuming no co-contraction and equal stress in every
active muscle (force ∝ physiological cross-sectional area). Adding the
muscle forces to the reaction forces shows the ankle's compressive load
peaks above 5500 N, more than 11 × body weight, with the muscles supplying
over 80% of it.

## Key ideas

- **Assumptions:** no co-contraction (only the plantarflexors act); each
  active muscle has the same stress $S_e(t)$, so its force is PCA × stress;
  the ankle joint centre is fixed (lateral malleolus marker, Fig. 5.20).
- **Geometry:** each origin/insertion is stored in polar coordinates relative
  to the joint centre — distance $R_i$ and angle $\theta_{mi}$ from the
  segment's neutral axis (the foot axis = ankle → fifth metatarsophalangeal
  joint) — and rotated with the segment angle $\theta_s(t)$.
- **Effective line of pull:** the direction in which the tendon *leaves* the
  segment, not the anatomical straight line. Soleus and gastrocnemius pull
  straight from the Achilles insertion; flexor hallucis longus wraps a
  pulley-like groove on the talus and the peronei wrap the lateral malleolus,
  so their effective directions ($F'_h$, $F'_p$) differ from the muscle's.
- With stress known, each muscle force follows; summing muscle forces with the
  ground ($R_g$) and ankle ($R_a$) reaction forces gives the total
  compressive and shear load on the joint.
- **Results** (Fig. 5.21, stance 0.22 s):
  - Compression peaks above **5500 N** (> 11 × body weight); the reaction force
    contributes only ~1000 N, muscles over 4500 N (reaction < 20% of total).
  - Shear (perpendicular to the tibia): the reaction would shear the talus
    posteriorly (~800 N); the soleus/gastrocnemius pull the foot anteriorly,
    cutting total shear to ~300 N — an **antishear mechanism**.

## Equations and numbers

Plantarflexor moment with the equal-stress assumption (five muscles):

$$M_a(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{5} PCA_i \times S_{ei}(t) \times d_{ei}(t) \tag{5.12}$$

Attachment point of muscle $i$ on a segment whose joint centre is at
$(X_i, Y_i)$:

$$X_{mi}(t) = X_i(t) + R_i\cos[\theta_{mi}(t) + \theta_s(t)] \tag{5.13a}$$
$$Y_{mi}(t) = Y_i(t) + R_i\sin[\theta_{mi}(t) + \theta_s(t)] \tag{5.13b}$$

Moment arm, with $\beta_i$ the angle between the effective line of pull and the
line from the insertion to the joint centre:

$$d_{ei} = R_i \sin\beta_i \tag{5.14}$$

Since $PCA_i$ and $d_{ei}(t)$ are known, $S_e(t) = M_a(t) / \sum_i PCA_i\,d_{ei}(t)$
(equal stress), then $F_{ei}(t) = PCA_i\,S_e(t)$.

## Relevance to migera

Low for animation, moderate for physics intuition. Eq. 5.13 is the same
"attachment point in the segment's local polar frame, rotated by the segment
angle" transform that any muscle/spring attachment in a rig uses. Eq. 5.14
plus the effective-line-of-pull idea (tendons wrapping pulleys) would matter
only if muscle actuators are reintroduced. The 11 × body-weight joint load is a
reminder that ragdoll joint constraint forces (which omit muscle
compression) massively understate real joint loading.
