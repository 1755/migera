# 5.3.1 Indeterminacy in muscle force estimates

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.3.1, pp. 131–132 ·
> [open PDF at p. 131](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=144) ·
> Up: [5.3 Bone-on-bone forces during dynamic conditions](./INDEX.md)

Even with an exact net joint moment, the individual muscle forces are
indeterminate: one equation, many unknown forces, each with its own changing
moment arm. Resolving it needs a per-joint anatomical model plus simplifying
assumptions.

## Key ideas

- Link-segment models treat each joint as a hinge driven by a torque motor,
  so their joint reaction force equals the contact force. Real muscles are
  **linear** motors that add compression and shear across the joint, so
  those muscle forces must be overlaid on the free-body diagram (ligaments
  too, at the extremes of range — ignored here).
- Fig. 5.18: fifteen major muscles produce the sagittal moments at ankle,
  knee and hip; nine of them act at the knee. During weight bearing all three
  joint moments influence knee angle, so knee motion cannot be tied to one
  moment or one muscle combination.
- Each muscle's line of action, and so its moment arm, changes continuously.
- Resolving the indeterminacy needs valid individual muscle force estimates
  and a detailed kinematic/anatomical model of lines of pull relative to the
  joint centre — a separate model per joint. (First raised in Section 1.3.5.)

## Equations and numbers

$$M_j(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_e} \mathbf F_{ei}(t)\times\mathbf d_{ei}(t) - \sum_{i=1}^{N_f} \mathbf F_{fi}(t)\times\mathbf d_{fi}(t) \tag{5.11}$$

$N_e$, $N_f$: numbers of extensor and flexor muscles; $\mathbf F_{ei}(t)$:
force in the $i$th extensor at time $t$; $\mathbf d_{ei}(t)$: its moment-arm
vector (likewise $f$ for flexors).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 131 (PDF 144): 5.3 intro (torque motors vs linear motors) and Eq. 5.11.
- p. 132 (PDF 145): Fig. 5.18 (fifteen lower-limb muscles), symbol list.

## Relevance to migera

Low–moderate. migera's ragdoll uses one PD torque motor per joint — the
link-segment idealization — so it sidesteps the indeterminacy entirely; that
is a deliberate simplification, not a gap. The note matters if muscle-like
actuators (Lugaru-style springs) ever return: Eq. 5.11 shows co-contraction is
free in the net moment, i.e. joint stiffness can be raised without changing
the net torque — the biological analogue of raising PD gain at a fixed
target.
