# 5.2.5 Combined force plate and kinematic data

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.2.5, pp. 123–124 ·
> [open PDF at p. 123](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=136) ·
> Up: [5.2 Force transducers and force plates](./INDEX.md)

In stance the foot's distal load is the measured ground reaction, applied at
the COP. Plugging it into the same three equations of
[5.1](../5.1-link-segment-equations-free-body-diagram.md) gives the ankle
reaction and moment, which then start the recursion up the leg. The pushoff
example yields a large plantarflexor moment, −128.5 N·m.

## Key ideas

- The foot free-body diagram (Fig. 5.13) carries: ground force $F_x, F_y$ at
  the COP (acting on the foot, positive sense), weight $mg$ at the COM, and
  the unknown ankle reaction $F_{ax}, F_{ay}$ and ankle moment $M_a$ drawn
  positive.
- Coordinates are absolute (same frame as the kinematics), with the COP at
  ground level $y = 0$.
- Sign reading: a negative $M_a$ (clockwise on the foot, subject walking to the
  right) is a plantarflexor moment; here it pushes the foot off, rotating it
  clockwise about the metatarsophalangeal joint.

## Equations and numbers

Example 5.4 (Fig. 5.13, frame 35 of the walking trial, late stance/pushoff).
Coordinates in cm: ankle $a=(89.7, 16.0)$, foot COM $(95.3, 8.4)$, metatarsal
marker $(100.9, 0.9)$, COP $p=(103.2, 0)$. Foot $m=1.12$ kg,
$I=0.01$ kg·m²; $a_x=3.25$, $a_y=1.78$ m/s², $\alpha=-45.35$ rad/s²;
$F_x=160.25$ N, $F_y=765.96$ N; $g=9.81$.

$$F_{ax} + F_x = m a_x \Rightarrow F_{ax} = 1.12(3.25) - 160.25 = -156.6\ \text{N}$$
$$F_{ay} + F_y - mg = m a_y \Rightarrow F_{ay} = 1.12(1.78) - 765.96 + 1.12(9.81) = -753.0\ \text{N}$$

Moments about the foot COM (lever arms from the coordinates above):

$$M_a + 0.084F_x + 0.079F_y - 0.056F_{ay} - 0.076F_{ax} = I\alpha = 0.01(-45.35)$$
$$M_a = -0.4535 - 13.46 - 60.51 - 42.17 - 11.90 = -128.5\ \text{N·m}$$

(The printed line writes the last two terms as $-0.056\times753.0$ and
$-0.076\times156.6$, i.e. with the negative $F_{ay}, F_{ax}$ already
substituted.)

Scale check: 128.5 N·m at pushoff versus 1.34 N·m at the same ankle in swing
([5.1](../5.1-link-segment-equations-free-body-diagram.md), Example 5.2) — two
orders of magnitude; the ground reaction's lever arm dominates everything.

## Relevance to migera

High. This is the stance-foot base case of an inverse-dynamics oracle and a
complete test fixture (inputs → −156.6 N, −753.0 N, −128.5 N·m). It also
gives the order of magnitude for ankle torque authority: a pushoff needs
~130 N·m on an ~70–80 kg body, while swing needs ~1 N·m. A ragdoll foot/ankle
controller sized for swing cannot hold stance, and one sized for stance is
wildly over-strong in swing — the kind of stance/swing-dependent ceiling the
ragdoll's per-joint `max_torque` (an acceleration, see
[avian: apply angular acceleration, not torque](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-apply-angular-acceleration-not-torque.md))
may need.
