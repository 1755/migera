# 5.2.9 Kinematics and kinetics of the inverted pendulum model

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.2.9, pp. 130–131 ·
> [open PDF at p. 130](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=143) ·
> Up: [5.2 Force transducers and force plates](./INDEX.md)

Treating the body as an inverted pendulum pivoting at the ankles gives one
equation linking the two independent measures: the COP − COM distance is
proportional to the COM's horizontal acceleration, with the opposite sign.
The same law holds sagittally and frontally; what differs is which muscles
move the COP. Measured data fit it with |r| ≈ 0.85–0.95.

## Key ideas

- Balance is a continuous task; the base of support ranges from one foot
  (running) to four points (a football stance). The COM must stay within the
  base, or move safely between the feet when it is temporarily outside it
  (running, single support in walking).
- The COP's position relative to the COM decides the direction of the
  pendulum's angular acceleration ([5.2.8](./5.2.8-center-of-mass-vs-center-of-pressure.md)).
  Full analysis: Winter et al. (1998).
- **Sagittal (A/P):** $COP_x = M_a / R$, with $M_a$ the *sum* of left and
  right ankle plantarflexor moments and $R$ the total vertical reaction at
  the ankles. The ankles alone move the A/P COP.
- **Frontal (M/L):** $COP_z = M_t / R$, $M_t = M_{al} + M_{ar} + M_{hl} +
  M_{hr}$ (left/right frontal ankle and hip moments): four "torque motors" at
  the corners of the closed parallelogram formed by the two legs and the
  pelvis. In side-by-side stance the **hip abductor/adductor moments
  dominate** (Winter et al., 1996); ankle invertor/evertor moments are
  negligible for balance. M/L balance is a load/unload shift of weight
  between the legs driven from the hips.
- **Validity test:** COP and COM are measured independently, so the
  correlation of (COP − COM) with COM acceleration measures the model's
  validity. Quiet standing (Gage et al., 2004): r = −0.954 A/P, r = −0.85 M/L
  (lower M/L because M/L COM displacement is only ~45% of A/P). Gait
  initiation and termination (Jian et al., 1993): r ≈ −0.94 in both
  directions.

## Equations and numbers

$$COP_x - COM_x = -\frac{I_s\,\ddot{COM}_x}{W h} \tag{5.9}$$
$$COP_z - COM_z = -\frac{I_f\,\ddot{COM}_z}{W h} \tag{5.10}$$

- $I_s$, $I_f$: moment of inertia of the body about the ankles in the
  sagittal / frontal plane (kg·m²)
- $\ddot{COM}_x$, $\ddot{COM}_z$: forward / medial–lateral COM acceleration
- $W$: body weight above the ankles (N); $h$: height of the COM above the
  ankles (m)

Derivation sketch (not in the book, consistent with it): for a small sway
angle $\theta$, $COM_x \approx h\theta$ and $I_s\ddot\theta = W(COM_x -
COP_x)$; substituting $\ddot\theta = \ddot{COM}_x/h$ gives Eq. 5.9. If the
body is lumped as a point mass, $I_s = (W/g)h^2$ and Eq. 5.9 reduces to
$COP_x - COM_x = -(h/g)\ddot{COM}_x$ — the linear inverted pendulum /
zero-moment-point relation used in robotics and games. Its characteristic
rate $\omega = \sqrt{g/h}$ is a *divergence* rate (the uncontrolled COM runs
away as $e^{\omega t}$), not an oscillation frequency; for an adult with the
COM $h \approx 0.9$ m above the ankles, $\omega \approx 3.3$ s⁻¹ and
$h/g \approx 0.09$ s². Winter's $I_s$ includes segment inertia about each COM,
so $I_s$ is slightly larger than $(W/g)h^2$.

## Relevance to migera

High — this is the most directly usable equation in the chapter.
- **Balance controller for the active ragdoll:** to accelerate the COM by
  $\ddot{COM}$ the controller must place the COP at
  $COM - (I/Wh)\ddot{COM}$; if that point is outside the support polygon the
  demand is infeasible and a step (or fall) must follow. A/P authority lives
  in the ankle joints; M/L authority lives in the **hips**
  (abduction/adduction of the stance leg), not the ankles — so a ragdoll's
  side-to-side balance should be driven through the hip joints.
- **Pelvis sway in walking / stance:** for a procedurally swayed pelvis
  (`src/character/anim/pelvis.rs`, `gait.rs`), the implied COP from Eq. 5.9
  must stay under the stance foot; a sway whose acceleration implies a COP
  outside the foot is physically impossible and reads as "floaty". Since
  $h/g \approx 0.09$ s², a 1 m/s² lateral COM acceleration needs the COP
  ~9 cm to the other side of the COM — about a foot width, a quick sanity
  bound (my arithmetic, point-mass form).
- **Test oracle:** compute COM from segment masses and the animated pose,
  differentiate twice, apply Eq. 5.9, and assert the COP lies inside the
  contact polygon every frame.
