# 5.2.4 Synchronization of force plate and kinematic data

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.2.4, pp. 122–123 ·
> [open PDF at p. 122](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=135) ·
> Up: [5.2 Force transducers and force plates](./INDEX.md)

Kinematic and force data come from separate systems, so they must be put on a
common clock: record the imaging system's sync pulses alongside the force
signals.

## Key ideas

- Optoelectric systems provide sync pulses to record with the force records.
- TV systems must emit a pulse per field.
- Cine is the awkward case: a pulse per frame, and its frame number must reach
  whoever digitizes the film.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 122 (PDF 135): the whole section (one paragraph).

## Relevance to migera

Low: lab hardware. The general lesson — a force sample and a pose sample from
different clocks must be aligned before being combined — applies only if
migera ever mixes the fixed-step physics schedule (avian) with
variable-rate frame data in one analysis; compare them at matching
timestamps.
