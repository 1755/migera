# 5.2.1 Multidirectional force transducers

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.2.1, p. 117 ·
> [open PDF at p. 117](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=130) ·
> Up: [5.2 Force transducers and force plates](./INDEX.md)

A multidirectional force transducer is simply two or three single-axis
transducers mounted at right angles; the practical difficulty is making the
applied force act through each unit's central axis.

## Key ideas

- A force transducer outputs an electrical signal proportional to force. All
  common types sense a tiny strain the force produces:
  - **Strain gauge:** a calibrated metal plate/beam deforms (well under 1%),
    changing resistances in a bridge circuit (Section 3.2.3) → voltage
    imbalance ∝ force.
  - **Piezoelectric** (e.g. quartz): crystal deformation changes surface
    charge, converted electronically to a force signal.
  - **Piezoresistive:** crystal deformation changes resistance, unbalancing a
    bridge like a strain gauge.
- Bi-/tri-directional transducers = orthogonal single-axis units; off-axis
  loading is the main error source.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 117 (PDF 130): section 5.2 intro (transducer types) and 5.2.1 (one
  paragraph).

## Relevance to migera

Low: measurement hardware. The only transferable idea is that a force is
read from a small elastic deflection — the same thing a soft contact or a
penalty spring does in a physics engine.
