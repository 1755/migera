# 5.2.7 A note about the wrong way to analyze moments of force

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §5.2.7, pp. 126–127 ·
> [open PDF at p. 126](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=139) ·
> Up: [5.2 Force transducers and force plates](./INDEX.md)

The FRFV ("floor reaction force vector") method projects the ground reaction
vector up the body and takes each joint moment as the vector's magnitude times
its perpendicular distance to the joint centre (Fig. 5.15). It is wrong in
magnitude, wrong in its causal reading, and useless whenever the foot is off
the ground. Extending it to the neck predicts an extensor neck moment several
times any leg moment — the reductio.

## Key ideas

Three errors:

1. **Magnitude:** it omits the mass × acceleration and $I\alpha$ terms of the
   segments between the ground and the joint. The correct recursion subtracts
   them joint by joint, so by the hip all foot, leg and thigh inertia is
   accounted for. Error: negligible at the ankle, small but significant at the
   knee, large at the hip (Wells, 1981).
2. **Polarity / causality:** the GRF is a *reaction* to gravity and muscle
   forces; it reflects the moment, it does not cause it. A plantarflexor
   moment moves the COP forward of the ankle; with no ankle muscle force the
   vector would stay under the ankle. (In early stance with a posteriorly
   directed vector, FRFV labels ankle plantarflexor, knee flexor, hip
   extensor.)
3. **Swing:** with no ground contact there is no vector, so no moments —
   a serious gap for walking swing, worse in running and jumping.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 126 (PDF 139): the method and Fig. 5.15 (vector projected past ankle,
  knee, hip, neck with lever arms $d_a, d_k, d_h, d_n$).
- p. 127 (PDF 140): the three errors.

## Relevance to migera

Moderate, as a guard. The FRFV shortcut is exactly what is tempting in a game
("torque = contact force × lever arm to the joint") for estimating how hard a
standing ragdoll's hip or knee must work, or for a cheap balance heuristic. It
is acceptable at the ankle only; for knee and hip use the full
distal-to-proximal recursion of
[5.1](../5.1-link-segment-equations-free-body-diagram.md), and for swing it
gives nothing at all.
