# 1.2 Biomechanics and its relationship with physiology and anatomy

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.2, pp. 7–9 ·
> [open PDF at p. 7](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=20) ·
> Up: [Chapter 1 — Biomechanics as an interdiscipline](./INDEX.md)

The neuromuscular system converges through four summation levels (Fig. 1.6).
The one biomechanics computes, the **net moment of force at a joint**, should
be read as a neurological signal: it is the final pattern the central nervous
system (CNS) wants. Because the system is redundant, variability shrinks at
each level (EMG > moments > kinematics), and the redundancy is also what
lets the body compensate for deficits.

## Key ideas

The neuromuscular system releases metabolic energy as controlled tendon
tension. That tension depends on fibre type and fatigue state, and acts
alongside passive structures (ligaments, articular surfaces, bone).

**Four levels of integration** (Fig. 1.6):
1. **Neural.** All excitatory and inhibitory synaptic inputs sum on the
   α motoneuron, the "final common pathway" to one motor unit.
2. **Tendon.** The twitches of all active motor units superpose. They are
   recruited by the size principle (Henneman) and modulated by muscle
   length and velocity. The result is tendon force.
3. **Joint.** The moment is the algebraic sum of every muscle's force ×
   moment arm (agonists and antagonists, one- and two-joint muscles) plus
   passive-structure moments. It is in N·m, but it is the CNS's intended
   output.
4. **Synergy.** Moments at two or more joints cooperate toward one goal.
   Example: the **support moment**, the combined lower-limb extensor
   activity that prevents collapse under gravity in stance (Winter 1980,
   1984).

**Consequences:**
- Bernstein's (1967) "principle of equal simplicity": the CNS controls at
  the joint or synergy level, since controlling every muscle would be too
  complex.
- **Variability**: EMG > joint moments > kinematics. Redundancy makes the
  system adaptable.
- Case (Winter 1989): a knee-osteoarthritis patient avoided her quadriceps
  to reduce painful bone-on-bone force. She substituted hip extensors and
  kept near-normal gait, and kept the pattern long after knee replacement.
  So a pathological moment pattern is either a disturbance or a CNS
  adaptation.

## Equations and numbers

From Fig. 1.6 (p. 8), for motor units *i* and muscles *j*:

$$F_j = \sum_i \text{Twitch}_i \times \text{Rate}_i \qquad
M = \sum_{i} F_i(\text{ext})\,d_i + \sum_{j} F_j(\text{fl})\,d_j \qquad
M_s = M_a + M_k + M_h$$

Here *d* is the moment arm, and ext/fl mean extensor and flexor muscles.
M_s is the support moment: the sum of the ankle, knee and hip moments with
extensor positive.

## Relevance to migera

This supports migera's control architecture. The active ragdoll controls
**one PD torque per joint**, not individual muscles, which is exactly
Winter's level 3 and Bernstein's argument. A synergy term like the support
moment (a shared extensor "stiffness" across hip, knee and ankle in stance)
is a natural next layer for `ragdoll.rs` strength and for leg IK stance
logic. The variability ordering also means tuning should aim at joint
moments or kinematics, not at matching muscle-level detail.
