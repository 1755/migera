# 1.1.3 Assessment and interpretation

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.1.3, pp. 6–7 ·
> [open PDF at p. 6](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=19) ·
> Up: [1.1 Measurement, description, analysis, and assessment](./INDEX.md)

The purpose of every assessment is a **decision** about a movement: keep
going, change the plan, or cancel a procedure. Analysis that answers no
question asked of it contained no information. Assessing from a
description alone is speculative.

## Key ideas

- Examples of assessment questions: a coach asks whether the movement's
  mechanical energy improved and why; a surgeon wants the change in knee
  moments a month after surgery; a researcher tests a theory of neural
  control against the motor response to a perturbation.
- A decision can be positive (confirm the current treatment), a new plan
  (from an initial assessment), or negative (cancel surgery and prescribe
  therapy).
- **Assessing from the description**: clinicians often read force-plate
  ground reaction force curves directly (Fig. 1.5). A trained eye can see
  that a pattern changed, but not *why*. Winter calls this speculative and
  low in information about the underlying cause.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 6 (PDF 19): the purpose of assessment and the example questions.
- p. 7 (PDF 20): Fig. 1.5, a vertical GRF curve used in gait diagnosis, and the critique.

## Relevance to migera

Small, but it restates a verification rule migera already follows: a
check must be framed so that it can answer a specific question. A GRF-like
curve (for example the ragdoll's foot contact force over a stride) can show
that a gait changed. It cannot show which joint or controller caused the
change. For that, measure the joint quantities directly, for example over
BRP (see
[full-strength read-back hides the physics](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/full-strength-readback-hides-the-physics.md)).
