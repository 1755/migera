# 1.1.1 Measurement, description, and monitoring

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.1.1, pp. 3–5 ·
> [open PDF at p. 3](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=16) ·
> Up: [1.1 Measurement, description, analysis, and assessment](./INDEX.md)

A measuring device can have its data described in many ways, and one
description can come from different devices. Monitoring means documenting
change over time. It never explains why the change happened.

## Key ideas

- **Description forms**: pen-recorder curves, time histories of landmark
  coordinates, **stick diagrams**, and single outcome measures (gait speed,
  load lifted, jump height). A camera is the measuring device. The plots
  are the description.
- **Time-history plots** of one landmark show detailed changes and, to a
  trained eye, velocity and acceleration.
- **Stick diagrams**: each segment is a line, the lines are joined, and the
  figure is repeated at equal time intervals. This gives the whole planar
  movement at a glance. One stick figure needs a full page of coordinate
  data.
- The same coordinate data feed any analysis: reaction forces, moments,
  energy, efficiency.
- **Monitoring** notes change over time, such as a patient's progress. It
  cannot show that an intervention *caused* the change. Winter criticises
  coaches and therapists who assume it does. Only an analysis at the motor
  level can show the mechanism.

## Relevance to migera

A useful verification lesson. A gizmo stick-figure view (joint positions
drawn as lines) is a *description*, and comparing two screenshots over time
is *monitoring*. Neither proves the cause of a change. That needs a
measurement of the specific quantity, which is the same point as
migera's rule to state a yes/no claim before looking at a pose image (see
[gizmos need show-real-mesh off](../../../character-animation/animation-core/gizmos-need-show-real-mesh-off.md)).
