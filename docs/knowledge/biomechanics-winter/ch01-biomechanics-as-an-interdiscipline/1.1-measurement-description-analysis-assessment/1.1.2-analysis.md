# 1.1.2 Analysis

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.1.2, pp. 5–6 ·
> [open PDF at p. 5](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=18) ·
> Up: [1.1 Measurement, description, analysis, and assessment](./INDEX.md)

Analysis is any mathematical operation on calibrated, low-noise data that
presents it in another form, or combines sources into a variable that
cannot be measured directly. The flagship example is the **inverse
solution**: kinematics + anthropometry + external forces go into a
link-segment model, and net joint forces and moments come out.

## Key ideas

- **Simple analysis, the EMG linear envelope** (Fig. 1.3): full-wave
  rectify (take the absolute value), then low-pass filter. Winter notes
  that the low-pass filter has the same transfer function as the path from
  a neural pulse to its muscle twitch. The figure shows rectified EMG at
  f_c = 100 Hz (the pen recorder's bandwidth) and envelopes at
  **f_c = 3 Hz** for medial hamstrings and soleus in walking.
- **Complex analysis, the link-segment model** (Fig. 1.4). Neural inputs
  (EMG, stimulation) go into a *muscle model*, which gives muscle forces
  and moments. These plus external forces go into the *link-segment model*,
  which outputs the observed kinematics (θ, ω, α, and x, y with their
  derivatives) and mechanical energy.
- **Inverse solution**: run Fig. 1.4 backwards. From accurate kinematics
  and anthropometry, compute the net forces and muscle moments that must
  have caused the motion. These cannot be measured in vivo.
- **Muscle models** could similarly predict individual muscle forces from
  neural drive, length, velocity and cross-sectional area.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 5 (PDF 18): definition of analysis, and Fig. 1.3 (rectified EMG vs linear envelope).
- p. 6 (PDF 19): Fig. 1.4, the neural → muscle model → link-segment model → kinematics block diagram, and the inverse solution.

## Relevance to migera

Fig. 1.4 is the architecture of a physics-driven character. migera's
active ragdoll runs it *forward*: PD controllers play the muscle model and
produce joint torques, and avian plays the link-segment model and produces
kinematics. An inverse solution run on migera's animated pose (joint
moments needed to realise the target motion) is the natural way to set
per-joint PD torque ceilings. The linear envelope (rectify + low-pass) is
also a cheap model of "activation" if a strength signal ever needs a
muscle-like lag.
