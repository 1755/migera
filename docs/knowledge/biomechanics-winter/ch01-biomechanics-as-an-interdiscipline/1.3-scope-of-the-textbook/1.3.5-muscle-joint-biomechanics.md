# 1.3.5 Muscle and joint biomechanics

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.3.5, p. 11 ·
> [open PDF at p. 11](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=24) ·
> Up: [1.3 Scope of the textbook](./INDEX.md)

The mechanical properties of muscle and joints fall outside the other
categories but cannot be ignored. Active and passive muscle properties, and
the articular surfaces that stabilise joints and limit their range, shape
every movement.

## Key ideas

The section is a list of questions the book answers:
- How does tension vary with muscle **length** and with **velocity**?
- What are the passive properties: mass, **elasticity**, **viscosity**?
- What do joints contribute, and what do **two-joint muscles** gain?
- How does activity differ when a muscle is **lengthening** vs
  **shortening**?
- How does neural **recruitment** shape tension?
- Which **mathematical muscle models** fit best?
- How do you locate a joint's **centre of rotation**?

It also notes that passive articular structures limit the range of
movement.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 11 (PDF 24): the whole section.

## Relevance to migera

Medium. Passive elasticity and viscosity are a spring-damper, the same
model as migera's per-bone damped springs and the ragdoll PD. The
force-velocity asymmetry (lengthening vs shortening) and passive range
limits bear on joint limits and on any torque ceiling that varies with
state.
