# 1.3.1 Signal processing

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.3.1, pp. 9–10 ·
> [open PDF at p. 9](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=22) ·
> Up: [1.3 Scope of the textbook](./INDEX.md)

The 4th edition collects signal processing into one chapter (Chapter 2).
The reason: every measured or computed variable is a time-domain signal
and must be treated as one.

## Key ideas

- Signals include EMG, forces, displacements, accelerations, energies,
  powers and moments.
- Standard operations: frequency analysis, digitising, analog or digital
  filtering, correlating, and ensemble-averaging waveforms.
- A signal's characteristics decide the **sampling rate**, the **minimum
  record length** and the **filter cutoff**.
- Correlation and covariance methods reveal total-limb and total-body
  motor patterns.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 9–10 (PDF 22–23): the one-paragraph rationale. The chapter itself is at printed pp. 14–44.

## Relevance to migera

Medium. Smoothing and differentiating motion data is exactly what spring
and filter code in `src/character/anim` does. Chapter 2's frequency view
(gait energy lies below about 6 Hz) is the justification for choosing a
spring or low-pass bandwidth.
