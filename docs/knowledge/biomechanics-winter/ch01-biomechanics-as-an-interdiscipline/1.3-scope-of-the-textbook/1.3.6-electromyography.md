# 1.3.6 Electromyography

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.3.6, pp. 11–12 ·
> [open PDF at p. 11](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=24) ·
> Up: [1.3 Scope of the textbook](./INDEX.md)

Neural control cannot be separated from the movement. The electromyogram
(EMG) carries each muscle's final control signal and is the primary signal
for describing the *input* to the muscular system.

## Key ideas

- EMG shows **which muscles** are responsible for a joint moment and
  whether **antagonists** are co-active.
- Since EMG relates to tension, several EMG-driven biomechanical models
  exist.
- EMG also carries information about **fibre-type recruitment** and
  **fatigue state**.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 11–12 (PDF 24–25): the whole section.

## Relevance to migera

Low. migera has no muscles or measured EMG. The one transferable idea is
co-activation: antagonists firing together raise joint stiffness without
net moment. This is the biological counterpart of raising a PD
controller's gains (strength) without changing its target.
