# 1.3.2 Kinematics

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.3.2, p. 10 ·
> [open PDF at p. 10](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=23) ·
> Up: [1.3 Scope of the textbook](./INDEX.md)

Kinematic variables describe a movement independently of the forces that
cause it: linear and angular displacements, velocities and accelerations of
anatomical landmarks.

## Key ideas

- **Landmarks**: segment centres of gravity, joint centres of rotation,
  segment ends, bony prominences.
- **Relative vs absolute reference.** Relative coordinates and angles are
  given in an anatomical frame that changes from segment to segment. Joint
  angles are relative. Absolute ones are referred to an external frame.
- **2D angle convention**: horizontal to the right is 0°, and
  counterclockwise is positive.
- Every displacement and rotation is a vector. Along one direction or
  about one axis, it is treated as a scalar signal.
- In **3D**, each segment has its own axis system, so its planes need not
  line up with those of neighbouring segments.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 10 (PDF 23): the whole section.

## Relevance to migera

Relative vs absolute is migera's local vs world rotation. Winter's joint
angle is a difference of two absolute segment angles, which in 3D becomes
the parent-inverse × child composition that migera's retargeting gets wrong
if bind frames are ignored. The per-segment 3D axis point is the same trap
as a pose delta naming a world axis.
