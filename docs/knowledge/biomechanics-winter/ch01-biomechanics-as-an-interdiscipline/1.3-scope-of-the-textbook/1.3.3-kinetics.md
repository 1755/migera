# 1.3.3 Kinetics

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.3.3, pp. 10–11 ·
> [open PDF at p. 10](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=23) ·
> Up: [1.3 Scope of the textbook](./INDEX.md)

Kinetics is the study of the forces that cause movement. Winter makes it
the book's main focus, because only kinetics gets at the *cause* of a
movement and at the nervous system's strategies and compensations.

## Key ideas

- **Internal forces**: muscle activity, ligaments, and friction in muscles
  and joints. **External forces**: ground, external loads, active bodies
  (a tackler), and passive sources (wind).
- The kinetic toolbox covers moments of force at joints, the mechanical
  power flowing to or from muscles, and the energy changes that result.
- The concepts are taught in 2D. 3D adds a third force component in the
  global reference system (GRS) and two more moment components, but within
  any one plane the interpretation is the same as in 2D.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 10–11 (PDF 23–24): the whole section.

## Relevance to migera

High. The moments, powers and energies are the quantities an active
ragdoll's controllers produce and consume. The Chapter 5–6 methods (and
the Appendix A numbers) give realistic joint-torque and power magnitudes
for tuning PD gains and strength.
