# 1.4 References

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.4, pp. 12–13 ·
> [open PDF at p. 12](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=25) ·
> Up: [Chapter 1 — Biomechanics as an interdiscipline](./INDEX.md)

Six works back the chapter's claims about neural convergence, joint-level
control and the support moment.

## Key ideas

| Work | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Bernstein, N. A., *The Coordination and Regulation of Movements*, Pergamon, 1967 | Degrees-of-freedom problem; CNS control at the joint or synergy level. The classic motor-control argument behind per-joint controllers |
| Winter, D. A., "Overall principle of lower limb support during stance phase of gait," *J. Biomech.* 13:923–927, 1980 | Defines the **support moment** (sum of ankle, knee, hip extensor moments). Useful for stance leg control |
| Winter, D. A., "Kinematic and kinetic patterns in human gait: variability and compensating effects," *Human Movement Sci.* 3:51–76, 1984 | Cited for variability EMG > moments > kinematics, and the resulting adaptability. Also the support moment |
| Winter, D. A., "Biomechanics of normal and pathological gait: implications for understanding human locomotor control," *J. Motor Behav.* 21:337–355, 1989 | The knee-replacement compensation case |
| DeLuca, LeFever, McCue, Xenakis, "Control scheme governing concurrently active motor units during voluntary contractions," *J. Physiol.* 329:129–142, 1982 | Motor-unit control (low relevance to migera) |
| Henneman & Olson, "Relations between structure and function in the design of skeletal muscle," *J. Neurophysiol.* 28:581–598, 1965 | Size principle of recruitment (low relevance to migera) |

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 12–13 (PDF 25–26): the reference list.

## Relevance to migera

Winter 1980 and 1984 are the ones worth fetching if migera builds a
support-moment-style stance controller. Bernstein is the conceptual
justification for per-joint PD rather than per-muscle actuation.
