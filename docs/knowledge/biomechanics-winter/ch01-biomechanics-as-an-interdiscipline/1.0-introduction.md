# 1.0 Introduction

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §1.0, pp. 1–2 ·
> [open PDF at p. 1](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=14) ·
> Up: [Chapter 1 — Biomechanics as an interdiscipline](./INDEX.md)

Winter defines the biomechanics of human movement as the interdiscipline
that **describes, analyzes and assesses** human movement. The same physical
and biological principles apply whether the subject is a patient's gait, a
worker lifting a load, or an elite athlete. Only the task and the level of
detail asked for change.

## Key ideas

- The users are many: surgeons, coaches, rehabilitation engineers,
  therapists, prosthetists, orthotists, equipment designers.
- **Kinesiology** is the umbrella science of human movement. It blends
  psychology, motor learning, exercise physiology and biomechanics.
- Biomechanics builds on physics, chemistry, mathematics, physiology and
  anatomy. Winter names Leonardo, Galileo, Lagrange, Bernoulli, Euler and
  Young as early "biomechanicians".
- The three verbs (describe, analyze, assess) set up §1.1, which separates
  them.

## Relevance to migera

Framing only. The point that one set of principles covers every movement
task supports reusing a single link-segment and physics model for all of
migera's characters, rather than special-casing each motion.
