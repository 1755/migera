# 2.1.8 Applications of cross-correlations

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.1.8, pp. 23–26 ·
> [open PDF at p. 23](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=36) ·
> Up: [2.1 Auto- and cross-correlation analyses](./INDEX.md)

Cross-correlation measures what two signals share and how far one leads the
other. Its headline gait result: during walking the paraspinal muscles fire
top-down — neck first, lumbar last — so the head is stabilized ahead of the
trunk and its fore-aft acceleration is about a quarter of the pelvis's.

## Key ideas

**2.1.8.1 Cross-talk in surface EMG.** Electrodes close together pick up a
common signal; $R_{xy}$ quantifies that shared part. Details deferred to
§10.2.5.

**2.1.8.2 Delay between physiological signals** (Prince et al., 1994).
- Problem: the head's anterior/posterior (A/P) acceleration in walking is
  strongly attenuated relative to the hip's. How do the paraspinal muscles
  achieve that?
- EMG at nine vertebral levels (C7 to L4) was ensemble-averaged over the
  stride (Fig. 2.7) and each level cross-correlated with the L4 profile.
- The profiles show 2nd-harmonic peaks (twice per stride) at the weight
  acceptance of each foot. C7 amplitude is lower than L4 (less inertial load
  above C7), and C7 fires earlier.
- Phase shifts relative to L4 grow smoothly (exponential fit) from the lower
  levels up to C7 (Fig. 2.8): a **top-down anticipatory strategy** —
  stabilize the head first, then cervical, thoracic, lumbar.
- Healthy elderly lose this strategy: head/hip acceleration ratio 41.9% vs.
  22.7% in young adults (p < .02; Wieman, 1991).

**2.1.8.3 Synergy and coactivation.** Cross-correlating agonist/antagonist
EMG distinguishes coactivation (in phase) from reciprocal out-of-phase
synergy; e.g. left/right gluteus medius during prolonged standing
(Nelson-Wong et al., 2008), detailed in Chapter 11.

## Equations and numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Head A/P acceleration (young, walking) | 0.48 m/s² |
| Hip A/P acceleration | 1.91 m/s² |
| C7 lead over L4 (one subject) | ≈ 70 ms |
| Head/hip acceleration, young | 22.7% |
| Head/hip acceleration, healthy elderly | 41.9% (p < .02) |
| Subjects (lag study) | 10 young adults, levels C7, T2–T12, L2 vs. L4 |

## Relevance to migera

- **Head/spine secondary motion target.** A walking character's head should
  move fore-aft far less than its pelvis — roughly a quarter (0.48/1.91 ≈
  0.25) in young adults, about 0.4 for an elderly gait. That is a concrete
  number for a head-stabilization or look-at counter-motion layer
  (`lookat.rs`, the spine terms in `phase.rs`), and a ratio a test can
  assert on sampled world-space accelerations.
- **Twice-per-stride trunk activity** (weight acceptance of each foot) is
  the same 2nd-harmonic rhythm as the head bob in `phase.rs`.
- **Top-down lead** suggests spine layers above the pelvis should lead,
  not lag, the pelvis motion they counteract — the opposite of a naive
  spring chain, where the head trails. Treat this as a design hint, not a
  measured migera result.
