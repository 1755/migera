# 2.1.2 Formulae for auto- and cross-correlation coefficients

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.1.2, pp. 16–17 ·
> [open PDF at p. 16](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=29) ·
> Up: [2.1 Auto- and cross-correlation analyses](./INDEX.md)

Auto- and cross-correlation are Pearson's r evaluated between one time
series and a copy (of itself, or of a second series) shifted by τ. The
result is not one number but a function of the shift, $R(\tau)$, bounded to
[−1, +1] by normalizing with the zero-shift values.

## Key ideas

- **Autocorrelation** correlates a signal with itself; **cross-correlation**
  correlates two different signals.
- The user chooses the range of shifts (positive = forward, negative =
  backward); the output is a curve of correlation vs. τ ("phase shift").
- Continuous form: the sum becomes an integral over the record length T and
  the mean divides by T instead of N.
- **Both signals must be zero-mean**, exactly as Pearson subtracts means —
  see [2.1.5](./2.1.5-removing-mean-bias.md) for what happens if you forget.

## Equations and numbers

Autocorrelation ($x$ zero-mean):

$$
R_{xx}(\tau) = \frac{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T x(t)\,x(t+\tau)\,dt}{R_{xx}(0)}
\qquad (2.2)
$$

Cross-correlation ($x$, $y$ zero-mean):

$$
R_{xy}(\tau) = \frac{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T x(t)\,y(t+\tau)\,dt}{\sqrt{R_{xx}(0)\,R_{yy}(0)}}
\qquad (2.3)
$$

Here $R_{xx}(0)$ in the denominator means the *unnormalized* numerator at
τ = 0, i.e. the mean square (variance, for zero-mean x). The denominators
play the role of $s_x^2$ and $s_x s_y$ in Eq. 2.1.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 16–17 (PDF 29–30): Eqs. 2.2, 2.3 and the side-by-side comparison with Pearson.

## Relevance to migera

$R_{xy}(\tau)$ is the tool for measuring the lag between two limb curves —
e.g. confirming the left and right hip angles of a procedural walk are
half a stride apart, or checking `gait.rs`'s documented rule that peak knee
flexion *leads* peak thigh swing, as a number instead of a screenshot. For a
periodic gait use the circular (wrap-around) form from
[2.1.6](./2.1.6-digital-correlation-implementation.md).
