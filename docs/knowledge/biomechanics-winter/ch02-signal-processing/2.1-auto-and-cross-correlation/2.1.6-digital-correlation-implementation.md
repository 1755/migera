# 2.1.6 Digital implementation of auto- and cross-correlation functions

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.1.6, pp. 22–23 ·
> [open PDF at p. 22](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=35) ·
> Up: [2.1 Auto- and cross-correlation analyses](./INDEX.md)

On sampled data the integrals become sums over sample index n, with the
means subtracted inside the sum. A linear shift of ±τ samples leaves fewer
overlapping products; for a periodic signal such as gait, wrap the record on
itself (circular correlation) and use every sample at every shift.

## Key ideas

- The shift range is chosen up front, and it limits N: with 1000 samples
  and τ = ±100, only 800 cross products exist at every shift, so N = 800.
- **Circular correlation**: when the signal is periodic (one or more whole
  strides), index $n+\tau$ modulo the record length. No data are lost and
  every shift uses the same N.

## Equations and numbers

As printed:

$$
R_{xx}(\tau) = \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big[(x(n)-\bar x)(x(n+\tau)-\bar x)\big]}
{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(x(n)-\bar x)^2}
\qquad (2.8)
$$

$$
R_{xy}(\tau) = \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big[(x(n)-\bar x)(y(n+\tau)-\bar y)\big]}
{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(x(n)-\bar x)(y(n)-\bar y)}
\qquad (2.9)
$$

**Erratum — use this denominator instead.** The printed Eq. 2.9 normalizes
by the zero-lag *cross*-covariance, which is inconsistent with the
continuous Eq. 2.3 and is not bounded to ±1 (it is undefined when x and y
are uncorrelated at zero lag — exactly the sine-vs-cosine case of §2.1.4).
The form consistent with Eq. 2.3 is

$$
R_{xy}(\tau) = \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}(x(n)-\bar x)(y(n+\tau)-\bar y)}
{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_n (x(n)-\bar x)^2\;\cdot\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_n (y(n)-\bar y)^2}}
$$

Circular version for a record of N samples covering whole cycles: replace
$y(n+\tau)$ by $y((n+\tau) \bmod N)$.

## Relevance to migera

This is the implementation recipe for a gait-lag test helper: sample a
procedural joint curve at a fixed number of points over exactly one stride
(see [2.3.2](../2.3-ensemble-averaging/2.3.2-time-base-normalization-to-100-percent.md)),
subtract means, compute the circular $R_{xy}$ with the √-variance
denominator, and read the argmax as a stride fraction. Circular is the
right choice because a procedural cycle is exactly periodic by
construction; a linear correlation would needlessly discard samples and bias
the lag near the ends.
