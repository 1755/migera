# 2.1.4 Three properties of the cross-correlation function

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.1.4, pp. 20–21 ·
> [open PDF at p. 20](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=33) ·
> Up: [2.1 Auto- and cross-correlation analyses](./INDEX.md)

Unlike the autocorrelation, the cross-correlation is asymmetric and its peak
sits at whatever shift best aligns the two signals — which makes the lag of
the peak a direct measurement of the delay between them.

## Key ideas

1. **Not even.** Shifting y forward and backward against x gives different
   cross products, so $R_{xy}(\tau) \ne R_{xy}(-\tau)$ in general. The sign
   of the peak lag therefore says *which* signal leads — compute both sides.
2. **Peak not necessarily at τ = 0.** The largest positive peak is where
   the signals are most in phase; the largest negative peak where they are
   most out of phase.
   - Example: x = sine, y = cosine, same frequency. At τ = 0 they are 90°
     apart and the products over a cycle sum to 0. Shifting the cosine one
     way by 90° puts them in phase ($R_{xy} = 1$); the other way puts them
     180° apart ($R_{xy} = -1$).
   - Physiological example (Fig. 2.5): a stimulated nerve/muscle signal
     recorded at two sites S1, S2 a distance d apart; $R_{S1S2}(\tau)$ peaks
     at $\tau_1 = t$, the transit time, so conduction velocity $V = d/t$.
3. **Frequency domain.** The Fourier transform of $R_{xy}$ is the *cross
   spectral density*, from which the *coherence function* (frequencies
   common to both signals) is computed. Used to identify a system's transfer
   function when the input's frequency content cannot be controlled — e.g.
   EMG → muscle force (Bobet & Norman, 1990).

## Equations and numbers

- Lag estimate: $\hat\tau = \arg\max_\tau |R_{xy}(\tau)|$; conduction
  velocity $V = d/\hat\tau$ (Fig. 2.5).
- Sine vs cosine: $R_{xy}(0) = 0$, $R_{xy}(\pm T/4) = \pm 1$ (sign depends
  on the shift direction convention).

## Relevance to migera

The central tool for phase-relationship tests on a procedural walk:
cross-correlate left vs. right hip angle and assert the peak lag is 50% of
the stride (anti-phase legs); cross-correlate knee flexion vs. thigh swing
and assert the knee leads, as `gait.rs` documents. Because the function is
not even, the *sign* of the lag carries the "which leads" claim — checking
only |lag| would miss a knee that trails (the same class of blindness as
[unsigned measurements](../../../engineering-practice/testing/unsigned-measurements-cannot-see-direction.md)).
Remove means first ([2.1.5](./2.1.5-removing-mean-bias.md)); joint angles
carry large offsets.
