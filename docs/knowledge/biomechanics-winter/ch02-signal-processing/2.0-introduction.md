# 2.0 Introduction

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.0, p. 14 ·
> [open PDF at p. 14](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=27) ·
> Up: [Chapter 2 — Signal processing](./INDEX.md)

All biomechanical variables are time-varying signals, whether measured
directly (accelerometer, force plate, EMG) or computed (joint moments,
reaction forces, energy, power), and all benefit from the same signal
processing: cleaning, averaging, correlating, and transforming to the
frequency domain.

## Key ideas

- Measured vs. derived signals are processed identically — a computed joint
  moment is as much a "signal" as a raw marker trajectory.
- The chapter covers: auto- and cross-correlation (§2.1), Fourier analysis
  and its consequences for sampling rate and record length (§2.2), the
  *theory* of digital filtering (§2.2.4.4), and ensemble averaging of
  repetitive movements (§2.3).
- Division of labour with later chapters: the *application* of digital
  filtering to kinematics is in Chapter 3 (§3.4.4); analog filtering of EMG
  is in Chapter 10.
