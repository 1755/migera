# 2.2.1 Introduction — time domain vs. frequency domain

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.2.1, pp. 26–27 ·
> [open PDF at p. 26](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=39) ·
> Up: [2.2 Frequency analysis](./INDEX.md)

A signal's spectrum — the amplitudes of all its harmonics, lowest to
highest — is not an optional analysis: it decides three processing
parameters up front. The highest frequency sets the sampling rate, the
lowest sets the record length, and the signal/noise split sets the filter
cutoff.

## Key ideas

- Frequency-domain analysis rests on the Fourier transform (Fourier, 1807).
- **Highest frequency → sampling rate** (sampling theorem, §2.2.4.2).
- **Lowest frequency → record length** (§2.2.4.3).
- **Where signal ends and noise begins → low-pass cutoff** (§2.2.4.4).
- The section aims to make the reader an informed consumer of commercial
  analysis software, not to derive transform theory.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 26–27 (PDF 39–40): the whole introduction.

## Relevance to migera

Framing only, but the three-way rule transfers: when sampling a procedural
curve for a test, the gait's highest meaningful harmonic sets how many
samples per stride you need, and the stride period sets the minimum window
(at least one whole cycle, several for anything stochastic like a layered
idle).
