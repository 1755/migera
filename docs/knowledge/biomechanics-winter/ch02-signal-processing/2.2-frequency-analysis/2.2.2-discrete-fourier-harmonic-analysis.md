# 2.2.2 Discrete Fourier (harmonic) analysis

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.2.2, pp. 27–30 ·
> [open PDF at p. 27](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=40) ·
> Up: [2.2 Frequency analysis](./INDEX.md)

Any perfectly periodic signal is a dc offset plus sinusoids at integer
multiples of its fundamental frequency, each with its own amplitude and
phase. The Fourier coefficients are *averages over the analysis window*: a
harmonic present only part of the time is reported as a weaker harmonic
present all the time.

## Key ideas

- **ac and dc.** An alternating (ac) signal changes continuously — periodic,
  random, or both — around a dc bias (Fig. 2.9).
- **Discrete vs. continuous spectra (Fig. 2.10).** A sine has one spectral
  line at $f_0$; a periodic non-sinusoid has lines at harmonics
  ($f_0, 3f_0, 5f_0$ for a square wave); a random signal has a continuous
  spectrum between a lowest $f_1$ and highest $f_2$.
- **Harmonics.** The *n*-th harmonic is $n f_0$; the fundamental is the
  first. The sum of harmonics with the right amplitudes is the Fourier
  series.
- **Stationarity caveat.** Eq. 2.10 assumes constant $V_n$, $\theta_n$ over
  the whole period. Real movement signals violate this (a foot marker is
  high-frequency in swing, low-frequency in stance), so the coefficients
  are averages — the root of the reconstruction error in §2.2.4.5.
- **Cost.** For N samples, harmonics run from n = 1 to the Nyquist limit
  n = N/2; each coefficient needs N products, so ≈ N² operations in total,
  dominated by evaluating sines and cosines.

## Equations and numbers

Fourier series, $\omega_0 = 2\pi f_0$:

$$
V(t) = V_{dc} + V_1\sin(\omega_0 t + \theta_1) + V_2\sin(2\omega_0 t + \theta_2) + \dots + V_n\sin(n\omega_0 t + \theta_n)
\qquad (2.10)
$$

Square wave of amplitude V (odd harmonics only):

$$
V(t) = \frac{4V}{\pi}\left(\sin\omega_0 t + \tfrac13\sin 3\omega_0 t + \tfrac15\sin 5\omega_0 t + \cdots\right)
\qquad (2.11)
$$

Triangular wave of duration 2t repeating every T seconds (as printed):

$$
V(t) = \frac{2Vt}{T}\left[\frac12 + \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^2\cos\omega_0 t + \left(\frac{2}{3\pi}\right)^2\cos 3\omega_0 t + \cdots\right]
\qquad (2.12)
$$

Coefficients over one period T:

$$
a_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_0^T x(t)\cos n\omega_0 t\,dt \qquad (2.13)
\qquad
b_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_0^T x(t)\sin n\omega_0 t\,dt \qquad (2.14)
$$

$$
c_n = \sqrt{a_n^2 + b_n^2}, \qquad \theta_n = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{a_n}{b_n}\right) \qquad (2.15)
$$

With the sine-phase form of Eq. 2.10, $b_n = c_n\cos\theta_n$ and
$a_n = c_n\sin\theta_n$, so in code use `theta = atan2(a_n, b_n)` to get
the quadrant right. The dc term is $V_{dc} = a_0/2$ = the mean.

Discrete form for N samples over T, as printed:

$$
a_n = \frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N} x_i\cos(n\omega_0 i/N) \qquad (2.16)
\qquad
b_n = \frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N} x_i\sin(n\omega_0 i/N) \qquad (2.17)
$$

**Erratum — code this instead.** The printed argument $n\omega_0 i/N$ has
units of rad/s; the angle of sample *i* in harmonic *n* is
$2\pi n i/N$ (equivalently $n\omega_0 t_i$ with $t_i = iT/N$), and the sum
runs over the N distinct samples $i = 0 \dots N-1$ (i = N repeats i = 0 for
a periodic record):

$$
a_n = \frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i\cos\frac{2\pi n i}{N}, \qquad
b_n = \frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i\sin\frac{2\pi n i}{N}
$$

Operation count: N/2 harmonics × N samples = N²/2 per coefficient type,
N² total.

## Relevance to migera

- **`phase.rs` is Eq. 2.10 truncated to one term.** `PhaseOscillator` is
  `amplitude * sin(harmonic * clock + offset)` — a single harmonic with
  amplitude and phase. `gait.rs` explains why one sinusoid cannot be a leg
  (60/40 stance/swing asymmetry, non-sinusoidal knee, knee leading thigh)
  and uses piecewise curves instead. Eq. 2.10 is the principled middle
  ground: a *sum* of a few harmonics per joint captures exactly those
  asymmetries, and [2.2.4](./2.2.4-spectrum-analysis-applications.md) says
  how many are needed (≈ 7 for a toe trajectory).
- **Fitting reference data.** Eqs. 2.13–2.15 (corrected discrete form)
  turn one stride of Appendix A joint angles into a compact coefficient
  table — a few numbers per joint that a procedural walk could replay or be
  tested against.
- **Test assertion.** Harmonic content is a robust, phase-free check:
  e.g. "≥ 90% of the head-bob's power is in harmonic 2" is stronger than
  counting zero crossings, and it cannot be fooled by a small high-frequency
  wobble.
