# 2.3.1 Examples of ensemble-averaged profiles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.3.1, pp. 41–42 ·
> [open PDF at p. 41](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=54) ·
> Up: [2.3 Ensemble averaging of repetitive waveforms](./INDEX.md)

A normative ensemble — mean curve ± one standard deviation over 0–100% of
stride — lets one patient's curve be judged at a glance: wherever it leaves
the band, something is abnormal. Normalizing moments by body mass first
halves the spread across subjects.

## Key ideas

- **Fig. 2.20:** support, hip, knee and ankle moments of an above-knee
  amputee (dashed) over the averages of 29 young adults (solid, dotted ±1 SD;
  Winter, 1995). Deviations at each joint are obvious and can guide therapy
  or prosthesis adjustment.
- **Mass normalization.** Moments are expressed in N·m/kg; dividing by body
  mass reduced inter-subject variability by about 50%.
- **Time normalization.** Each subject's stride (in seconds) is re-sampled
  onto a common 0–100% stride axis before averaging — the procedure of
  [2.3.2](./2.3.2-time-base-normalization-to-100-percent.md).
- Axes in Fig. 2.20: extensor (hip, knee, support) and plantarflexor
  (ankle) moments positive. Read off the plot (approximate): normal ankle
  plantarflexor moment peaks ≈ 1.6 N·m/kg near 45–50% of stride; the
  support moment is double-humped at ≈ 1 N·m/kg; all moments fall to ≈ 0
  after toe-off at ≈ 60% stride.

## Equations and numbers

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Normal subjects in ensemble | 29 young adults |
| Band shown | mean ± 1 SD |
| Moment units | N·m/kg (divided by body mass) |
| Variability reduction from mass normalization | ≈ 50% |

## Relevance to migera

This is the shape of a gait-plausibility test: build (or take from the
book) a mean ± SD band per joint over % stride, time-normalize the
procedural walk's curve the same way, and assert it stays inside — or
report the fraction of the stride outside the band. For migera's
rotation-space stack, joint *angle* bands are the directly comparable
quantity (Appendix A gives one subject's stride; the book's ensembles are
in Winter 1995). Moments matter only for the ragdoll/PD side. Normalizing
by body mass (moments) or leg length (distances) is what lets one band fit
different character sizes.
