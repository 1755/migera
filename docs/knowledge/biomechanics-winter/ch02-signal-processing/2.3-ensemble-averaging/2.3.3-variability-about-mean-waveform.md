# 2.3.3 Measure of average variability about the mean waveform

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §2.3.3, p. 43 ·
> [open PDF at p. 43](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=56) ·
> Up: [2.3 Ensemble averaging of repetitive waveforms](./INDEX.md)

The single-measure coefficient of variation σ/X̄ generalizes to a whole
waveform: take the RMS of the standard deviation across the N points of the
stride and divide by the mean absolute value of the averaged curve. One
dimensionless number then says how repeatable a movement pattern is.

## Key ideas

- Scalar CV = σ / X̄ — variability relative to size.
- Waveform CV: numerator is the "average σ" over the stride, computed as
  the square root of the mean variance; denominator is the mean of |X̄ᵢ|
  (absolute value, because gait curves cross zero and a signed mean could
  be near 0).
- Some researchers use the RMS of the signal in the denominator; the book
  notes this changes CV very little.
- Context from §2.3: intra-subject joint *angles* are much less variable
  than joint *moments*; comparing CVs across joints underlies the
  covariance/synergy analysis of Chapter 11.

## Equations and numbers

$$
CV = \frac{\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_i^2}}{\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_i|}
\qquad (2.20)
$$

- $X_i$: ensemble-average value at point i of the time-normalized stride.
- $\sigma_i$: standard deviation across the strides (or subjects) at point i.
- N: points per stride (e.g. 100 after [2.3.2](./2.3.2-time-base-normalization-to-100-percent.md)).
- Commonly reported ×100 as a percentage (convention; the book's formula is the ratio).

## Relevance to migera

- **Variability of the procedural walk.** A deterministic walk has CV = 0
  stride-to-stride; the non-commensurate `phase.rs` layers add some. Eq.
  2.20 measures how much, so "alive but not jittery" can be tuned against a
  number (compare with human intra-subject CVs, which the book's Chapter 11
  and Winter 1995 report).
- **Normalized error metric.** Replacing $\sigma_i$ with the point-wise
  difference between a procedural curve and a reference curve gives
  $\sqrt{\overline{(x-r)^2}}/\overline{|r|}$ — an amplitude-sensitive,
  unitless score that complements the shape-only correlation of 2.1.1. A
  test can assert it stays below the reference ensemble's own CV ("within
  normal human variability").
- **Pitfall.** The denominator is small for curves that hover near zero
  (e.g. a mean-subtracted hip angle), inflating CV; normalize by the curve's
  range or use the unshifted angle when that happens.
