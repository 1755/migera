# 10.3.1 Full-wave rectification

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.3.1, pp. 270–271 ·
> [open PDF at p. 270](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=283) ·
> Up: [10.3 Processing of the electromyogram](./INDEX.md)

Raw EMG averages to zero (the amplifier high-passes at ~10 Hz), so no
low-pass filter can recover contraction level from it directly. Taking the
absolute value gives a signal that never crosses zero and whose mean rises and
falls with contraction strength.

## Key ideas

- **Full-wave rectification** = $|x(t)|$, conventionally positive; half-wave
  keeps only one polarity (listed but not recommended in the section intro).
- The rectified signal carries a bias level proportional to activity; its
  own quantitative use is limited — it is the **input** to the linear
  envelope (10.3.2) and the integrators (10.3.3).
- Standalone use: semi-quantitative, visual assessment of the phasic on/off
  pattern of muscle groups.
- Unit: millivolts, same as the raw EMG.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 269 (PDF 282): §10.3 intro, the five common processing schemes.
- p. 270 (PDF 283): Fig. 10.15 — one EMG processed six ways (raw, rectified,
  linear envelope, integrated over contraction, 50 ms reset integrator,
  level-reset integrator; pulse = 38 mV·s); rectification text.

## Relevance to migera

Indirect. If migera ever derives an "effort" or "activation" signal from an
oscillating quantity (a joint's PD torque, a spring's error, procedural
tremor noise), rectify before low-passing, otherwise the smoothed result
averages to zero. Rectify-then-low-pass is exactly the linear envelope.
