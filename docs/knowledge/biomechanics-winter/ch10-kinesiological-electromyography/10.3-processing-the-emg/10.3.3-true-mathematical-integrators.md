# 10.3.3 True mathematical integrators

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.3.3, pp. 272–273 ·
> [open PDF at p. 272](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=285) ·
> Up: [10.3 Processing of the electromyogram](./INDEX.md)

A true integrator measures area under the rectified EMG and only ever grows
while activity is present (unit mV·s). Resetting it on a timer or at a
threshold turns it into a trend follower similar to the linear envelope.

## Key ideas

- **Integrate over the contraction.** Start at a preset time, read out at the
  end of one or several contractions. Result in mV·s; dividing by contraction
  time is the only true way to get the *average* EMG (mV).
- **Time-reset integrator.** Reset to zero every 40–200 ms. Each peak is the
  average over the previous interval (a stepped "moving" average, in mV·s);
  the peaks sum to the whole-contraction integral. Too long an interval
  cannot follow fast changes; too short lets noise into the trend. Divide by
  the interval to report mV.
- **Level-reset integrator.** Integrate until a preset level, reset, repeat.
  Activity is encoded as **reset frequency**, reminiscent of neural firing
  rate; total counts × threshold (with known gain) recovers total mV·s.
- Fig. 10.15 shows all three next to the linear envelope; the level-reset
  example uses 38 mV·s per pulse.

## Equations and numbers

- Whole-contraction mean: $\bar{E} = \frac{1}{t_c}\int_0^{t_c} |e(t)|\,dt$
  (mV), where the integral is in mV·s.
- Reset interval for the time-reset form: 40–200 ms.

## Relevance to migera

Low but concrete. A running integral of |effort| is the natural input for a
fatigue accumulator (total work done by a joint), whereas the linear envelope
is the right signal for instantaneous force. Keep the two distinct, as the
book insists on for the names.
