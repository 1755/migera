# 10.2.2 Input impedance

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.2.2, pp. 258–260 ·
> [open PDF at p. 258](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=271) ·
> Up: [10.2 Recording of the electromyogram](./INDEX.md)

The electrode-skin interfaces and the amplifier's input impedance form a
voltage divider; keep the amplifier impedance far above the electrode
impedance ($R_i \gg R_s$) so the EMG is not attenuated.

## Key ideas

- The amplifier is differential: two active inputs plus a common (third)
  electrode (Fig. 10.5a).
- Electrode-skin impedance depends on skin thickness and cleaning, electrode
  area, and paste temperature (it warms after attachment). Indwelling
  electrodes have higher impedance (small bare area).
- Modelled as series resistors $R_{s1}$, $R_{s2}$ (really an impedance with a
  capacitive part) feeding input impedance $R_i$ (Fig. 10.5b).

## Equations and numbers

Divider: $V_{in} = V_{EMG}\,\dfrac{R_i}{R_i + R_{s1} + R_{s2}}$ (implied by the
book's worked numbers).

| $R_{s1}=R_{s2}$ | $R_i$ | 2 mV EMG appears as |
|---|---|---|
| 10 kΩ | 80 kΩ | 1.6 mV (0.2 mV lost across each electrode) |
| 1 kΩ | 1 MΩ | 1.998 mV as printed (the divider formula gives 1.996 mV; either way negligible loss) |

Recommendations: input impedance ≥ 1 MΩ, skin prepared to ≤ 1 kΩ; indwelling
electrodes up to 50 kΩ need ≥ 5 MΩ input.
