# 10.2.5 Cross-talk in surface electromyograms

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.2.5, pp. 265–268 ·
> [open PDF at p. 265](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=278) ·
> Up: [10.2 Recording of the electromyogram](./INDEX.md)

Adjacent surface electrodes have overlapping pick-up zones (0.5–1.5 cm deep),
so they record some of the same motor units. The squared peak of their
normalized cross-correlation estimates the shared fraction, which falls off
steeply with electrode separation.

## Key ideas

- Fig. 10.12: seven electrodes across the mid-thigh, each with an arc-shaped
  pick-up zone; overlap between neighbours is cross-talk. Deep muscles
  (vastus intermedius, adductor magnus) need indwelling electrodes.
- **Manual resistance test:** have the subject contract muscle A alone, then
  B alone, and check the other channel stays quiet. Example (Fig. 10.13):
  peroneus longus (evertor) vs tibialis anterior and lateral gastrocnemius;
  visual EMG feedback helps the subject isolate the contraction.
- **Cross-correlation test** (when manual tests are impossible): record
  ~10 s of moderate isometric contraction, compute normalized $R_{xy}(\tau)$
  for $\tau$ within ±30 ms. Electrode pairs parallel to the fibres see common
  units in phase, so the peak is at $\tau = 0$.
- The **shared fraction is $R_{xy}^2$**, not $R_{xy}$.
- Reduce cross-talk with smaller electrodes, or by differentiating the EMG:
  distant (cross-talk) units give longer, lower-frequency m.u.a.p.'s, so a
  differentiator emphasizes the nearby ones.

## Equations and numbers

$$R_{xy}(\tau) = \frac{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T x(t)\,y(t-\tau)\,dt}{\sqrt{R_{xx}(0)\,R_{yy}(0)}} \qquad (10.7)$$

$R_{xx}(0)$, $R_{yy}(0)$: mean squares of $x$, $y$ over $T$.

| Electrode-pair separation | Peak $R_{xy}$ | Cross-talk $R_{xy}^2$ |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0 cm | 0.60 | 36% |
| 2.5 cm | 0.48 | 23% |
| 5.0 cm | 0.24 | 6% |
| 7.5 cm | 0.14 | 2% |

## Relevance to migera

Low. The reusable idea is methodological: to measure how much two signals
share, use the *squared* normalized cross-correlation at the best lag, not the
raw coefficient. That applies to any A/B comparison of two animation
channels (e.g. how much one joint's motion is explained by another's).
