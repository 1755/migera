# 10.2.3 Frequency response

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.2.3, pp. 260–261 ·
> [open PDF at p. 260](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=273) ·
> Up: [10.2 Recording of the electromyogram](./INDEX.md)

The amplifier must pass every frequency present in the EMG equally. Surface
EMG lives between ~10 and 1000 Hz (most power 20–200 Hz); pick cutoffs to
reject movement artifact below and pass the m.u.a.p. content above.

## Key ideas

- Bandwidth = $f_2 - f_1$, where $f_1$, $f_2$ are the lower and upper
  cutoffs at which gain has fallen to 0.707 of mid-band (power to 0.5, the
  **half-power points**, −3 dB).
- EMG spectrum spans ~5–2000 Hz; surface m.u.a.p.'s are longer so have
  negligible power beyond 1000 Hz (Fig. 10.7).
- Interference: ECG has power to ~100 Hz (hard to remove near the thorax);
  mains hum (60 Hz N. America, 50 Hz Europe) sits in the middle of the EMG
  band and cannot be filtered out; movement artifacts lie at 0–10 Hz and are
  removed by setting $f_1$ ≈ 20 Hz, else fix the cabling or use
  skin-mounted microamplifiers.
- Fig. 10.8 shows the waveform distortion when $f_1$ or $f_2$ is varied
  from 30 to 400 Hz.

## Equations and numbers

$$\text{gain (dB)} = 20\log_{10}(\text{linear gain}) \qquad (10.3)$$

Gain 1000 = 60 dB; at the cutoffs 57 dB.

| Electrodes | Recommended band | For m.u.a.p. pattern recognition |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | 10–1000 Hz | upper cutoff 5 kHz |
| Indwelling | 20–2000 Hz | upper cutoff 10 kHz |
