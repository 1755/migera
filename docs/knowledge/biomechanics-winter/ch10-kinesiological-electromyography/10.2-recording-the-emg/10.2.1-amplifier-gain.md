# 10.2.1 Amplifier gain

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.2.1, p. 258 ·
> [open PDF at p. 258](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=271) ·
> Up: [10.2 Recording of the electromyogram](./INDEX.md)

Choose a gain so the largest expected EMG fills, but does not overdrive, the
recorder's range; always report amplitude as it was at the electrodes, in mV.

## Key ideas

- Gain = output voltage / input voltage (2 mV in × 1000 = 2 V out).
- The right gain depends on the downstream recorder (pen, tape, scope,
  computer); most have adjustable input stages.
- Amplifier noise is the random high-frequency signal with the inputs
  shorted together.
- Report amplitudes referred to the electrodes, independent of gain.

## Equations and numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Surface EMG max (at MVC) | 5 mV peak-to-peak |
| Indwelling EMG max | up to 10 mV |
| Single m.u.a.p. | ≈ 100 µV |
| Amplifier noise | ≤ 50 µV, preferably 20 µV |
| Selectable gain range | 100 – 10,000 |
