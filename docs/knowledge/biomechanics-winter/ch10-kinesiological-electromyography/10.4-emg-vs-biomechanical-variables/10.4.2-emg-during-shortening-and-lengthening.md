# 10.4.2 Electromyogram during muscle shortening and lengthening

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.4.2, pp. 275–276 ·
> [open PDF at p. 275](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=288) ·
> Up: [10.4 Relationship between electromyogram and biomechanical variables](./INDEX.md)

When a muscle changes length under load, the same EMG produces very different
tensions: less while shortening, more while lengthening. EMG therefore
reports the **activation** of the contractile element, and the force-velocity
curve converts that activation into tendon force. Consequence: lowering a
load (negative work) takes considerably less EMG than raising it (positive
work).

## Key ideas

- Komi (1973): subjects produced **maximal** tension on an isokinetic
  machine while the muscle shortened or lengthened at controlled velocities.
  EMG amplitude stayed fairly constant although tension decreased with
  shortening velocity and increased in eccentric contraction — the
  force-velocity relation of §9.2.
- Interpretation: EMG ≈ activation state of the contractile element, not
  the tension measured at the tendon.
- Komi et al. (1987): EMG for negative work is considerably less than for
  the same amount of positive work. If EMG is a relative measure of
  metabolism, this matches the lower metabolic cost of negative work.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 275 (PDF 288): motivation (positive/negative work requires length change).
- p. 276 (PDF 289): Komi's results and interpretation (one paragraph).

## Relevance to migera

- **Activation and force are separated by a velocity-dependent gain.** In
  migera's ragdoll a joint's strength sets a torque ceiling independent of
  joint velocity. The book's data say a real muscle at fixed activation
  yields *more* force when being stretched (resisting, braking) and *less*
  when shortening fast. A force-velocity multiplier on the torque ceiling —
  larger when the PD is opposing the joint's current motion, smaller when
  assisting it — would make a hit-and-recover or a landing look like braking
  muscle rather than a uniform spring. Not built; a design lead grounded in
  §9.2.
- **Eccentric is cheap.** If a game-side fatigue/effort budget is ever
  charged from ragdoll effort, charge braking (negative-work) phases less than
  driving (positive-work) phases at the same torque.
