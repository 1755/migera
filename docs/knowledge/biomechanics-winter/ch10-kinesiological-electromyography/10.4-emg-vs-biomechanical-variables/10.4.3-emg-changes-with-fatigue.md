# 10.4.3 Electromyogram changes during fatigue

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.4.3, pp. 276–277 ·
> [open PDF at p. 276](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=289) ·
> Up: [10.4 Relationship between electromyogram and biomechanical variables](./INDEX.md)

A fatigued muscle produces less tension for the same activation; its twitches
become weaker *and slower*, extra motor units must be recruited to hold a
force, and units start firing in synchronized bursts that show up as an
8–10 Hz tremor. In the EMG this reads as **higher amplitude and a lower
frequency spectrum**, measured by median or mean power frequency.

## Key ideas

- **Cause:** the tissue cannot supply metabolism at the contractile element
  — ischaemia (too little oxygen) or local depletion of substrates.
- **Mechanical signature:** decreased tension at constant activation
  (constant surface EMG or stimulation rate). Holding a constant tension
  instead needs recruitment of new units to offset the falling firing rate
  of those already active (Vredenbregt & Rau 1973). Recruited units show
  lower peak twitch tension and **longer contraction times**; net effect,
  less tension.
- **EMG signature:** individual m.u.a.p. shapes are invisible in a strong
  contraction, but autocorrelation shows longer average duration, and the
  spectrum loses high frequencies (Kadefors et al. 1973). Attributed to:
  1. lower conduction velocity, below the unfatigued 4.5 m/s (Mortimer 1970;
     Krogh-Lund & Jørgensen 1991);
  2. large, fast units with short m.u.a.p.'s dropping out;
  3. **synchronization**: normally units fire independently (EMG ≈ sum of
     randomly timed m.u.a.p.'s); in fatigue they fire in synchronized bursts,
     raising EMG amplitude and producing an **8–10 Hz tremor** in both EMG
     and force, of neurological origin.
- **Measures:** median power frequency $f_m$ and mean power frequency (MPF).
  They are practically interchangeable in how they correlate with
  independent fatigue measures (Kerr & Callaghan 1999). $f_m$ also correlates
  with low-band (15–45 Hz) amplitude and with the >95 Hz / low-band ratio
  (Allison & Fujiwara 2002).

## Equations and numbers

Median power frequency $f_m$ splits the power spectrum in half (Eq. 10.9):

$$\int_0^{f_m} X^2(f)\,df = \int_{f_m}^{\infty} X^2(f)\,df = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty} X^2(f)\,df$$

Mean power frequency, the spectral centroid up to the maximum analysed
frequency $F$ (Eq. 10.10):

$$\text{MPF} = \frac{\int_0^{F} f\,X^2(f)\,df}{\int_0^{F} X^2(f)\,df}\ \text{Hz}$$

$X(f)$: harmonic amplitude at $f$; $X^2(f)$: power at $f$.

Representative magnitudes (Krogh-Lund & Jørgensen 1991; triceps brachii held
at 25% MVC to the limit of endurance, per the reference title):

| Measure | Change with fatigue |
|---|---|
| Conduction velocity | −10% |
| Median power frequency | −45% |
| rms EMG amplitude | +250% |
| Tremor | 8–10 Hz, synchronized firing |

## Relevance to migera

Fatigue is a natural **gameplay-driven parameter** for a character, and the
book gives three separable, mechanically meaningful effects to drive from a
single fatigue value (design leads, none built):

1. **Weaker:** lower the ragdoll strength / torque ceiling at a given
   command (less tension at constant activation).
2. **Slower:** lengthen the activation time constant $T$ of the linear-envelope
   filter ([10.3.2](../10.3-processing-the-emg/10.3.2-linear-envelope.md)),
   since fatigued twitches have longer contraction times — a tired character
   reacts and recovers more sluggishly.
3. **Shakier:** add a small 8–10 Hz oscillation to targets or torque as
   fatigue rises, the physiological tremor band. That band sits right at
   the ~10 Hz ceiling the explicit-integration bound puts on a ζ = 1 PD at
   64 Hz physics, so inject tremor into the *target*, never by raising PD
   frequency, and expect the PD to track it only partially.

A fatigue value could accumulate from integrated |effort|
([10.3.3](../10.3-processing-the-emg/10.3.3-true-mathematical-integrators.md)),
charging eccentric/braking effort less
([10.4.2](./10.4.2-emg-during-shortening-and-lengthening.md)). The spectral
measures themselves (Eqs. 10.9–10.10) have no use in migera beyond being a
standard spectral-centroid definition.
