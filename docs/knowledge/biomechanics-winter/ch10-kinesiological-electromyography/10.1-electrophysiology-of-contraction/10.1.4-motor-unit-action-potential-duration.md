# 10.1.4 Duration of the motor unit action potential

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.1.4, p. 256 ·
> [open PDF at p. 256](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=269) ·
> Up: [10.1 Electrophysiology of muscle contraction](./INDEX.md)

The duration of a recorded m.u.a.p. is set by electrode area, conduction
velocity and fibre depth. Slower conduction (fatigue, myopathy) or greater
distance makes potentials longer and lower in frequency; because peak
amplitude per phase is unchanged, the rectified average *rises*, which is the
mechanism behind the fatigue signature in §10.4.3.

## Key ideas

- Larger electrode area → longer recorded m.u.a.p.; surface durations are
  about twice needle durations.
- For fixed electrodes, duration depends on **propagation velocity** (faster
  → shorter) and **depth** below the electrode.
- In fatigue and some myopathies the mean velocity of recruited units drops,
  durations lengthen, phase areas grow, and the full-wave-rectified average
  appears to increase.
- Measuring duration in free movement needs spectral analysis (a frequency
  shift) or autocorrelation (average m.u.a.p. duration).
- Distance attenuates amplitude (Eq. 10.2) *and* lengthens duration
  (Fig. 10.4): deeper units contribute lower-frequency content.

## Equations and numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Needle-electrode m.u.a.p. duration | 3–20 ms |
| Surface-electrode duration | ≈ 2× needle |
| Conduction velocity (normal) | ≈ 4 m/s (Buchthal 1955); §10.4.3 quotes 4.5 m/s |
| Mean power frequency of a 50-fibre unit, 1 mm away | 160 Hz |
| Same, 20 mm away | 25 Hz |

Fig. 10.4 setup: 50-fibre unit, 4 mm² bipolar electrodes, 11 mm spacing,
distances 1, 10, 20 mm.
