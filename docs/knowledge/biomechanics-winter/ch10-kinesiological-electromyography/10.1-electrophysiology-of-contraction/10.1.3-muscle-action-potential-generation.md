# 10.1.3 Generation of a muscle action potential

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.1.3, pp. 251–256 ·
> [open PDF at p. 251](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=264) ·
> Up: [10.1 Electrophysiology of muscle contraction](./INDEX.md)

A depolarization wave followed by a repolarization wave travels along the
fibre; an electrode sees it as a current source/sink pair (a dipole) whose
potential depends on distance, so one electrode records a biphasic wave and a
bipolar pair records a triphasic difference. Small electrodes close to the
fibres give larger, shorter, higher-frequency potentials.

## Key ideas

- **Electrode types.** Surface: ~1 cm Ag/AgCl disks, average superficial
  activity, more reproducible. Indwelling (needle, fine-wire): needed for
  deep muscles and fine movements. Motor unit territory: 2–15 mm diameter.
- **Dipole model** (valid when the fibre is >1 mm from the electrode): the
  depolarization is a current source $I$, repolarization a sink $-I$,
  separated by $b$ = 0.5–2.0 mm.
- As the dipole approaches the electrode ($r_1<r_2$) the potential is
  positive and rising, peaks, crosses zero directly beneath ($r_1=r_2$), then
  goes negative: a **biphasic** wave.
- **Electrode geometry** (Fig. 10.2, Fuglevand et al. 1992): a finite
  electrode averages point-source potentials. A strip parallel to the fibre
  sums phase-shifted copies (lower, longer); a strip across the fibre sums
  distance-attenuated copies. Conclusion: use the smallest practical
  electrode area, which also reduces cross-talk.
- **Pick-up zone** grows with fibre diameter (dipole current) and with the
  number of fibres in the unit: ~0.5 cm for a small (50-fibre) unit, ~1.5 cm
  for the largest (2500-fibre) units. So surface EMG rarely picks up adjacent
  muscles unless the target is very small.
- **Bipolar recording** (Fig. 10.3): two electrodes see nearly the same wave
  shifted in time; the difference is **triphasic**. Closer spacing makes the
  difference approach a time derivative, pushing the spectrum higher.

## Equations and numbers

Potential at a point electrode a distance $r$ from a source $I$ in an
isotropic medium of conductivity $\sigma$ (Eq. 10.1), and the net source+sink
potential (Eq. 10.2):

$$\Phi = \frac{I}{4\pi\sigma}\cdot\frac{1}{r} \qquad
\Phi = \frac{I}{4\pi\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{r_1}-\frac{1}{r_2}\right)$$

$r_1$, $r_2$: distances from electrode to source and sink; both change with
time as the wave propagates.

## Relevance to migera

Negligible. This is sensor physics. The one idea with an analogue elsewhere
is "a differenced pair acts like a differentiator and boosts high
frequencies", the same effect that makes finite-difference velocities noisy
(ch. 3).
