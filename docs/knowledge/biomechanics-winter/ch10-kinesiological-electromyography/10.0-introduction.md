# 10.0 Introduction

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §10.0, p. 250 ·
> [open PDF at p. 250](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=263) ·
> Up: [Chapter 10 — Kinesiological electromyography](./INDEX.md)

The EMG is the electrical signal a contracting muscle produces; its magnitude
rises with voluntary tension, but it is not a pure tension meter. The chapter
opens by warning that understanding the electrophysiology and the recording
technology is a prerequisite for interpreting any EMG-biomechanics
relationship.

## Key ideas

- **Electromyogram (EMG):** the signal; **electromyography:** its study.
- Voluntary activity gives an EMG whose magnitude grows with tension.
- Other variables also change the signal at any instant: velocity of
  shortening or lengthening, the rate of tension build-up, fatigue, and
  reflex activity. These are the confounders §10.4 returns to.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 250 (PDF 263): the one-paragraph introduction, followed immediately by
  the start of §10.1.

## Relevance to migera

Low directly: migera does not record EMG. The useful takeaway is conceptual:
the "activation" a muscle receives and the force it produces are different
signals linked by a filter and by state (velocity, fatigue). That split is the
same one migera's ragdoll makes between a commanded strength dial and the
torque that actually reaches a body.
