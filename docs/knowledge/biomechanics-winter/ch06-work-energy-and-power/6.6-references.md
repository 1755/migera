# 6.6 References

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.6, pp. 174–176 ·
> [open PDF at p. 174](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=187) ·
> Up: [Chapter 6 — Mechanical work, energy, and power](./INDEX.md)

The list is dominated by 1970s–80s gait energetics from Winter's Waterloo
group plus the classic work-calculation papers the chapter criticizes. It
has 27 entries; the ones below are the most useful for procedural
animation and physics.

## Most useful cited works

| Reference | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Winter, D. A. "A new definition of mechanical work done in human movement." *J. Appl. Physiol.* 46:79–83, 1979 | Sum-of-segment-energies work (Eq. 6.24) and the critique of the COM method — basis for any whole-body energy metric |
| Robertson, D. G. E. and Winter, D. A. "Mechanical energy generation, absorption and transfer amongst segments during walking." *J. Biomech.* 13:845–854, 1980 | Table 6.2 and the per-segment power balance (Eqs 6.27–6.28) applied through a full walking stride |
| Winter, D. A. "Moments of force and mechanical power in slow jogging." *J. Biomech.* 16:91–97, 1983 | Knee power phases K1–K5 (Fig. 6.17); running joint powers |
| Winter, D. A. and Robertson, D. G. "Joint torque and energy patterns in normal gait." *Biol. Cybern.* 29:137–142, 1978 | Joint power and passive swing-to-trunk transfer in walking |
| Winter, D. A., Quanbury, A. O. and Reimer, G. D. "Analysis of instantaneous energy of normal gait." *J. Biomech.* 9:253–257, 1976 | Segment energies through a stride, including HAT exchange |
| Quanbury, A. O., Winter, D. A. and Reimer, G. D. "Instantaneous power and power flow in body segments during walking." *J. Human Movement Studies* 1:59–67, 1975 | Origin of passive joint-force transfer (6.0.9) |
| Cavagna, G. A. and Margaria, R. "Mechanics of walking." *J. Appl. Physiol.* 21:271–278 (1966; the book misprints 1996) | The COM pendular-exchange method — useful as an inverted-pendulum gait model even though it underestimates work |
| Williams, K. R. and Cavanagh, P. R. "A model for the calculation of mechanical power during distance running." *J. Biomech.* 16:115–128, 1983 | Corrections to segment-energy work for running |
| Winter, D. A. "Energy assessments in pathological gait." *Physiotherapy Canada* 30:183–191, 1978 | The four causes of inefficiency (6.1.1) |
| Falconer, K. and Winter, D. A. "Quantitative assessment of cocontraction at the ankle joint in walking." *Electromyogr. Clin. Neurophysiol.* 25:135–148, 1985 | %COCON (Eqs 6.12–6.13) |

Others cited: Abbot et al. 1952 (negative-work cycling), Bresler and Berry
1951, Cappozzo et al. 1976, Cotes and Meade 1960, Elftman 1939, Fenn 1929,
Gaesser and Brooks 1975, Hill 1960, Liberson 1965, Milner-Brown et al.
1973, Morrison 1970, Norman et al. 1976, Ralston and Lukin 1969, Saunders
et al. 1953, Whipp and Wasserman 1969, Winter 1976, Yack 1986.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 174–175 (PDF 187–188): full reference list; it ends on p. 175 and
  Chapter 7 starts on p. 176.

## Relevance to migera

Low directly. Robertson and Winter (1980) and Winter (1979) are the ones to
fetch if migera builds a real energy audit of its walk; Cavagna and
Margaria (1966) is the root of the inverted-pendulum walking model.
