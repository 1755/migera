# 6.2.1 Energy of a body segment and exchanges of energy within the segment

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.2.1, pp. 157–160 ·
> [open PDF at p. 157](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=170) ·
> Up: [6.2 Forms of energy storage](./INDEX.md)

A segment can trade potential for kinetic energy and back without any
muscle work — a pendulum. In walking the upper body (HAT: head, arms,
trunk) does this: it rises and slows to midstance, then falls forward and
speeds up into double support, so height and forward speed run almost
exactly out of phase. The leg, by contrast, conserves almost nothing. Two
formulas quantify how much a segment conserves.

## Key ideas

- **Examples of exchange:** a diver converts PE to KE; a boomerang trades
  translational KE for PE on the way up and regains it.
- **HAT in gait (Fig. 6.13):** two PE peaks per stride, at midstance of each
  leg, where forward velocity is at its minimum; PE minimum and velocity
  maximum near double support. PE is roughly sinusoidal.
- **Perfect exchange** (frictionless pendulum, Fig. 6.14): PE and KE swing
  in antiphase, total constant.
- **No exchange** (Fig. 6.15): all components in phase (not necessarily the
  same size); total swings as the sum.
- **Signature:** exchange = opposite-signed changes in PE and KE.
- **The leg is nonconservative** (Example 6.4): its energy changes are
  almost all real work, done by muscles or passed through joints.

## Equations and numbers

**Approximate exchange** from peak-to-peak ranges over a period (only valid
with one max and one min per component):

$$E_\text{ex} = \Delta E_p + \Delta E_{kt} + \Delta E_{kr} - \Delta E_s \tag{6.18}$$

No exchange: $\Delta E_p+\Delta E_{kt}+\Delta E_{kr} = \Delta E_s$. 100%
exchange: $\Delta E_s = 0$.

**Example 6.4 (leg, walking stride):** $E_s$ 29.30/13.14 J, $E_p$
15.18/13.02 J, $E_{kt}$ 13.63/0.09 J, $E_{kr}$ 0.95/0 J (max/min). So
$\Delta E_s = 16.16$, $\Delta E_p = 2.16$, $\Delta E_{kt}=13.54$,
$\Delta E_{kr} = 0.95$; sum 16.65 J, exchange only 0.49 J (≈3%). "Highly
nonconservative."

**Exact formulas** for curves with several extrema, over N sample periods:

$$W_s = \sum_{i=1}^{N} |\Delta E_s| \quad[\text{J}] \tag{6.19}$$

Work if the three components could *not* exchange (Norman et al. 1976):

$$W_s' = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(|\Delta E_p| + |\Delta E_{kt}| + |\Delta E_{kr}|\right) \quad[\text{J}] \tag{6.20}$$

$$W_c = W_s' - W_s \tag{6.21}$$

$$C_s = \frac{W_c}{W_s'}\times100\% \tag{6.22}$$

$W_s' = W_s$ (all components in phase) → no conservation; $W_s = 0$ (ideal
pendulum) → 100%. (The book prints "If $W_s' - W_s$" where it means
"$W_s' = W_s$".)

**Fig. 6.13 magnitudes (read off the graph, not stated in the text):** HAT
COM height ≈ 1.24–1.29 m (≈5 cm peak to peak); horizontal velocity ≈
1.25–1.6 m/s after the first sample. Per kilogram that is
$g\,\Delta h \approx 0.49$ J/kg of PE against
$\tfrac12(1.58^2-1.25^2)\approx0.47$ J/kg of KE — nearly equal, i.e. close to
a pendular exchange (this per-kg arithmetic is ours, not the book's).

## Relevance to migera

This is the target for **pelvis bob phasing and forward-speed variation**:

- Pelvis (≈HAT) height should peak at **midstance** of each leg and bottom
  out in **double support**, twice per stride. `GaitParams::vertical_bob`
  and `hip_dip` in `src/character/anim/gait.rs` set the amplitudes; the
  phase is what this figure pins.
- Forward speed should dip at midstance and peak in double support, in
  antiphase with height, with equal-ish PE and KE swings. migera's walk is
  currently tested to hold root speed within ±2% of its mean
  (`a_walking_body_travels_at_a_steady_speed` in
  `src/character/anim/locomotion.rs`); Fig. 6.13 shows real HAT speed
  swinging roughly ±12%. A perfectly constant speed with a bob means PE is
  changing with nothing to trade against — the "no exchange" pattern of
  Fig. 6.15. Whether to add a small antiphase surge is a design choice; this
  is the evidence for it. (Note the rejected ±25% lurch recorded in that test
  came from uneven stance sweep, not from a phase-locked surge.)
- Eq. 6.22 on the pelvis over a stride is a numeric test: human HAT
  conservation is high; a generated gait whose pelvis PE and KE move in
  phase scores low.
