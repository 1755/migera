# 6.2.2 Total energy of a multisegment system

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.2.2, pp. 160–162 ·
> [open PDF at p. 160](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=173) ·
> Up: [6.2 Forms of energy storage](./INDEX.md)

Total body energy is the sum of every segment's energy at each instant.
Its changes are what muscles net-add or net-remove, but the sum is blind to
two things: transfers between segments (they cancel in the sum) and
simultaneous generation at one joint with absorption at another (they net
out). In steady cyclic motion the total returns to where it started each
stride, so positive and negative internal work must be equal.

## Key ideas

- Transfers between segments ([6.0.9](../6.0-energy-and-work-of-muscles/6.0.9-energy-transfer-between-segments.md))
  leave $E_b$ unchanged.
- Cancellation example: two muscle groups generate 30 J while another
  absorbs 20 J → $E_b$ rises by only 10 J. Only joint power analysis
  (§6.3.1.4) exposes the other 40 J. Such events are inefficient but often
  necessary.
- **Muscled pendulum (Fig. 6.16),** a hinged mass with antagonists m1, m2:
  - t1–t2: no muscle activity — normal pendulum exchange, total constant.
  - t2–t3: m2 contracts in the direction of rotation → positive work, both
    KE and PE rise, total rises.
  - t3–t4: both inactive — total constant at the higher level.
  - t4–t5: m1 contracts against the rotation, lengthening → negative work,
    total falls.
- **Conclusions (p. 162):**
  1. Positive muscle work increases total body energy.
  2. Negative muscle work decreases it.
  3. In cyclical activity (e.g. constant-speed level running) the net
     energy change per stride is zero (ignoring small air and shoe
     friction), so internal positive work per stride equals internal
     negative work per stride.

## Equations and numbers

$$E_b = \sum_{i=1}^{B} E_{si} \quad[\text{J}] \tag{6.23}$$

$E_{si}$ total energy (Eq. 6.17) of segment i at that instant; B number of
segments.

## Relevance to migera

Conclusion 3 is a **steady-state test** for a walking ragdoll or a
physically evaluated animation: sampled at the same gait phase on
consecutive strides, $E_b$ must return to the same value; a drift up means
the controller or integrator is pumping energy in, a drift down means the
character is being dragged by kinematic targets rather than self-powered.
Flat regions of $E_b$ with muscle torques off (t1–t2, t3–t4) are also a
clean test that the free ragdoll conserves energy between hits.
