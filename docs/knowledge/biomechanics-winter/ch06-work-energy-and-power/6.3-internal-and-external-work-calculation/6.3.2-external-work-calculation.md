# 6.3.2 External work calculation

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.3.2, p. 167 ·
> [open PDF at p. 167](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=180) ·
> Up: [6.3 Calculation of internal and external work](./INDEX.md)

Joint power (Eq. 6.1/6.6) and muscle power (Eq. 6.25) already contain any
external work, but mixed in with internal work. The only way to pull it out
is to instrument the interface between body and load: force at the contact
and velocity of the contact point.

## Key ideas

- A cyclist needs force transducers on both pedals plus pedal velocity.
- Lifting or lowering a load needs a force transducer between hands and
  load, or imaging of both body and load from which an inverse solution
  yields the reaction force and velocity.
- Integrate over each power phase separately (between sign changes), as for
  muscle work ([6.0.7](../6.0-energy-and-work-of-muscles/6.0.7-mechanical-work-muscles.md)).

## Equations and numbers

$$W_e = \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\mathbf F_r\cdot\mathbf V_c\,dt \tag{6.26}$$

$\mathbf F_r$ reaction force vector at the contact (N); $\mathbf V_c$
velocity of the contact point (m/s); $t_1, t_2$ start and end of each power
phase.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 167 (PDF 180): the whole subsection and Eq. 6.26.

## Relevance to migera

In simulation the interface is known exactly, so Eq. 6.26 is trivial to
evaluate: for a planted foot, $\mathbf F_\text{contact}\cdot\mathbf V_\text{contact}$
should be ~0 (a nonzero value means the foot is sliding under load — a
foot-lock failure detector); for a `RagdollHit`, the impulse times the hit
body's velocity is the energy the hit injected, which the body's energy
change should then match.
