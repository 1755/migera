# 6.1.2 Summary of energy flows

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.1.2, pp. 154–155 ·
> [open PDF at p. 154](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=167) ·
> Up: [6.1 Efficiency](./INDEX.md)

Fig. 6.12 is a pipeline: metabolic energy (inferred from O2 uptake or CO2
expired) → heat losses inside the contracting muscle → mechanical energy at
the tendon → losses to cocontraction, isometric holding and absorption at
other joints → whatever is left changes the body's segment energies and can
leave as external work.

## Key ideas

- **Metabolic side** (heats, per Hill 1960):
  - *Maintenance heat* — resting muscle's cost of staying alive (basal level).
  - *Activation heat* — tied to the rate of tension build-up, with internal
    shortening of the contractile elements.
  - *Stable heat* — cost of maintaining tension.
  - *Labile heat* — seen in isometric contraction, not explained by tension
    or its rate.
  - *Shortening heat* — from actual shortening under load.
- **Mechanical side, at the tendon**, muscle tension feeds one of four
  things: a cocontraction, isometric "work" against gravity, a simultaneous
  absorption by another muscle, or a net change in body energy (up for
  positive work, down for negative). If the body pushes on something, some
  energy then leaves as external work.
- The three mechanical losses are exactly causes 1–3 of
  [6.1.1](./6.1.1-causes-of-inefficient-movement.md); cause 4 (jerkiness)
  shows up as repeated net increases and decreases of body energy.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 154 (PDF 167): metabolic measurement is indirect (O2/CO2) and out of scope.
- p. 155 (PDF 168): Fig. 6.12 flow diagram; the five heats; four mechanical sinks.

## Relevance to migera

Low for the metabolic half — migera has no metabolism. The mechanical half
is a useful checklist for what an "effort" metric on a procedural character
can and cannot see: work-based metrics see only the net-body-energy branch
plus (with joint power) cross-joint absorption; holding and cocontraction
need torque-based measures.
