# 6.4.1 Energy transfer via muscles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.4.1, pp. 167–169 ·
> [open PDF at p. 167](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=180) ·
> Up: [6.4 Power balances at joints and within segments](./INDEX.md)

Besides generating and absorbing, an active muscle can **pass energy from
one segment to the next** when both segments rotate in the same direction.
The moment takes $M\omega_1$ out of one segment and delivers $M\omega_2$ to
the other; only the difference, $M(\omega_1-\omega_2)$, is generated or
absorbed, and the smaller of the two is transfer. Even an isometric
(constant-angle) muscle in a moving limb transfers energy.

## Key ideas

- **Fig. 6.18:** two segments rotating the same way at different rates with
  moment M across the joint. $M\omega_2>0$: energy flows from the muscle
  into segment 2. $M\omega_1<0$: energy flows out of segment 1 into the
  muscle.
  - $\omega_1=\omega_2$ (isometric in a moving limb): pure transfer 1 → 2.
  - $\omega_1>\omega_2$: muscle lengthening → absorption plus transfer.
  - $\omega_1<\omega_2$: muscle shortening → generation plus transfer.
- With both $\omega$ of the same sign, the transfer rate is the lesser of
  the two power magnitudes.
- Without this split, a per-segment power balance cannot close: each
  segment sees $M\omega_\text{segment}$, not the joint power.

## Equations and numbers

$$P_m = M_j(\omega_1-\omega_2) \quad[\text{W}] \tag{6.27}$$

(ω of the two adjacent segments; the book says it modifies "Eq. 6.5", meaning
Eq. 6.1's $\omega_j$.)

**Table 6.2** (Robertson and Winter 1980) — as drawn, with the moment
direction shown in the book's sketches:

| Movement | Contraction | Function | Amount and direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opposite directions, (a) joint angle decreasing | concentric | generation | $M\omega_1$ to seg 1; $M\omega_2$ to seg 2 |
| Opposite directions, (b) joint angle increasing | eccentric | absorption | $M\omega_1$ from seg 1; $M\omega_2$ from seg 2 |
| Same direction, (a) angle decreasing (e.g. $\omega_1>\omega_2$) | concentric | generation + transfer | $M(\omega_1-\omega_2)$ generated to seg 1; $M\omega_2$ transferred 2 → 1 |
| Same direction, (b) angle increasing (e.g. $\omega_2>\omega_1$) | eccentric | absorption + transfer | $M(\omega_2-\omega_1)$ absorbed from seg 2; $M\omega_1$ transferred 2 → 1 |
| Same direction, (c) angle constant ($\omega_1=\omega_2$) | isometric (dynamic) | transfer | $M\omega_2$ transferred 2 → 1 |
| Segment 1 fixed, (a) angle decreasing ($\omega_1=0$) | concentric | generation | $M\omega_2$ to seg 2 |
| Segment 1 fixed, (b) angle increasing ($\omega_1=0$) | eccentric | absorption | $M\omega_2$ from seg 2 |
| Segment 1 fixed, (c) constant ($\omega_1=\omega_2=0$) | isometric (static) | none | zero |

(The book's row header reads "rotating in some direction" for "same
direction", and prints "Mω" for "Mω₁" in the eccentric row.)

## Relevance to migera

A ragdoll joint torque $\boldsymbol\tau$ applied equal-and-opposite to parent
and child is exactly this: the child receives $\boldsymbol\tau\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_c$,
the parent $-\boldsymbol\tau\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_p$, and only
$\boldsymbol\tau\cdot(\boldsymbol\omega_c-\boldsymbol\omega_p)$ is net
work the controller did. Logging the absolute-ω products as "joint work"
would count transfer as effort. Checked 2026-09-28: `apply_joint_torques`
in `src/character/anim/ragdoll_plugin.rs` computes each body's drive from
that body's own rotation and absolute angular velocity and applies it with
`forces.apply_angular_acceleration` to that body only — there is no
equal-and-opposite reaction on the parent. Energetically the drive is
therefore an *external* moment on each body (it can change the whole
body's angular momentum), not a joint moment in Winter's sense, and its
power is $I\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_\text{body}$ (multiply
the acceleration by the body's inertia to get watts). A Winter-style
generation/transfer split only applies if the drive becomes an internal
torque pair.
