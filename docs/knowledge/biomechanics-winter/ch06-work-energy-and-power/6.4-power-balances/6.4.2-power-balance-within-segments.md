# 6.4.2 Power balance within segments

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.4.2, pp. 168–173 ·
> [open PDF at p. 168](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=181) ·
> Up: [6.4 Power balances at joints and within segments](./INDEX.md)

Energy enters or leaves a segment by four routes: passively through the
joint force at each end, and actively through the muscle moment at each
end. Their sum must equal the rate of change of the segment's own energy
(Eq. 6.17). Winter works this for the leg and thigh of the appendix walking
subject and closes both balances to within 1–2 W.

## Key ideas

- **Fig. 6.19a:** state of a segment — reaction forces $F_{xp},F_{yp}$ and
  $F_{xd},F_{yd}$ at the proximal and distal joint centres, those centres'
  velocities $V_{xp},V_{yp}$ and $V_{xd},V_{yd}$, moments $M_p$ and $M_d$,
  segment angular velocity $\omega_s$.
- **Fig. 6.19b:** positive = energy entering the segment. A negative
  product means energy leaving.
- Muscle terms use the **segment's own** $\omega_s$, not the joint's
  relative ω — that is what makes the transfer of
  [6.4.1](./6.4.1-energy-transfer-via-muscles.md) appear.
- The two ends' passive terms (Eq. 6.6, which the book misnumbers "Eq. 6.9")
  and active terms (Eq. 6.27) must all be computed.

## Equations and numbers

$$\frac{dE_s}{dt} = P_{jp} + P_{mp} + P_{jd} + P_{md} \tag{6.28}$$

$$P_{jp} = F_{xp}V_{xp} + F_{yp}V_{yp},\quad P_{mp} = M_p\,\omega_s,\quad
P_{jd} = F_{xd}V_{xd} + F_{yd}V_{yd},\quad P_{md} = M_d\,\omega_s$$

**Example 6.5 — frame 5 of the appendix walk (early swing).** Inputs
(Tables A.2, A.3, A.5, A.6): $V_{xh},V_{yh}$ = 1.36, 0.27; $V_{xk},V_{yk}$
= 2.61, 0.37; $V_{xa},V_{ya}$ = 3.02, 0.07 m/s; $\omega_{lg}$ = 1.24,
$\omega_{th}$ = 3.98 rad/s. Leg: $F_{xk},F_{yk}$ = 15.1, 14.6 N;
$F_{xa},F_{ya}$ = −12.3, 5.5 N; $M_a$ = −1.1, $M_k$ = 5.8 N·m. Thigh:
$F_{xk},F_{yk}$ = −15.1, −14.6 N; $F_{xh},F_{yh}$ = −9.4, 102.8 N;
$M_k$ = −5.8, $M_h$ = 8.5 N·m. Energies: leg 20.0 → 20.5 J, thigh
47.9 → 47.4 J from frame 4 to 6 (Δt = 0.0286 s).

*Leg:* knee passive $15.1(2.61)+14.6(0.37) = 44.81$ W; knee muscle
$5.8(1.24) = 7.19$ W; ankle passive −33.3 W (book); ankle muscle
$-1.1(1.24) = -1.36$ W. Sum 17.34 W vs $\Delta E/\Delta t = 17.5$ W;
residual 0.16 W.

*Thigh:* hip passive $-9.4(1.36)+102.8(0.27) = 14.97$ W; hip muscle
$8.5(3.98) = 33.83$ W; knee passive −44.81 W; knee muscle
$-5.8(3.98) = -23.08$ W. Sum −19.09 W vs −17.5 W; residual 1.59 W.

*Knee muscles (Fig. 6.20):* 23.08 W leave the thigh into the knee
extensors, 7.19 W of it enters the leg → the extensors transfer 7.19 W
thigh → leg and absorb 15.89 W (= $M_k(\omega_{lg}-\omega_{th})$ =
5.8 × (1.24 − 3.98)). Another 44.81 W crosses the knee passively.

**Book typo in the leg sum.** The ankle passive term is printed as
"5.5 × 0.7" although the listed $V_{ya}$ is 0.07. With 0.07 the ankle term
is $-12.3(3.02)+5.5(0.07) = -36.76$ W, not −33.3 W, and Table A.7 frame 5
lists the leg→foot joint transfer as 36.8 W, confirming 0.07. The corrected
leg sum is 13.88 W, a 3.6 W residual instead of 0.16 W. Fig. 6.20 also labels
$\Delta E_{lg}/\Delta t$ as −17.5 where the text has +17.5.

**Cross-check against Table A.7, frame 5:** knee −15.9 W, hip 31.6 W
$= 8.5(3.98-0.28)$ with HAT ω = 0.28 rad/s, thigh→leg joint 44.7 W and
muscle 7.2 W, pelvis→thigh muscle 2.4 W — the same split applied at the hip.

## Relevance to migera

Eq. 6.28 is a ready **per-body energy oracle** for the avian ragdoll: each
step, compute the body's energy change from its state and compare it to the
powers crossing its joints (constraint force · anchor velocity) plus its
drive power. The Example 6.5 numbers make a unit-test fixture with known
inputs and a known answer — use the corrected ankle term, and assert a
tolerance of a few watts, since even Winter's hand-balanced data leave a
residual of 0.16–3.6 W. A large, persistent residual on one body localizes
an energy leak (a mis-framed anchor, a drive applied to the wrong body)
far better than a whole-body check.
