# 6.0.4 Positive work of muscles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.4, pp. 143–144 ·
> [open PDF at p. 143](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=156) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Positive work happens in a **concentric** contraction: the net muscle moment
acts in the same direction as the joint's angular velocity, so their product
(power) is positive and energy is **generated** — transferred from the
muscles into the limb segments.

## Key ideas

- The sign rule is independent of which convention labels flexion positive:
  flexor moment (+) with flexing velocity (+) gives +; extensor moment (−)
  with extending velocity (−) also gives + (Fig. 6.4a, b).
- The time integral of that positive power over the contraction is the net
  work done by the muscle group, i.e. energy it put into the limbs.
- "Muscle" here means the **net** moment at the joint — the resultant of all
  agonists and antagonists — so this is net generation; cocontraction hides
  inside it ([6.3.1](../6.3-internal-and-external-work-calculation/6.3.1-internal-work-calculation.md), §6.3.1.5).

## Equations and numbers

$$P_m = M_j\,\omega_j > 0 \iff \operatorname{sign}(M_j) = \operatorname{sign}(\omega_j)$$

## Where to read in the book

- p. 143 (PDF 156): definition; Fig. 6.4 (a) flexor moment while flexing,
  (b) extensor moment while extending.

## Relevance to migera

For the ragdoll's per-joint PD torques, "generating" means the applied
torque and the joint's relative angular velocity point the same way. In a
natural walk the big generation bursts belong at the ankle at push-off and
the hip at pull-off (see [6.3.1](../6.3-internal-and-external-work-calculation/6.3.1-internal-work-calculation.md)),
which is a checkable target once joint power is logged.
