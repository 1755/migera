# 6.0.8 Mechanical work done on an external load

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.8, pp. 146–148 ·
> [open PDF at p. 146](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=159) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

A force only does work if its point of application moves. Work is force
times displacement along the force; when force varies (the usual case)
compute instantaneous power $P = \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf V$ and integrate it.
Force and velocity opposing each other give negative power — the load is
doing work on the body.

## Equations and numbers

$$dW = F\,ds \tag{6.3}$$
$$W = \int_0^{S_1} F\,ds = F S_1 \ \ (\text{constant }F) \tag{6.4}$$
$$P = \frac{dW}{dt} = F\frac{ds}{dt} = F\cdot V \tag{6.5}$$
$$P = FV\cos\theta = F_xV_x + F_yV_y \tag{6.6}$$
$$P = FV,\qquad W = \int_0^t P\,dt = \int_0^t FV\,dt \ \ (\theta = 0) \tag{6.7}$$

θ is the angle between force and velocity; units W, N, m/s, J.

**Example 6.1** (constant force). 1.0 kg ball, 100 N for 180 ms from rest:
$a = 100\ \text{m/s}^2$, $S_1 = \tfrac12 a t^2 = 1.62\ \text{m}$,
$W = F S_1 = 162\ \text{J}$ (matches the ball's KE at release,
$\tfrac12\cdot1\cdot18^2 = 162$ J, Example 6.3 in
[6.2](../6.2-forms-energy-storage/INDEX.md)).

**Example 6.2** (time-varying force, Fig. 6.7). Force rises to ~100 N and
decays over ~320 ms; velocity (integral of a) reaches ~18 m/s; power
$P=FV$ peaks at roughly 1.1–1.2 kW near 180 ms; cumulative work levels off
near ~145 J (values read off the figure). The book notes the peak is brief
and the average over the throw is under 500 W.

**Catching:** the hand still pushes against the ball but velocity is
reversed, so power and work are negative — the ball does work on the body.

## Relevance to migera

Eq. 6.6 is the formula for the power a contact force delivers: ground
reaction × contact-point velocity for a planted foot (≈ 0 if the foot truly
does not slide — a cheap foot-lock sanity check), and hit impulse × body
velocity for `RagdollHit`. Also a ready unit-test fixture: Example 6.1 has a
closed-form answer (162 J) for a 1 kg body under constant 100 N.
