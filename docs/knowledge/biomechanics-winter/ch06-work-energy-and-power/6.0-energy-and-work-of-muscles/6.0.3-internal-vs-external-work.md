# 6.0.3 Internal versus external work

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.3, pp. 141–143 ·
> [open PDF at p. 141](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=154) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Muscles are the body's only real energy generators and its main energy
absorbers (joint friction and connective-tissue viscosity dissipate only a
small fraction). Work done changing the energy of the body's own segments is
**internal work**; work done on a load is **external work**. Walking and
running on the level have no external load — all generation and absorption
go into moving the segments.

## Key ideas

- **Many sources, many intermediaries.** Energy reaches a load through
  several intervening segments and changes form on the way. Lifting example
  (Fig. 6.2): 200 W into the load (external) plus 400 W raising the body's
  own energy (internal) = 600 W total from many source muscles.
- **Exception:** lifting one's own body weight to a new height counts as
  external work, so running uphill involves both kinds.
- **External work can be negative:** a tackled player, or a catcher's hand
  giving way to a baseball — the external body does work on the person.
- **Coupled bicycles (Abbot et al., 1952; Fig. 6.3):** one rider pedals
  forward (positive work), the other resists backward (negative work). They
  are *not* doing equal work: the positive rider supplies both riders'
  internal work plus the negative rider's absorption. If each rider's
  internal work is 75 W, freewheeling both costs the positive rider 150 W;
  if the negative rider then absorbs 150 W the positive rider is at 300 W
  (Fig. 6.3 draws 300 W, 225 W through the chain, 150 W). Positive work also
  costs far more metabolically than negative work, so the forward rider
  tires quickly while the backward one cycles easily.

## Equations and numbers

$$P_\text{total} = P_\text{internal} + P_\text{external}\quad(\text{e.g. }400 + 200 = 600\ \text{W})$$

## Relevance to migera

A walking character does essentially only internal work, so an energy
metric for gait naturalness must count the segments' own energy changes
(and cancellation between joints, [6.1.1](../6.1-efficiency/6.1.1-causes-of-inefficient-movement.md)),
not work on anything external. External work becomes relevant for
`RagdollHit` impacts (an external body doing negative work on the character)
and any future carry/push interactions.
