# 6.0.1 Mechanical energy and work

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.1, pp. 139–140 ·
> [open PDF at p. 139](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=152) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Energy and work share a unit (J) but are different things: energy is the
*state* of a body at one instant, work is the *flow* of energy from one body
to another and needs elapsed time. "Body A does work on body B" means energy
flows A → B; a muscle does work on a segment when energy flows from the
muscle into the segment.

## Key ideas

- **Chapter motivation (p. 139).** Winter ranks energetics as the single most
  informative biomechanical variable: without energy flows there is no
  movement. Clinically, joint mechanical powers were the most discriminating
  variable in all his pathological-gait assessments — they caught things EMG
  and moment-of-force curves alone missed.
- **Energy = capacity.** A body with 200 J kinetic and 150 J potential energy
  can do 350 J of work on another body.
- **Work = transfer.** Work is only defined between two bodies and over a
  time interval; its rate is power (see
  [6.0.6](./6.0.6-muscle-mechanical-power.md)).
- Consequence: an energy curve (state) and a power curve (flow) answer
  different questions; the chapter keeps them separate and later balances
  one against the other ([6.4.2](../6.4-power-balances/6.4.2-power-balance-within-segments.md)).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 139 (PDF 152): chapter introduction, why powers matter diagnostically;

- p. 140 (PDF 153): "body A does work on body B" convention.

## Relevance to migera

Low on its own, but it fixes vocabulary for any energy-based metric on the
walk or the ragdoll: a per-frame segment energy (state, from
[6.2.1](../6.2-forms-energy-storage/6.2.1-segment-energy-and-within-segment-exchange.md))
is not the same as the work a PD joint did (flow, integral of torque × relative
angular velocity). A diagnostic that mixes them double-counts.
