# 6.0.7 Mechanical work of muscles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.7, pp. 145–146 ·
> [open PDF at p. 145](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=158) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Work is the integral of power over time (1 J = 1 W·s; 100 W for 0.1 s is
10 J of energy moved from muscle to limb). Because muscle power keeps changing
sign, total positive and total negative work must be integrated phase by
phase between the zero crossings — the net integral alone can be zero for a
movement that cost plenty.

## Key ideas

- In the elbow example of Fig. 6.6: t1–t2 positive, t2–t3 negative, t3–t4
  positive, t4–t5 negative. If the forearm returns to its start, net work
  $\int_{t_1}^{t_5}P_m\,dt = 0$.
- Knowing the exact times the power reverses polarity is therefore
  essential to get total positive and total negative work.
- A cost measure that uses net work would score this movement as free.
  Summing absolute phase works (or positive and negative separately) is the
  meaningful measure.

## Equations and numbers

$$W_m = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} P_m\,dt \quad [\text{J}] \tag{6.2}$$

Discrete form with frame interval Δt and phase split:

$$W^+ = \sum_i \max(P_i,0)\,\Delta t,\qquad W^- = \sum_i \min(P_i,0)\,\Delta t$$

(the split form is the book's instruction written as code, not a numbered
equation).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 145 (PDF 158): definition of work from power.
- p. 146 (PDF 159): Eq. 6.2; the zero-net-work argument for Fig. 6.6.

## Relevance to migera

Use $W^+$ and $|W^-|$ per joint per stride, not the net, as a naturalness or
effort score for a generated gait. On a steady-state level walk the whole
body's $W^+$ and $|W^-|$ per stride should be about equal
([6.2.2](../6.2-forms-energy-storage/6.2.2-multisegment-total-energy.md));
a large imbalance over many strides indicates the animation or the physics
is adding or losing energy.
