# 6.0.9 Mechanical energy transfer between segments

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.9, pp. 148–149 ·
> [open PDF at p. 148](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=161) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

When a joint centre moves, the reaction force between the two segments does
work: energy passes from one segment to the other through the joint
(Quanbury et al., 1975). This flow is **passive** — it costs no muscle
activity, and across the whole body all such flows cancel, so they move
energy around without adding or removing any.

## Key ideas

- Fig. 6.8: $F_{j1}$ (force of segment 2 on segment 1) makes angle
  $\theta_1$ with the shared joint velocity $V_j$; $F_{j1}V_j\cos\theta_1>0$
  means energy enters segment 1. The equal-and-opposite $F_{j2}$ gives
  $F_{j2}V_j\cos\theta_2<0$: the same amount leaves segment 2.
- In an n-joint chain there are n such flows and their algebraic sum is
  zero.
- This is **in addition to** the muscle power $M_j\omega_j$ at the same
  joint ([6.0.6](./6.0.6-muscle-mechanical-power.md)).
- **Walking example** (Winter and Robertson, 1978): at the end of swing the
  foot and leg lose much of their energy by transfer **up through the thigh
  to the trunk**, where it is conserved and becomes kinetic energy pushing
  the upper body forward. Table A.7 in the appendix shows it numerically:
  in late swing (frames 21–23) the thigh-to-leg joint transfer is about −65
  to −73 W and pelvis-to-thigh about −72 to −76 W (negative = flowing
  proximally).

## Equations and numbers

$$P_{j1} = \mathbf F_{j1}\cdot\mathbf V_j = F_{j1}V_j\cos\theta_1,\qquad P_{j2} = -P_{j1}$$

Planar form (Eq. 6.6): $P_j = F_xV_x + F_yV_y$ with the force acting on the
segment of interest and the joint-centre velocity.

## Relevance to migera

Two consequences for the stack. (1) In a chained ragdoll the avian joint
constraint forces move energy between bodies exactly like this; a power
audit that ignores them will not balance per body even if the whole body is
fine. (2) For animation, the swing leg decelerating before heel strike is
not "wasted" motion — its energy feeds forward progression of the trunk, one
reason the pelvis/trunk speed peaks around double support (see
[6.2.1](../6.2-forms-energy-storage/6.2.1-segment-energy-and-within-segment-exchange.md)).
