# 6.0.6 Muscle mechanical power

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §6.0.6, pp. 144–145 ·
> [open PDF at p. 144](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=157) ·
> Up: [6.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Muscle power at a joint is the net muscle moment times the joint angular
velocity. It is positive when muscles generate energy and negative when they
absorb it, and it changes sign several times even in the simplest movement,
so it has to be computed as a time series, not as one number.

## Key ideas

- Power is rarely constant; the field computes it frame by frame (Elftman
  1939; Quanbury et al. 1975; Cappozzo et al. 1976; Winter and Robertson 1978).
- $\omega_j$ is the **joint** (relative) angular velocity, i.e. the
  difference of the two adjacent segments' angular velocities. Section 6.4.1
  makes that explicit as $M_j(\omega_1-\omega_2)$ and shows the split into
  generation plus transfer.
- **Fig. 6.6, elbow extend-then-flex:** $M_j$ and $\omega_j$ are roughly
  90° out of phase, giving four bursts:
  1. t1–t2: triceps (extensor moment) extend the forearm → **positive**.
  2. t2–t3: biceps (flexor moment) brake the extension → **negative**.
  3. t3–t4: biceps now accelerate it into flexion → **positive**.
  4. t4–t5: triceps brake the flexion while lengthening → **negative**.
- The accelerate-then-brake pattern — a generation burst followed by an
  absorption burst at the same joint — is the signature of any point-to-point
  movement that starts and ends at rest.

## Equations and numbers

$$P_m = M_j\,\omega_j \quad [\text{W}] \tag{6.1}$$

$M_j$ net muscle moment (N·m); $\omega_j$ joint angular velocity (rad/s).
Sign conventions for $M_j$ and $\omega_j$ must match (both flexion-positive
or both extension-positive); then $P_m>0$ is generation, $P_m<0$ absorption.

## Relevance to migera

Direct recipe for a **per-joint power probe** on the ragdoll or on the
kinematic animation (after inverse dynamics): $P = \boldsymbol\tau\cdot(\boldsymbol\omega_\text{child}-\boldsymbol\omega_\text{parent})$,
the 3D form of Eq. 6.1 (see
[7.4.4](../../ch07-three-dimensional-kinematics-and-kinetics/7.4-kinetic-analysis-reactions-and-moments/7.4.4-joint-mechanical-powers.md)).
Using the child's absolute ω instead of the relative one silently mixes in
transfer ([6.4.1](../6.4-power-balances/6.4.1-energy-transfer-via-muscles.md)).
Plotting it for a walk shows at a glance whether push-off energy comes from
the ankle, as it should, or is smeared across every spring.
