# 8.4 External forces and torques

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.4, pp. 216–217 ·
> [open PDF at p. 216](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=229) ·
> Up: [Chapter 8 — Synthesis of human movement, forward solutions](./INDEX.md)

External forces and joint torque motors enter the Lagrangian formulation
only through the virtual work they do. Total that work $W$ in terms of the
generalized coordinates, and each partial derivative is a generalized
force. An internal actuator, whether a muscle or a joint motor, is a
*pair* of equal and opposite loads on the two segments it connects.

## Key ideas

- **Force list.** A force $j$ at point $a$:
  $frc(j) = [a, F_x, F_y, F_z]$ (Eq. 8.32).
- **Bidirectional forces** (force motors, linear actuators) are two equal
  and opposite forces on two segments. The list is extended with the second
  end point.
- **Torque motor** $i$ acting between frames $j$ and $k$:
  $trq(i) = [j, k, \tau_x, \tau_y, \tau_z]$ (Eq. 8.33). Like any internal
  moment, it acts $+\tau$ on one frame and $-\tau$ on the other.
- **Generalized force** (Eq. 8.34), where $W$ is the total work done by
  the external forces and torques, as in Eq. 8.6:

$$Q_i = \frac{\partial W}{\partial q_i}$$

In the 3-link example (§8.6), with segment angles as coordinates, a joint
torque $T_k$ between segments $a$ and $b$ contributes $T_k(q_b - q_a)$ to
$W$. So it appears as $+T_k$ in segment $b$'s equation and $-T_k$ in
segment $a$'s.

## Relevance to migera

By the book's rule, migera's ragdoll is **not** driven by joint torque
motors. `apply_joint_torques` in `src/character/anim/ragdoll_plugin.rs`
(checked 2026-09-28) computes one PD term per body. The term drives the
body's *world* orientation toward a world-space target and is applied with
`apply_angular_acceleration` on that body only, with no $-\tau$ reaction on
the parent. In Winter's terms each body gets an **external** torque
(`trq(i) = [0, k, …]` against the global frame), not a `trq(i) = [j, k, …]`
pair. This is the "hand of God" style common in game ragdolls. It tracks
the pose well and is robust with a pinned root, but it injects net angular
momentum into the character. So a free-root (unpinned) ragdoll driven this
way can hold poses that no muscle could, for example balancing without
foot forces. If a physically honest free ragdoll is ever wanted, the
torques must become internal pairs, converted through each body's inertia
because the PD output is acceleration-shaped
([use apply_angular_acceleration, not apply_torque](../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-apply-angular-acceleration-not-torque.md)).
