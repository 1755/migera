# 8.3.2 Spring potential energy and dissipative energy

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.3.2, p. 216 ·
> [open PDF at p. 216](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=229) ·
> Up: [8.3 System energy](./INDEX.md)

Springs add quadratic potential energy about their free length (linear
spring) or unloaded angle (torsional spring), and this energy goes into
$L$. Viscous dampers are handled with Rayleigh's dissipation function,
half the damping coefficient times relative velocity squared. Its negative
derivative with respect to each generalized velocity is that coordinate's
damping force.

## Equations and numbers

**Linear spring-damper.** Stiffness $k_l$, damping $c_l$, free length
$l_s$, ends $pt(e)$ and $pt(f)$ (Fig. 8.3). $l$ is the length of
$\mathbf r_{ef}$.

$$\mathrm{PE}_s = \tfrac12 k_l\,(l - l_s)^2 \tag{8.27}$$

```
spl(j) = [e, f, k_l, c_l, l_s]      (8.28; printed as [e, f, k_t, c_1, l_s])
```

**Torsional spring.** Stiffness $k_t$, between two adjacent frames
$\mathrm{LRS}(g)$ and $\mathrm{LRS}(h)$. $\theta$ is the angle between
them about a common axis, and $\theta_s$ is the unloaded angle.

$$\mathrm{PE}_s = \tfrac12 k_t\,(\theta - \theta_s)^2$$

```
spt(j) = [g, h, k_t, c_t, θ_s]      (8.29)
```

**Rayleigh dissipation.** The general form is
$p = c\,v^{n+1}/(n+1)$, with $n = 1$ for viscous friction and $v$ the
relative velocity between the damper's ends. For $d$ dampers:

$$DE = \sum_{j=1}^{d}\tfrac12 c_j\,(v_j)^2 \tag{8.30}$$

$$Q_i = -\frac{\partial\, DE}{\partial \dot q_i} \tag{8.31}$$

Add it to either side of Lagrange's equations: $+\partial DE/\partial\dot q_i$
on the left, or $Q_i$ on the right.

For a torsional spring-damper at a joint, these give the generalized torque
$-k_t(\theta-\theta_s) - c_t\dot\theta$. That is exactly a PD controller
with $\theta_s$ as the target.

## Relevance to migera

- **PD = torsional spring + Rayleigh damper.** migera's ragdoll PD
  (`src/math/pd.rs`) is this pair with a moving
  $\theta_s$. It is parameterised by frequency and damping ratio
  ($k = \omega^2$, $c = 2\zeta\omega$, per unit inertia) and applied as
  angular acceleration. The book's energy view guarantees that the damper
  removes energy *in continuous time only*. At avian's 64 Hz step the
  explicit update is stable only while $c\,dt < 2$
  ([PD damping has an explicit-integration bound](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/pd-damping-explicit-integration-bound.md)).
- **Spring joint limits** (§8.1: nonlinear springs at the knee, ankle and
  MTP, linear at the hip) are a one-sided torsional spring that is active
  only past $\theta_s = $ the limit angle. It is an alternative to avian's
  hard limits if hard-stop chatter shows up.
- **Pose springs.** The animation-side springs (`math/spring.rs`,
  `dho.rs`) are the same damped harmonic oscillator, but integrated with an
  exact exponential solution, which is why they avoid the discrete
  damping bound
  ([the rational exp approximation diverges](../../../character-animation/animation-core/spring-exp-approximation-diverges.md)).
