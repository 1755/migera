# 8.2.5 Lagrange's equations

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.2.5, p. 208 ·
> [open PDF at p. 208](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=221) ·
> Up: [8.2 Mathematical formulation](./INDEX.md)

With $n$ generalized coordinates and $m$ constraint equations, the system is
$n$ second-order nonlinear ODEs plus $m$ algebraic constraints. The
unknowns are the $n$ coordinates $[q]$ and the $m$ multipliers $[\lambda]$.

## Equations and numbers

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q_i}\right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = Q_i,\qquad i = 1,\dots,n \tag{8.8}$$

$$[\Phi] = [0] \tag{8.9}$$

*Transcription note:* the book prints $\partial(\partial L/\partial\dot q_i)/\partial t$.
It has to be the **total** time derivative, which is what produces the
velocity-product (Coriolis/centripetal) terms in the worked examples. Here
$Q_i$ is the sum of the applied-force, constraint (Eq. 8.7), damper
(Eq. 8.31) and external-work (Eq. 8.34) contributions.

## Key ideas

- The procedure is the same whatever the model's size: form $L$, form $Q$,
  differentiate. Only the amount of algebra grows.
- If only independent coordinates are used ($m = 0$), the multipliers
  disappear and so do the joint reaction forces. See §8.5 for the
  alternative.
- The result can be arranged as $M(q)\ddot q + h(q,\dot q) + G(q) = Q$.
  $M$ is the mass matrix and $h$ holds the $\dot q^2$ terms. Integrating
  $\ddot q = M^{-1}(Q - h - G)$ forward in time is the forward solution.

## Relevance to migera

This is the equation avian solves (in maximal coordinates) for the ragdoll
every substep. Writing it out for a small chain gives a **physics oracle**:
integrate the ODE with a small-step RK4 in a test, and compare avian's
trajectory for the same chain and initial state. This separates solver
error from controller error. [8.6](../8.6-illustrative-example.md) gives a
ready 3-link instance.
