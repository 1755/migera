# 8.2.4 Generalized forces [Q]

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.2.4, pp. 207–208 ·
> [open PDF at p. 207](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=220) ·
> Up: [8.2 Mathematical formulation](./INDEX.md)

A generalized force $Q_i$ is the part of the applied forces that does work
when $q_i$ alone changes and every other coordinate is held fixed. Applied
forces are projected through the Jacobian of their application points.
Constraints add $\lambda_j\,\partial\Phi_j/\partial q_i$. The two
contributions are summed before they go into Lagrange's equations.

## Key ideas

- Every nonconservative force $\mathbf F_j$ can be resolved into one
  component per generalized coordinate, and so can every constraint force.
- **Moments** caused by these forces, and externally applied moments, depend
  heavily on the choice of angle system. With Euler angles the mapping is
  not the identity (Eq. 8.22 in
  [8.2.7](./8.2.7-displacement-and-velocity-vectors.md)).

## Equations and numbers

For $f$ applied forces with application points $\mathbf R_j$ (Eq. 8.6):

$$Q_i = \sum_{j=1}^{f}\left(F_{xj}\frac{\partial R_{xj}}{\partial q_i} + F_{yj}\frac{\partial R_{yj}}{\partial q_i} + F_{zj}\frac{\partial R_{zj}}{\partial q_i}\right)$$

*Transcription note:* the book prints a factor $\lambda_j$ in front of the
bracket. That factor is a typo. $\lambda$ belongs only to the constraint
term. Equivalently, $Q_i = \partial W/\partial q_i$ for the virtual work
$W$ (Eq. 8.34).

For $m$ constraint equations $\Phi_j = 0$ (Eq. 8.7):

$$Q_i = \sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_j\,\frac{\partial\Phi_j}{\partial q_i}$$

Add Eqs. 8.6 and 8.7 together before use.

## Where to read in the book
- p. 207 (PDF 220): Eqs. 8.6–8.7.

## Relevance to migera

This is the formal version of something migera does implicitly. Eq. 8.6 is
$Q = J^{\mathsf T}F$. A PD torque about a ball joint maps onto joint
coordinates through the same kind of Jacobian transpose. When joint angles
are Euler angles, that map is a non-trivial matrix (Eq. 8.22), and applying
a torque "per Euler axis" directly would be wrong. migera sidesteps this:
its PD controller works on quaternions and applies world-space angular
acceleration (see
[use apply_angular_acceleration, not apply_torque](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-apply-angular-acceleration-not-torque.md)).
No generalized-force mapping is needed as long as it stays that way.
