# 8.2.7 Displacement and velocity vectors

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.2.7, pp. 210–214 ·
> [open PDF at p. 210](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=223) ·
> Up: [8.2 Mathematical formulation](./INDEX.md)

Contents: [2D](#two-dimensional-systems-8271) · [sliding block](#sliding-block-worked-through-812d812g) · [3D](#three-dimensional-systems-8272) · [transcription errors](#transcription-errors-in-the-printed-book)

A point's global position is its frame origin plus the rotated local
offset. Its velocity adds the rotated local velocity and the
$\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r$ term, written with a skew-symmetric
matrix. The results are cached in `disp`, `velo` and `omga` lists so that
each is derived only once. In 3D the rotation comes from zxz Euler angles.
**The printed 3D matrices contain several typos**; the forms below are
corrected and checked numerically.

## Key ideas

- Notation: lower case ($\mathbf r, \mathbf v, x, y, z$) is local (LRS),
  upper case ($\mathbf R, \mathbf V, X, Y, Z$) is global (GRS). $c$ and $s$
  stand for cosine and sine.
- Cache lists: $\mathrm{disp}(i) = [x_i, y_i, z_i]$ (8.13),
  $\mathrm{velo}(i) = [\dot x_i, \dot y_i, \dot z_i]$ (8.14), and
  $\mathrm{Omga}(j) = [\omega_{jx}, \omega_{jy}, \omega_{jz}]$ (8.15).
  Saving the derived components rather than re-deriving them saves a lot
  of time in complex models.

## Two-dimensional systems (8.2.7.1)

For point $a$ in LRS $i$ (Fig. 8.3), where $\phi$ is the angle from the
GRS $X$ axis to $x_i$:

$$\mathbf R_a = \mathbf R_i + [\phi]\,\mathbf r_{ia},\qquad [\phi] = \begin{bmatrix} c & -s\\ s & c\end{bmatrix} \tag{8.16}$$

$$\mathbf V_a = \mathbf V_i + [\phi]\,\mathbf v_{ia} + [\phi][\tilde\omega]\,\mathbf r_{ia},\qquad [\tilde\omega] = \begin{bmatrix}0 & -\omega_z\\ \omega_z & 0\end{bmatrix} \tag{8.17, 8.18}$$

$\omega_z = d\theta_z/dt$. For a point fixed in a rigid segment
$\mathbf v_{ia} = 0$, which leaves only the rotation term.

### Sliding block, worked through (8.12d–8.12g)

Take $\theta = 45^\circ$ ($c = s = 0.707$, $\omega = 0$). The slope frame
does not rotate.

```
DISP(1) = [q1, 0]                     VELO(1) = [q̇1, 0]              (8.12d)
DISP(2) = [q1 + 0.707(d1 − q2) − 0.707 q3,  0.707(d1 − q2) + 0.707 q3]
VELO(2) = [q̇1 − 0.707(q̇2 + q̇3),  0.707(q̇3 − q̇2)]                  (8.12e)
```

Energy and Lagrangian, corrected (8.12f):

$$\mathrm{KE} = \tfrac12 m_1\dot q_1^2 + \tfrac12 m_2\left\{[\dot q_1 - 0.707(\dot q_2+\dot q_3)]^2 + [0.707(\dot q_3-\dot q_2)]^2\right\}$$
$$\mathrm{PE} = 0.707\,m_2 g\,(d_1 + q_3 - q_2)$$
$$L = \tfrac12(m_1+m_2)\dot q_1^2 + \tfrac12 m_2\left[\dot q_2^2 + \dot q_3^2 - 1.414\,\dot q_1(\dot q_2+\dot q_3)\right] - 0.707\,m_2 g\,(d_1+q_3-q_2)$$

Generalized forces from the constraint (8.12g): $Q_1 = 0$, $Q_2 = 0$,
$Q_3 = \lambda_3$.

Equations of motion (corrected):

$$0 = (m_1+m_2)\ddot q_1 - 0.707m_2\ddot q_2 - 0.707m_2\ddot q_3$$
$$0 = -0.707m_2\ddot q_1 + m_2\ddot q_2 - 0.707m_2 g$$
$$\lambda_3 = -0.707m_2\ddot q_1 + m_2\ddot q_3 + 0.707m_2 g,\qquad 0 = q_3 - d_2$$

These are four equations in $(q_1, q_2, q_3, \lambda_3)$. If the constraint
force is not wanted, drop $q_3$ and use the first two. Solving them for
general $\theta$ (this solution is not in the book) gives the classic wedge
result, which makes a handy test fixture:

$$\ddot q_1 = \frac{m_2 g\,s c}{m_1 + m_2 s^2},\quad \ddot q_2 = \frac{(m_1+m_2)\,g\,s}{m_1+m_2 s^2},\quad \lambda_3 = m_2(g c - s\,\ddot q_1) = \frac{m_1 m_2\,g\,c}{m_1+m_2 s^2}$$

For example, with $m_1 = m_2$ and $45^\circ$: $\ddot q_1 = g/3$,
$\ddot q_2 = 0.943g$, and normal force $\lambda_3 = 0.471\,m_2 g$.

## Three-dimensional systems (8.2.7.2)

For point $j$ in the LRS with origin $pt(i)$ (Fig. 8.4):

$$\mathbf R_j = \mathbf R_i + [\phi]\mathbf r_{ij},\qquad \mathbf V_j = \mathbf V_i + [\phi]\mathbf v_{ij} + [\phi][\tilde\omega]\mathbf r_{ij}$$

**zxz Euler angles.** First rotate $\theta_1$ about $Z$, giving
$(x_1,y_1,z_1)$. Then rotate $\theta_2$ about $x_1$, giving
$(x_2,y_2,z_2)$. Finally rotate $\theta_3$ about $z_2$. The elementary
matrices, as printed and correct, are:

$$[\phi_1] = \begin{bmatrix}c_1 & s_1 & 0\\ -s_1 & c_1 & 0\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\ [\phi_2] = \begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&c_2&s_2\\0&-s_2&c_2\end{bmatrix},\ [\phi_3] = \begin{bmatrix}c_3 & s_3 & 0\\ -s_3 & c_3 & 0\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix}$$

The DCM is $[\phi] = [\phi_3][\phi_2][\phi_1]$. **Corrected** Eq. 8.19:

$$[\phi] = \begin{bmatrix} c_1c_3 - s_1c_2s_3 & s_1c_3 + c_1c_2s_3 & s_2s_3\\ -c_1s_3 - s_1c_2c_3 & -s_1s_3 + c_1c_2c_3 & s_2c_3\\ s_1s_2 & -c_1s_2 & c_2\end{bmatrix}$$

This product maps **global → local** components. It equals
`Rotation.from_euler('ZXZ', [θ1, θ2, θ3]).as_matrix().T`. To take a local
offset $\mathbf r_{ij}$ to global, as $\mathbf R_j = \mathbf R_i + [\phi]\mathbf r_{ij}$
intends (and as the 2D Eq. 8.16 does), use its transpose.

**Euler rates → body-frame angular velocity**, corrected Eq. 8.20. The
Euler rates $\dot\theta_1,\dot\theta_2,\dot\theta_3$, which the book writes
$\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$, point along $Z$, $x_1$ and $z_2$:

$$\begin{bmatrix}\omega_x\\ \omega_y\\ \omega_z\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} s_2s_3 & c_3 & 0\\ s_2c_3 & -s_3 & 0\\ c_2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\dot\theta_1\\ \dot\theta_2\\ \dot\theta_3\end{bmatrix}$$

**Skew matrix**, corrected Eq. 8.21:
$[\tilde\omega] = \begin{bmatrix}0 & -\omega_z & \omega_y\\ \omega_z & 0 & -\omega_x\\ -\omega_y & \omega_x & 0\end{bmatrix}$.

**Body-axis torques → generalized Euler torques**, corrected Eq. 8.22. This
is the transpose of the 8.20 matrix, so that power
$\boldsymbol\tau\cdot\boldsymbol\omega = Q\cdot\dot\theta$:

$$\begin{bmatrix}Q_{\theta1}\\ Q_{\theta2}\\ Q_{\theta3}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} s_2s_3 & s_2c_3 & c_2\\ c_3 & -s_3 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tau_x\\ \tau_y\\ \tau_z\end{bmatrix}$$

The applied torques $\tau$ are always given in the body's $xyz$ axes. Both
maps become singular at $s_2 = 0$ ($\theta_2 = 0$ or $\pi$), which is
gimbal lock of the zxz sequence.

## Relevance to migera

migera uses quaternions throughout (`src/character/anim/math/quat_ext.rs`),
so the zxz machinery is not in the code path. The corrected formulas matter
in two cases. One is porting a biomechanics joint-angle convention or a
reference model that uses Euler angles. The other is writing an
independent oracle for a rotation test. Follow the project rule and check
against `scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation`, as was done here, rather than
re-deriving with the same formula the code uses. The sliding-block closed
form is a cheap constraint-force test for any physics wrapper.
