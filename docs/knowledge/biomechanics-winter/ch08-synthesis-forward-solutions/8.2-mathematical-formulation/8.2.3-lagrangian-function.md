# 8.2.3 The Lagrangian function L

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.2.3, p. 207 ·
> [open PDF at p. 207](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=220) ·
> Up: [8.2 Mathematical formulation](./INDEX.md)

The Lagrangian is total kinetic energy minus total potential energy. Kinetic
energy must be measured against an inertial (global) frame. Potential
energy covers only *conservative* effects: gravity, and elastic energy
stored in springs.

## Key ideas

- **KE of a segment**: the work needed to bring it from rest to velocity
  $\mathbf v$, with $\mathbf v$ measured in the global inertial frame. The
  existence of an inertial frame is a basic postulate of classical
  dynamics.
- **PE**: gravitational PE from the segment's height above a chosen datum,
  usually ground level. Spring PE is the elastic energy stored by
  deformation.
- Nonconservative effects (friction, dampers, muscles, external pushes)
  stay out of $L$. They enter as generalized forces (§8.2.4, §8.3.2, §8.4).

## Equations and numbers

$$L = \mathrm{KE} - \mathrm{PE} \tag{8.5}$$

The segment forms are $\mathrm{KE}$ in Eq. 8.23 and
$\mathrm{PE}_j = M g Z_c$ in Eq. 8.25. Spring terms are
$\tfrac12 k(l-l_s)^2$ (Eq. 8.27). All are covered in §8.3.

## Where to read in the book
- p. 207 (PDF 220): top half of the page.

## Relevance to migera

Useful as a **test invariant**. With PD torques off, damping zero and joints
ideal, a simulated chain should conserve $\mathrm{KE} + \mathrm{PE}$ (not
$L$). With a moving kinematic root it should not: the root does work on
the system, which is one more reason the pinned root is an outside input,
not part of the physics. An energy drift test on a swinging two- or
three-body avian chain would catch integration or inertia bugs that
pose-tracking tests miss. The strength of the numerical damping in avian's
solver is unmeasured, so treat any tolerance as something to measure
first, not assume.
