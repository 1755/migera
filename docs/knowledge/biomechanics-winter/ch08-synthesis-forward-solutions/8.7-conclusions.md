# 8.7 Conclusions

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.7, p. 222 ·
> [open PDF at p. 222](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=235) ·
> Up: [Chapter 8 — Synthesis of human movement, forward solutions](./INDEX.md)

The chapter's stated conclusion is about method only. Describe the model as
groups of linked lists, each linked to the others by its first element.
Once the lists are right, the equations of motion follow from the same
systematic procedure every time. That makes the method a good fit for a
computer, and for hand derivation the only difference between a simple and
a complex model is the amount of work.

## Key ideas

- The conclusion does not repeat the chapter's more important physics
  message, which is in §8.0–8.1 and at the end of §8.6. Forward models are
  extremely sensitive to model errors. Open-loop moment playback drifts to
  a fall within about 500 ms. Continuous correction of the input moments
  is required.
- The "prone to errors" warning applies to the book itself: many of the
  printed equations contain typos (see [8.2.7](./8.2-mathematical-formulation/8.2.7-displacement-and-velocity-vectors.md)
  and [8.6](./8.6-illustrative-example.md)). This is a practical argument
  *for* the chapter's thesis that derivation should be automated.

## Relevance to migera

Low directly. migera leaves equation generation to avian. The meta-lesson
fits the project's testing rules: hand-derived dynamics are error-prone,
so check any oracle derived from this chapter numerically, for example by
finite-differencing $L$ as was done for these notes, before trusting it in
a test.
