# 8.0.1 Assumptions and constraints of forward solution models

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.0.1, p. 201 ·
> [open PDF at p. 201](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=214) ·
> Up: [8.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

A simulation counts as a true forward solution only if six conditions hold.
The one that matters most is condition 2. The model must be completely free
to fall over, jump or collapse, with no prescribed trajectory anywhere.
Ground reactions and joint range limits must come out of the dynamics, not
be imposed on it.

## Key ideas

The six requirements, paraphrased:

1. **Same link-segment assumptions as the inverse solution** (§5.0.1).
   Each segment is a rigid body with a fixed mass at its center of mass, a
   fixed moment of inertia and fixed length, and joints are hinge or ball
   joints.
2. **No kinematic constraints whatsoever.** The motor inputs alone decide
   whether it falls, jumps or collapses.
3. **Full initial conditions:** the position *and* velocity of every
   segment.
4. **Only two kinds of input:** externally applied forces, and internally
   generated muscle forces or moments.
5. **All important DOF and constraints are included.** The hip and shoulder
   need three rotation axes each. Their range of motion is limited by
   passive structures such as ligaments, and these limits are modelled as
   *passive internal forces and moments*, not as geometric stops.
6. **External reaction forces are computed.** With the feet on the ground,
   the GRF equals the sum of all segments' mass × acceleration terms.
   Splitting the reaction between two or more contacts (double support, a
   hand on a rail) is a separate and possibly major problem.

## Relevance to migera

This list gives a precise vocabulary for what migera's ragdoll is.
Measured against it, the ragdoll is deliberately not a pure forward model:

- **Requirement 2 is broken on purpose.** The `Hips` body can be a pinned
  kinematic root that follows the animated hips (`KinematicRoot` and
  `follow_kinematic_roots` in `src/character/anim/ragdoll_plugin.rs`).
  `GravityScale = 1 − strength` removes gravity from fully driven bodies.
  Chapter 8.1 shows that even the best research model had to "violate some
  of the constraints" to stay upright. So these are the expected price of
  a character that stays standing, not bugs. A root that is free to fall
  is a separate feature and needs a balance controller.
- **Requirement 4 is bent.** The PD terms are not internal joint moments.
  Each body gets a world-space angular acceleration toward its own target,
  with no reaction on its parent (`apply_joint_torques`). In Winter's terms
  these are *external* torques, so the ragdoll can hold postures that real
  muscles could not. See [8.4](../8.4-external-forces-torques.md).
- **Requirement 5 differs.** migera uses hard avian swing/twist joint
  limits instead of passive spring moments. A spring limit gives a soft
  stop that stores energy. A hard constraint stops dead and can chatter at
  the limit. See [avian joint limits are not cone and twist](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-joint-limits-are-not-cone-and-twist.md).
- **Requirement 3** matters whenever the ragdoll is switched on mid-motion.
  The bodies need the animated pose *and* its velocities, or the first
  frames carry a velocity discontinuity.
- **Requirement 6**, the GRF split across contacts, is what a physics
  engine's contact solver does implicitly. Chapter 5 is the source for
  checking the sign and size of a measured GRF.
