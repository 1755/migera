# 8.1 Review of forward solution models

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.1, pp. 202–203 ·
> [open PDF at p. 202](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=215) ·
> Up: [Chapter 8 — Synthesis of human movement, forward solutions](./INDEX.md)

Forty years of gait simulations either oversimplified the body, prescribed
parts of the motion, or walked only because their moment patterns were
wrong too. The best effort Winter reports is a 3D 9-segment ADAMS model
driven by measured inverse-dynamics moments (Gilchrist and Winter 1997).
It had spring joint limits, dampers at every joint and a viscoelastic foot.
Even so, small moment errors grew after about **500 ms** until it fell
over or collapsed. Winter calls this drift inherent to *any* forward
solution: it can only be corrected by continuously fine-tuning the input
moments, which breaks the rules of a pure simulation.

## Key ideas

### Who modelled what

| Study | Model | Outcome / flaw |
|---|---|---|
| Townsend & Seireg 1972 | massless rigid lower limbs, 1 DOF (flex/ext) per hip | serious oversimplification |
| Hemami 1980 | 3-segment 3D, rigid legs, no feet | oversimplification |
| Pandy & Berme 1988 | 5-segment planar, no feet, single support only | oversimplification |
| Beckett & Chang 1968; Chao & Rim 1973; Townsend 1981 | fuller models, but trunk/pelvis *prescribed* as sinusoids | breaks the no-kinematic-constraint rule |
| Onyshko & Winter 1980 | 7 segments (2 feet, 2 legs, 2 thighs, HAT), sagittal only, rigid foot | failed internal validity; walked only after the moment patterns were altered |
| Hemami et al. 1982b; Chen et al. 1986 | more segments and DOF | no internal validation attempted |
| Phillips et al. 1983 | swinging limb, driven by hip acceleration + hip moment | simple motion, reasonably successful |
| Hemami et al. 1982a | coronal-plane sway with knees locked, hip/ankle ab/adductor actuators | system stability defined; successful |
| Gilchrist & Winter 1996, 1997 | 3D, 9 segments, ADAMS, measured moments as input | "some success", then drifted within ~500 ms (below) |

- **"Two wrongs can make a right."** An incomplete model produces a
  valid-looking movement only when it is driven by *faulty* motor
  patterns. If a simplified model walks, that alone proves nothing.

### What worked

- **The foot contact model matters most at initial contact.** Earlier
  models put a spring under the foot or shoe. This gave extremely large
  foot accelerations and spikes in the GRF. The fix (Gilchrist and Winter
  1996) was a **viscoelastic foot**: an array of parallel springs *and
  dampers* under a rigid foot segment.
- **Joint range as springs.** *Nonlinear* springs at the knee, ankle and
  metatarsal-phalangeal joints kept those joints inside their anatomical
  range. *Linear* springs acted at the hips.
- **Dampers at all joints** "to ensure a smooth motion".

### What did not

- With measured joint moments as the only input, small moment errors made
  the kinematic errors grow after about 500 ms. The model became unbalanced
  or collapsed.
- The cause is the **double integration**: accelerations from input and
  reaction moments integrate twice into position error that increases with
  time. The only remedy is to keep adjusting the input moments. This is
  closed-loop control, and it means the §8.0.1 constraints are necessarily
  violated.

- p. 202 (PDF 215): early models and the "two wrongs" remark.
- p. 203 (PDF 216): simple successful models, the foot-contact fix, spring

## Relevance to migera

This is the most practical page of the chapter for the active ragdoll
(`src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`, `ragdoll_plugin.rs`):

- **Feedback is not optional.** The 500 ms result explains why migera
  drives bodies with a PD tracker toward the animated pose rather than
  replaying torques. The PD error term is the "continuous fine-tuning of
  the input joint moments". Expect any open-loop torque experiment to
  diverge on a sub-second horizon, and do not read that as a bug.
- **Dampers everywhere have a discrete-time catch.** The book's dampers
  are continuous-time. In migera's explicitly integrated controller, more
  damping past `kd·dt ≈ 2` makes oscillation *worse*
  ([PD damping has an explicit-integration bound](../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/pd-damping-explicit-integration-bound.md)).
  The `CEILING_SCALE` comment in `ragdoll.rs` also records that extra
  joint damping "changed nothing" once the torque ceilings were adequate.
- **Spring limits vs. hard limits.** Gilchrist and Winter used soft
  nonlinear springs for joint range. migera uses avian's hard swing/twist
  limits
  ([avian joint limits are not cone and twist](../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-joint-limits-are-not-cone-and-twist.md)).
  If chatter at a limit becomes a problem, a stiff one-sided torsional
  spring-damper near the stop is the book's alternative. §8.3.2 gives its
  energy form.
- **Foot contact spikes.** If ragdoll feet ever carry the body on the
  ground, a stiff foot contact will show the same heel-strike acceleration
  spikes. Contact damping, or several contact points under a rigid foot,
  is the documented fix.
- **Mass errors matter.** Every segment is coupled to every other (§8.6),
  so the body masses need to be realistic. `default_body_masses` in
  `ragdoll_plugin.rs` already uses Dempster's segment fractions as
  tabulated by Winter (ch. 4), instead of avian's default of capsule volume
  at 1 kg/m³. Keep it that way.
