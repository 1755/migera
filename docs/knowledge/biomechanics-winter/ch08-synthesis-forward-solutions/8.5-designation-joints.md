# 8.5 Designation of joints

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §8.5, p. 217 ·
> [open PDF at p. 217](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=230) ·
> Up: [Chapter 8 — Synthesis of human movement, forward solutions](./INDEX.md)

Joint action and reaction do no net work, so a Lagrangian model has two
ways to handle a joint. If the reaction forces are not needed, the joint is
one **point shared** by both segments. If they are needed, the joint is
**split** into one point on each segment, and a **loop-closure
(constraint) equation** $\Phi$ ties them together. The multipliers
$\lambda$ that enforce $\Phi$ are the joint reaction forces.

## Key ideas

- For **pin** and **ball-and-socket** joints the constraint is
  "the vector between the two points is zero": 2 equations in 2D, 3 in 3D.
- Other joint types are beyond this introductory chapter.
- The choice is a trade-off, stated at the end of §8.6. Shared points give
  the minimum number of equations, but they are "highly nonlinear, tightly
  coupled, lengthy, and prone to errors". Split joints add quasi-DOF and
  constraints, giving *more* equations that are each shorter and easier
  to handle, and that also carry the reaction forces.

## Equations and numbers

For a ball joint between point $p$ on segment $a$ and point $p'$ on segment
$b$:

$$\boldsymbol\Phi = \mathbf R_p(q) - \mathbf R_{p'}(q) = \mathbf 0 \quad(3\text{ equations}),\qquad \text{reaction} = \boldsymbol\lambda\ \text{via Eq. 8.7}$$

## Relevance to migera

avian takes the **split-joint** route. Each ragdoll body is a full 6-DOF
rigid body, and each `SphericalJoint` is a positional constraint that
drives the gap between its two anchors to zero. The anchors are the
book's "two points, each on the appropriate segment LRS". This is why
anchor frames matter so much: an anchor written in the wrong body frame is
a wrong $\Phi$
([ragdoll body and anchor frames](../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/ragdoll-body-and-anchor-frames.md)).
The multipliers are the joint reaction forces. If the joint forces in a
simulation are ever needed, for example to compare against the inverse
dynamics in chapter 5, they come from avian's constraint impulses divided
by $dt$, not from the PD torques.
