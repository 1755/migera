# 3.5.1 Limb-segment angles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.5.1, pp. 75–76 ·
> [open PDF at p. 75](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=88) ·
> Up: [3.5 Calculation of other kinematic variables](./INDEX.md)

A segment's absolute angle in the sagittal plane is the direction of the line
between two markers on its long axis, measured **counterclockwise from the
horizontal (+X)**. Because it is absolute and signed, joint angles, angular
velocities and accelerations all follow consistently from it — which the book
calls essential for the kinetic analyses that follow.

## Key ideas

- The two markers need not be at the segment ends, only on the long-bone axis.
- **Fig. 3.23** (leg viewed from the right, walking toward +X): seven
  markers, four segments, three joints. By Appendix A's marker set:
  1 greater trochanter (hip), 2 lateral femoral epicondyle (knee), 3 head of
  fibula, 4 lateral malleolus (ankle), 5 heel, 6 fifth metatarsal, 7 toe.
  Thigh = 1–2, leg (shank) = 3–4, foot = 5–6, toes = 6–7. Angles:
  thigh $\theta_{21}$, shank $\theta_{43}$, foot $\theta_{65}$, toes $\theta_{76}$.
- **Direction of the vector:** $\theta_{43}$ uses $y_3 - y_4$ over
  $x_3 - x_4$, i.e. the vector from the *distal* marker (4, ankle) to the
  *proximal* one (3). A vertical shank reads 90°; a flat foot, whose vector
  runs from metatarsal (6) back to heel (5), reads about 180°.
- Angles are counterclockwise-positive, so angular velocity and acceleration
  derived from them are counterclockwise-positive too (Fig. 3.23 caption).
- Joint-angle conventions, by contrast, vary widely between researchers and
  must always be stated (§3.5.2).

## Equations and numbers

$$\theta_{43} = \arctan\frac{y_3 - y_4}{x_3 - x_4} \quad (3.13) \qquad
\theta_{ij} = \arctan\frac{y_j - y_i}{x_j - x_i} \quad (3.14)$$

Implementation note (not in the book): a plain arctan of the ratio returns
only −90°…90° and loses the quadrant; use the two-argument form
$\theta_{ij} = \operatorname{atan2}(y_j - y_i,\ x_j - x_i)$ to get the full
circle (needed for the ~180° foot angle). Before differentiating a sequence
of such angles, unwrap the ±180° jumps, or §3.5.3's finite difference will
produce a spurious spike of ≈ 2π/(2Δt).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 75 (PDF 88): statement of the method.
- p. 76 (PDF 89): Fig. 3.23 (markers, segment and joint angles with signs), Eqs. 3.13–3.14.

## Relevance to migera

The planar, signed, fixed-reference angle is exactly the kind of measurement
migera's leg tests lacked when an unsigned `angle_between(thigh, shin)` let a
backward-bending knee pass. An atan2 segment angle in the rig's sagittal
plane (built from the rig's own forward and up vectors, since
`puppet_base.gltf` faces +Z and the synthetic rig −Z) gives a direction-aware
quantity for assertions and gait plots; `RigGeometry::knee_fold_direction`
in `src/character/anim/rig.rs` is a signed measure in the same spirit. It
also gives a direct way to compare a procedural walk's thigh/shank/foot angle
curves with the book's Appendix A data.
