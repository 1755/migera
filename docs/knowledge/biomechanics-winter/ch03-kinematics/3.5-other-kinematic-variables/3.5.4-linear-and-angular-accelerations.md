# 3.5.4 Accelerations — linear and angular

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.5.4, p. 78 ·
> [open PDF at p. 78](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=91) ·
> Up: [3.5 Calculation of other kinematic variables](./INDEX.md)

Acceleration can be the central difference of central velocities (five
samples wide), but the book prefers the **three-point second difference**,
obtained by differencing the two half-step velocities: it uses only
$x_{i-1}, x_i, x_{i+1}$. Angular acceleration uses the same formulas on
angles.

## Key ideas

- Eq. 3.17 differences the velocities of Eq. 3.15 at $i\pm1$, so it needs
  $x_{i-2} \dots x_{i+2}$ (the book's text mistakenly cites Eq. 3.16 here).
- The "slightly better" alternative: half-step velocities $V_{i\pm1/2}$
  (3.18a–b) differenced over one Δt give Eq. 3.18c.
- Both assume smoothed input (§3.4.4); on raw data the result is noise
  (Fig. 3.22d).

## Equations and numbers

$$A_{x_i} = \frac{V_{x_{i+1}} - V_{x_{i-1}}}{2\Delta t}\ \text{m/s}^2 \quad (3.17)$$

$$V_{x_{i+1/2}} = \frac{x_{i+1} - x_i}{\Delta t} \quad (3.18a) \qquad
V_{x_{i-1/2}} = \frac{x_i - x_{i-1}}{\Delta t} \quad (3.18b)$$

$$A_{x_i} = \frac{x_{i+1} - 2x_i + x_{i-1}}{\Delta t^2}\ \text{m/s}^2 \quad (3.18c)$$

**Derived (not in the book; own calculation).** Expanded, Eq. 3.17 is
$(x_{i+2} - 2x_i + x_{i-2})/4\Delta t^2$. Comparison for a sinusoid of
frequency f and white noise σ:

| | Amplitude kept at 6 Hz, Δt = 1/60 s | Noise std |
|---|---|---|
| 3-point (3.18c) | 0.968 | $\sqrt6\,\sigma/\Delta t^2$ |
| 5-point (3.17) | 0.875 | $\sqrt6\,\sigma/(4\Delta t^2)$ |

The three-point form is more faithful to the signal (the book's "slightly
better") but four times noisier; the five-point form smooths implicitly.
With properly filtered input, the three-point form is the right choice.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 78 (PDF 91): Eqs. 3.17–3.18c and the five-vs-three-point remark.

## Relevance to migera

The three-point second difference is the natural oracle-free check for
"smoothness" in migera tests (e.g. that a transition or inertialization has
no acceleration spike, or that a locked foot has zero acceleration), but its
$1/\Delta t^2$ noise gain means f32 jitter of 1e-5 m at 60 Hz already reads
≈ 0.1 m/s²; set thresholds from that, and prefer comparing against an
analytic second derivative where one exists (springs, phase oscillators).
