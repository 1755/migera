# 3.5.3 Velocities — linear and angular

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.5.3, pp. 77–78 ·
> [open PDF at p. 77](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=90) ·
> Up: [3.5 Calculation of other kinematic variables](./INDEX.md)

From already-smoothed data, velocity is a finite difference. The simple
$(x_{i+1}-x_i)/\Delta t$ is the velocity *half a sample later* than $x_i$, which
misaligns it with the position data; the **central difference over 2Δt**
places the estimate at sample i. Angular velocity uses the same formula on
segment angles.

## Key ideas

- Assumes the raw data were low-pass filtered first (§3.4.4).
- Forward difference $\Delta x/\Delta t$ with $\Delta x = x_{i+1}-x_i$ is the
  velocity at $t_i + \Delta t/2$; relating it to positions at $t_i$ causes
  timing errors later (e.g. in power = force × velocity).
- Central difference: the chord from $x_{i-1}$ to $x_{i+1}$ is assumed parallel
  to the tangent at $x_i$ (Fig. 3.24).
- **Book typos:** the text says Eq. 3.15's velocity is "at a point halfway
  between the two samples" — it is at sample i, midway between $i-1$ and
  $i+1$; it introduces Eq. 3.16 as "the angular acceleration" — it is the
  angular velocity; and "Equation (3.14)" in that sentence means 3.15.

## Equations and numbers

$$V_{x_i} = \frac{x_{i+1} - x_{i-1}}{2\Delta t}\ \text{m/s} \quad (3.15) \qquad
\omega_i = \frac{\theta_{i+1} - \theta_{i-1}}{2\Delta t}\ \text{rad/s} \quad (3.16)$$

**Derived (not in the book; own calculation).** Truncation error of 3.15 is
$-\tfrac{\Delta t^2}{6}\dddot x$ (second-order accurate; the forward difference
is first-order). For a sinusoid of frequency f the central difference
returns $\sin(2\pi f\Delta t)/(2\pi f\Delta t)$ of the true amplitude: at
$\Delta t$ = 1/60 s that is 0.998 at 1 Hz and 0.935 at 6 Hz — so it also
acts as a mild low-pass. Noise gain for white noise σ: $\sigma/(\sqrt2\Delta t)$.
At the ends of a record (no $i-1$ or $i+1$) a one-sided difference is
unavoidable and is both less accurate and half a step shifted.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 77 (PDF 90): forward vs central difference, Eq. 3.15.
- p. 78 (PDF 91): Fig. 3.24 (chord vs tangent), Eq. 3.16.

## Relevance to migera

`footlock::annotate_contacts` in `src/character/anim/footlock.rs` already
uses exactly Eq. 3.15 for toe speed (with one-sided differences at the ends,
over Δt), and its comment gives Winter's reason — the estimate should
reflect motion on both sides rather than lag half a frame. Use the same form
for any other velocity estimate from sampled poses (root velocity checks,
angular velocity of a bone from successive rotations — for quaternions,
$\omega_i \approx \text{axis-angle}(q_{i+1} q_{i-1}^{-1})/2\Delta t$ in world
frame). In tests, a central-difference velocity on a 6 Hz feature at 60 Hz
under-reads by ~6.5%: set tolerances accordingly, or compare against an
analytic derivative.
