# 3.5.2 Joint angles

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.5.2, p. 77 ·
> [open PDF at p. 77](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=90) ·
> Up: [3.5 Calculation of other kinematic variables](./INDEX.md)

A joint angle is the difference of two adjacent absolute segment angles, plus
an offset and a polarity chosen so that the anatomical neutral reads zero and
the anatomically named direction (flexion, plantarflexion) reads positive.
Because conventions differ between researchers, each one must be stated.

## Key ideas

- **Knee:** fully extended = 0° flexion; the leg moving posterior relative to
  the thigh = flexion.
  $\theta_k = \theta_{21} - \theta_{43}$. If $\theta_{21} > \theta_{43}$ the
  knee is flexed; if $\theta_{21} < \theta_{43}$, extended (hyperextended).
- **Ankle:** 90° between leg and foot is the boundary between plantarflexion
  and dorsiflexion, hence the +90° offset.
  $\theta_a = \theta_{43} - \theta_{65} + 90°$. Positive = plantarflexed,
  negative = dorsiflexed.
- **Metatarsal-phalangeal** (Fig. 3.23): $\theta_{MT\text{-}PH} = \theta_{65} - \theta_{76}$.
- **Sanity check** with the distal-to-proximal segment vectors of §3.5.1
  (derived): standing straight, $\theta_{21} \approx \theta_{43} \approx 90°$
  → knee 0°; a flat foot $\theta_{65} \approx 180°$ → ankle
  $90 - 180 + 90 = 0°$. Walking toward +X viewed from the right, flexing the
  knee swings the ankle backward, tilting the shank vector forward so
  $\theta_{43} < 90° \le \theta_{21}$ → positive flexion, as defined.

## Equations and numbers

$$\theta_k = \theta_{21} - \theta_{43} \qquad
\theta_a = \theta_{43} - \theta_{65} + 90^\circ \qquad
\theta_{MT\text{-}PH} = \theta_{65} - \theta_{76}$$

## Where to read in the book

- p. 76 (PDF 89): Fig. 3.23 labels (knee +ve for flexion, ankle +ve for plantarflexion).
- p. 77 (PDF 90): the knee and ankle conventions.

## Relevance to migera

This is the planar recipe for a *signed* knee/ankle angle, the measurement
that would have caught the backward-bending knee that 30+ unsigned tests
missed. For migera: compute segment angles in the rig's own sagittal plane
(forward × up from the rig, never a hardcoded axis) and assert
$\theta_k > 0$ for a bent knee and the expected ankle sign through the gait
cycle. Knee angle ranges from the book's walking data (Appendix A, Table A.4)
are a direct reference for gait tuning. The rotation-space stack stores knee
flexion as a local rotation about `stance::KNEE_AXIS`, whose sign depends on
facing; this relative-angle formula is an independent, facing-aware check of
it.
