# 3.4.1 Nature of unprocessed image data

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.4.1, pp. 64–65 ·
> [open PDF at p. 64](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=77) ·
> Up: [3.4 Processing of raw kinematic data](./INDEX.md)

Imaging systems sample: they capture one instant per frame and nothing in
between. The digitized coordinates ("raw data") are the true trajectory plus
random additive noise, and must be smoothed before anything is derived from
them — which requires thinking in frequencies (Chapter 2).

## Key ideas

- **Sampling.** Film and TV record a short instant, then nothing until the
  next frame or field. Slow playback shows the jumps; at normal speed (24
  frames/s film, 60 fields/s TV) the eye's ~1/15 s persistence smooths them.
- **Raw data** = digitized coordinates with additive noise from:
  electronic noise in optoelectric devices, spatial precision of the TV scan
  or film digitizer, and human digitizing error. All produce **random**
  errors.
- **Consequence:** raw data must be smoothed, and choosing how to smooth
  needs harmonic (frequency) analysis.

## Where to read in the book

- pp. 64–65 (PDF 77–78): the whole subsection.

## Relevance to migera

The same model applies to any sampled pose stream migera differentiates:
per-frame bone positions read from `GlobalTransform`, toe positions fed to
`footlock::annotate_contacts`, or physics-body states. migera's synthesized
signals have little random noise, but they do carry step-like artefacts
(contact switches, IK clamps, spring max-speed clamps, float rounding) that
behave like broadband noise when differentiated.
