# 3.4.5 Comparison of some smoothing techniques

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.4.5, pp. 74–75 ·
> [open PDF at p. 74](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=87) ·
> Up: [3.4 Processing of raw kinematic data](./INDEX.md)

The same noisy film data were turned into angular acceleration three ways and
checked against an independent accelerometer. **Digital filtering then finite
differences** matched the accelerometer (and was less noisy than it); a
**9th-order polynomial** fit missed badly; **raw finite differences** were
"too noisy to mean anything".

## Key ideas

- **Experiment (Pezzack et al., 1977):** a lever arm swung by hand about a
  vertical axis, from rest through ~130° and back. Three independent records:
  a goniometer on the axis (angle), an accelerometer at the arm's end
  (tangential acceleration ÷ radius = angular acceleration), and cine film
  (angle from digitized coordinates).
- **Fig. 3.22a** — angle: goniometer and film agree closely; the goniometer
  is the noisier of the two.
- **Fig. 3.22b** — filtered (Winter et al., 1974) film data, second finite
  difference: matches the accelerometer "extremely well", with less noise than
  the direct measurement.
- **Fig. 3.22c** — 9th-order polynomial fit on the raw angle, differentiated
  analytically: considerable discrepancy. A much higher order would be needed
  for even a crude fit.
- **Fig. 3.22d** — second-order finite differences of raw coordinates:
  noise swamps the signal.

## Equations and numbers

Polynomial fit to the angle (θ in rad, t in s):

$$\theta(t) = 0.064 + 2.0t - 35t^2 + 210t^3 - 430t^4 + 400t^5 - 170t^6 + 25t^7 + 2.2t^8 - 0.41t^9 \quad (3.11)$$

Its second derivative:

$$\alpha(t) = -70 + 1260t - 5160t^2 + 8000t^3 - 5100t^4 + 1050t^5 + 123t^6 - 29.5t^7 \ \text{rad/s}^2 \quad (3.12)$$

(Checked: differentiating 3.11 twice gives these coefficients; the book
rounds 123.2 → 123 and 29.52 → 29.5.) Plot range in Fig. 3.22: α about
±80 rad/s² over t = 1.2–3.0 s.

- p. 74 (PDF 87): experiment, Eqs. 3.11–3.12.
- p. 75 (PDF 88): Fig. 3.22 a–d.

## Relevance to migera

Two uses. First, the method choice: to estimate accelerations or angular
velocities from a recorded pose sequence (e.g. to set ragdoll PD
feed-forward targets or check a walk's smoothness), filter with a zero-lag
low-pass and then difference; don't fit a global polynomial and don't
difference raw samples. Second, the validation *pattern*: an independent
measurement (accelerometer) as the oracle for a derived quantity. In migera
the equivalent oracle is an analytic derivative (e.g. of a phase oscillator
or spring's closed form) or a physics body's own reported velocity, never
the same finite-difference code on both sides of the comparison.
