# 3.3.2 f-stop setting and field of focus

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.3.2, pp. 54–55 ·
> [open PDF at p. 54](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=67) ·
> Up: [3.3 Imaging measurement techniques](./INDEX.md)

The f-stop sets how much light enters the lens; opening it to save lighting
narrows the range of distances that stay in focus, so the aperture is a
compromise between light and field of focus.

## Key ideas

- f-stop = fraction of lens aperture; a larger opening is a lower number.
  Typical series: 22, 16, 11, 8, 5.6, 4, 2.8, 2. Each step admits twice the
  light of the next higher number (f/2.8 gives twice f/4; f/11 gives four
  times f/22).
- **Field of focus**: the range of object distances that image in focus. The
  lower the f-number, the narrower it is.
- Example: a movement spanning 10–30 ft needs f/5.6 or higher, with the lens
  focused at about 15 ft.
- The final choice also depends on shutter speed and film speed (§3.3.3).
