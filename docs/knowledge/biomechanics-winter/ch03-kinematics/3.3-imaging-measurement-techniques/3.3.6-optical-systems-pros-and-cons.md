# 3.3.6 Advantages and disadvantages of optical systems

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.3.6, pp. 63–64 ·
> [open PDF at p. 63](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=76) ·
> Up: [3.3 Imaging measurement techniques](./INDEX.md)

The decisive advantage of imaging is that it records in an **absolute
spatial reference system**, which body-mounted sensors cannot; the costs are
money, processing time and, for active markers, wires.

## Key ideas

**Advantages**
1. All data are in an absolute spatial frame (in the plane normal to the
   camera's optical axis).
2. Most cine/TV systems allow any number of markers.
3. Light reflective markers barely encumber the subject and are quick to apply.
4. TV cameras and VCRs are fairly cheap.
5. Cine and TV can be replayed for teaching and qualitative whole-body review.

**Disadvantages**
1. Multi-camera systems (cine, TV, optoelectric) and their digitizing
   hardware are expensive.
2. Film has development turnaround and laborious digitizing (though its
   digitizing errors are smaller than many commercial systems').
3. Wired light sources (IREDs) take time to fit, encumber movement, and are
   limited in number.
4. Some systems (IREDs) cannot be used outdoors in daylight.
