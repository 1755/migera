# 3.3.4 Television

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.3.4, pp. 58–61 ·
> [open PDF at p. 58](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=71) ·
> Up: [3.3 Imaging measurement techniques](./INDEX.md)

Video has a fixed field rate (60 Hz North America, 50 Hz Europe) — enough for
most movements, too low for fast athletics — and offers instant replay and
automatic digitizing. Its artefacts (motion blur, and skew from scanning the
image top to bottom) are removed by strobes, shuttered CCD cameras and pulsed
infrared lighting; sub-pixel marker centroids raise precision to about 1 mm.

## Key ideas

- **Advantages over film:** instant replay for quality control and
  qualitative assessment; a frame grabber digitizes directly.
- **Imaging artefacts (§3.3.4.1):** a fast reflective marker blurs into a
  trailing streak, so the black/white threshold must be set carefully; a
  continuously exposed tube also scans top-to-bottom in ~15 ms, so a head
  marker can be sampled ~10 ms before a foot marker (**skew**). A strobe
  (≤1 ms exposure), shuttered CCD cameras, and infrared "donut" lights pulsed
  at 120 Hz for <1 ms freeze all markers at one instant. Clinical labs mount
  6–12 such cameras (Figs. 3.12–3.13).
- **History (§3.3.4.2):** first systems late 1960s (Delft; Winter et al.
  1968); first operational paper CINTEL (Dinn et al. 1970). Averaging ~10
  pixels per ping-pong-ball marker improved precision from 1 cm (scan-line
  spacing) to ~1 mm. VICON fitted a circle to detected marker edges
  (software AMASS); Motion Analysis used patented edge detection; ELITE
  (Milan) used grey-level cross-correlation shape recognition, reaching 1/2800
  of the field of view (~0.9 mm over a 2.5 m field).
- **Conversion (§3.3.4.3):** each commercial system has its own marker
  detection, centroiding and multi-camera labelling.

## Relevance to migera

Low: capture hardware. The **skew** idea has a loose analogue: values sampled
at slightly different times within one frame (e.g. a system reading a
transform before vs. after propagation) are not simultaneous, which matters
when differencing them.
