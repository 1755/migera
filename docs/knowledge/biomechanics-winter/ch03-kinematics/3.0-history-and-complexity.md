# 3.0 Historical development and complexity of problem

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.0, pp. 45–46 ·
> [open PDF at p. 45](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=58) ·
> Up: [Chapter 3 — Kinematics](./INDEX.md)

Kinematics describes *how* a body moves (displacements, velocities,
accelerations, linear and angular) and ignores the forces that cause it. Even
a simple movement produces a large number of variables, so any analysis picks
only the few its question needs.

## Key ideas

- **History.** Marey (France, 1885) used a photographic "gun" and
  chronophotography to get stick figures of a runner; Muybridge (USA) fired 24
  cameras in sequence to record a running man. Before that, movement records
  were artists' impressions.
- **Kinematics vs. kinetics.** Kinematics is the movement itself; kinetics
  (Chapter 5) is the forces and moments behind it.
- **Size of the problem.** Describing one lower limb in the sagittal plane
  over one stride can take up to **50 variables** (linear and angular
  displacements, velocities, accelerations).
- **Pick what the question needs.** A running broad jump may need only the
  body centre-of-mass velocity and height at take-off; a mechanical-power
  analysis of an amputee's gait needs nearly every kinematic variable.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 45 (PDF 58): history (Marey, Muybridge) and the definition of kinematics.
- p. 46 (PDF 59): the "use only what the question needs" examples.

## Relevance to migera

Low on its own; it frames the chapter. The practical point for migera's tests
and debug tooling is the same as the book's: a test should assert the one or
two kinematic quantities its claim rests on (e.g. foot world velocity during a
lock, knee fold sign), not dump every channel.
