# 3.2.1 Goniometers

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.2.1, pp. 48–50 ·
> [open PDF at p. 48](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=61) ·
> Up: [3.2 Direct measurement techniques](./INDEX.md)

A goniometer is an electrical potentiometer whose two arms are strapped to
adjacent segments with its axis on the joint axis; its wiper voltage is
proportional to the **relative** joint angle. It is cheap and gives an
immediate signal, but it cannot tell where the limb is in space.

## Key ideas

- A constant voltage E is applied across the potentiometer; the wiper picks
  off a fraction that depends on the joint angle θ. A linear-taper
  potentiometer is needed for the voltage to be proportional to θ.
- **Advantages:** inexpensive; signal available immediately for recording or
  A/D conversion; planar rotation is recorded independently of the plane in
  which the joint moves.
- **Disadvantages:** gives relative, not absolute, angles (severely limiting
  its assessment value); fitting and aligning takes long and drifts as fat and
  muscle move under the straps; many goniometers encumber movement; joints
  that are not hinges need more complex devices.

## Equations and numbers

$$v = kE\theta = k_1\theta \quad \text{(volts)}$$

## Relevance to migera

Low: measurement hardware. The one transferable idea is the distinction it
embodies: a joint (relative) angle carries no information about the limb's
orientation in the world, so a test on local joint rotations alone cannot
catch a world-frame error (see the rig-and-retargeting notes).
