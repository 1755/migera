# 3.2.2 Special joint angle measuring systems

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.2.2, p. 50 ·
> [open PDF at p. 50](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=63) ·
> Up: [3.2 Direct measurement techniques](./INDEX.md)

An ergonomics glove measures finger and thumb joint flexion with fiber-optic
loops: bending an etched fiber lets light escape, so received intensity falls
as the joint flexes, and each sensor is calibrated against bend angle.

## Key ideas

- Lightweight elastic glove; sensors on the proximal two joints of each finger
  and the thumb, plus a thumb-abduction sensor.
- Each sensor is a loop of fiber-optic cable with a constant infrared source,
  etched over the joint; flexion bends the fiber and more light escapes.
- Flexion is read as a drop in detected light, precalibrated per cable against
  bending angle (Fig. 3.4, p. 51).
- Main use: studying repetitive strain injuries (Moore et al., 1991).
