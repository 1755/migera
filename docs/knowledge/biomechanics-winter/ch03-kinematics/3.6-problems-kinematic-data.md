# 3.6 Problems based on kinematic data

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §3.6, pp. 79–80 ·
> [open PDF at p. 79](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=92) ·
> Up: [Chapter 3 — Kinematics](./INDEX.md)

Six problems exercise the chapter on the book's own walking trial
(Appendix A): seeing what filtering does, reading gait events off
trajectories, and computing velocities, accelerations, segment and joint
angles and segment COM velocity by hand — each checkable against the
tabulated values. They are ready-made known-input → known-answer fixtures.

## Key ideas

Tables used: **A.1** raw coordinates (cm), **A.2** filtered marker kinematics
(coordinates plus velocities/accelerations), **A.3** linear and angular
segment kinematics (foot, leg, thigh, ½ HAT), **A.4** relative joint angular
kinematics (ankle, knee, hip). Gait events: TOR = toe-off right, HCR/HRC =
heel contact right; one stride = 69 frames.

1. **Raw vs filtered:** plot the hip (greater trochanter) vertical coordinate,
   frames 1–30, from A.1 and A.2; describe the smoothing.
2. **Heel trajectory** (A.2, TOR frame 1 to next TOR frame 70): (a) heel-off
   instant in midstance (mind shoe compression); (b) maximum heel height in
   swing and when; (c) vertical heel path in late swing (frames 14–27),
   especially the 4 frames before heel contact; (d) vertical and (e)
   horizontal heel velocity at heel contact; (f) stride length from the heel's
   x during foot-flat periods (frames 35–40 and 102–106); (g) forward
   velocity given a 69-frame stride.
3. **Trunk (rib) marker** over one stride (frames 28–97): is the shape as
   expected; is there potential↔kinetic energy exchange?
4. **Toe clearance:** lowest toe height in late stance vs in swing.
   **Answer:** $y_{toe}$(frame 13) = 0.0485 m, $y_{toe}$(frame 66) =
   0.0333 m, clearance = 0.0152 m = **1.52 cm**.
5. From A.2, check against A.2–A.4: (a) knee X velocity, frame 10 (Eq. 3.15);
   (b) knee X acceleration, frame 10 (Eq. 3.17/3.18c); (c) thigh and leg
   angles, frame 30 (Eq. 3.14); (d) knee angle, frame 30 (§3.5.2); (e) leg
   angular velocity, frame 30, from A.3 angles (Eq. 3.16); (f) toe vertical
   acceleration at frames 25 and 33 from tabulated velocities.
6. From A.2, check against A.3: (a) foot COM, frame 80; (b) leg COM velocity,
   frame 70, in coordinate and polar form (needs segment COM fractions from
   Chapter 4).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 79 (PDF 92): problems 1–5(a).
- p. 80 (PDF 93): problems 5(b)–6.

## Relevance to migera

A source of test fixtures with real numbers: implement the segment-angle,
joint-angle and finite-difference helpers, feed them Table A.2 coordinates,
and assert they reproduce Tables A.3–A.4 — an independent oracle rather than
the same code on both sides. Problem 4's 1.52 cm toe clearance and
problem 2's stride length and heel trajectory are concrete targets for
judging a procedural walk's swing foot.
