# 9.1.2 Influence of parallel connective tissue

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.1.2, pp. 232–233 ·
> [open PDF at p. 232](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=245) ·
> Up: [9.1 Force-length characteristics of muscles](./INDEX.md)

Connective tissue around the contractile element acts like a **one-sided,
stiffening rubber band** in parallel with it: slack at or below resting length,
then pulling back more and more steeply as the muscle is stretched — whether or
not the muscle is active. Tendon force is the active and passive forces added.

## Key ideas

- **Parallel elastic component (PEC):** zero tension at l ≤ l0; beyond l0
  tension builds "slowly at first and then more rapidly" — strongly nonlinear,
  unlike an ordinary linear spring.
- **Sum:** $F_t = F_c + F_p$ (Fig. 9.8, with a sketch of CE ∥ PEC). At maximum
  activation the total shows the active hump, a shallow dip past l0 where
  active force falls faster than passive rises, then a steep passive rise.
- **Activation only scales the active part** (Fig. 9.9: 25/50/75/100%
  curves). All curves converge onto the same passive curve at long lengths —
  the passive force is not under voluntary control.
- **Demonstration:** the hamstrings (two-joint) lose force drastically when
  shortened by hip extension plus knee flexion (active insufficiency); the
  converse — extending the hip with the knee fully extended — stretches them
  toward the other end of the curve.

## Equations and numbers

$$F_t(l, a) = a\,F_c(l) + F_p(l), \qquad F_p(l) = 0 \text{ for } l \le l_0$$

with $a$ the activation fraction (this is the composition Fig. 9.9 shows; the
book gives no formula for $F_p$). Nonlinear spring forms offered later in
§9.3 (Fig. 9.16) are $F = kx^a$ and $F = k(e^x - 1)$.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 232 (PDF 245): PEC description.
- p. 233 (PDF 246): Fig. 9.8 ($F_c$, $F_p$, $F_t$), Fig. 9.9 activation family;
  the hamstring experiment.

## Relevance to migera

The PEC is the biological version of a **soft joint limit**: a passive
restoring force that is zero in the normal range and stiffens steeply near the
end of range, independent of how "strong" the joint currently is. migera
enforces range with avian's hard `SphericalJoint` limits (see
[avian joint limits are not cone and twist](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-joint-limits-are-not-cone-and-twist.md)),
and a limp joint (`RagdollStrength` 0) has **no** passive stiffness at all.
Two consequences:

- A limp ragdoll in migera flops until it hits a hard stop; a real body
  stiffens progressively. A passive, strength-independent torque that rises
  exponentially near each limit would soften the stops and reduce the chatter
  at limits measured in the anim studio.
- Because $F_p$ is independent of activation, it must not be scaled by
  `RagdollStrength` — it belongs outside the strength dial.

Not modeled today.
