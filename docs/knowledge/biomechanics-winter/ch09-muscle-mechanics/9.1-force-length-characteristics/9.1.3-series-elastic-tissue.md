# 9.1.3 Series elastic tissue

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.1.3, pp. 233–235 ·
> [open PDF at p. 233](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=246) ·
> Up: [9.1 Force-length characteristics of muscles](./INDEX.md)

All tissue in series with the contractile element, the tendon included, is the
**series elastic element (SE)**. It stretches as tension rises, so even an
isometric muscle's contractile element shortens internally. The effect is small
in ordinary movement but shapes the time course of tension in dynamic ones.

## Key ideas

- **Internal shortening** (Fig. 9.10): with external length L held constant,
  rising CE tension stretches the SE by exactly as much as the CE shortens.
  From rest to maximum tension this is **a few percent of resting length** in
  most muscles (van Ingen Schenau, 1984), **up to 7%** in others (Bahler, 1967).
- **Dynamics:** together with viscous components, the SE influences how fast
  tendon tension follows CE force — it is part of why the twitch is smooth
  ([9.3.1](../9.3-muscle-modeling/9.3.1-emg-driven-model-example.md) models it
  as the spring K).
- **Energy storage is contested.** It is widely inferred that SE stores large
  energy during a prestretch before explosive shortening (jumping). **van Ingen
  Schenau (1984) showed the SE's elastic capacity is far too small** to explain
  the prestretch benefit, and argued for a cross-bridge mechanism instead. The
  book reports this without settling it.
- **Quick-release experiment** (Fig. 9.11, isolated muscle only): stimulate
  isometrically to a tension, suddenly release one end so load drops to zero,
  record the almost instantaneous shortening x (≈ **2 ms**, too fast for the CE
  to change length), repeat at other tensions → force–shortening curve of the
  SE. Precautions: start at or below resting length so the parallel element is
  slack, and the recorder must have negligible mass and viscosity (or be
  corrected for).

## Relevance to migera

Series elasticity is **compliance between the actuator and the load**: the
motor drives a spring, the spring drives the bone. migera's PD torque in
`src/character/anim/math/pd.rs` acts directly on the body — no series spring.
In robotics this is a "series elastic actuator"; for a ragdoll it would mean
the controller's output is filtered by a spring before it reaches the body,
which softens impacts and makes torque rise smoothly even when the command
jumps. Given the book's own point that SE matters little in ordinary movement
and that its energy-storage role is doubtful, this is **low priority** for
migera: the activation filter of [9.0.5](../9.0-motor-units-and-twitches/9.0.5-muscle-twitch.md)
gives most of the smoothing for less complexity. Not modeled today.
