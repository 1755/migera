# 9.3.1 Example of a model—EMG driven

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.3.1, pp. 244–247 ·
> [open PDF at p. 244](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=257) ·
> Up: [9.3 Muscle modeling](./INDEX.md)

Tendon force during isometric contraction can be predicted from surface EMG
with a three-element model: the full-wave rectified EMG (through a fitted
nonlinearity) is treated as an impulsive contractile force that drives a
**critically damped mass–damper–series-spring** system. The twitch time T
alone fixes the dynamics; one gain maps microvolts to newtons.

## Key ideas

- **Input choice:** the motoneuron pool output can't be recorded, so EMG is the
  best proxy; **surface** EMG is more reliable than indwelling (Komi &
  Buskirk, 1970) and samples many more motor units.
- **Why impulsive:** some models assume the internal "active state" force is a
  first-order exponential (Hatze, 1978; Gottlieb & Agarwal, 1971). Winter
  argues for impulses instead: Hill (1953) found heat production in frog muscle
  at 0 °C peaks 30 ms after stimulation; reaction rates rise 2–3× per 10 °C,
  so at 37 °C rates are 15–40× faster and the chemical (hence mechanical)
  energy release is over in **< 2 ms** — an impulse. Since bigger motor units
  also give bigger action potentials, |EMG| is a train of impulses scaled like
  the contractile force.
- **Elements** (Fig. 9.18): contractile force $F_{ce}$; mass **M** (effective
  muscle mass accelerated as the force wave travels from the end-plate region
  to both tendons); linear damper **B** (velocity-dependent effects in CE and
  connective tissue); linear spring **K** (series elastic element: tendon,
  fascia, cross-bridges).
- **Isometric kinematics:** total length constant; the CE part $x_2$ shortens,
  lengthening the series element $x_1$ by the same amount.
- **Validation** (Fig. 9.20): biceps EMG vs. elbow-flexor force from a wrist
  cuff, for ramp increases and rapid short bursts — predicted $F_t$ (noisier
  trace) overlays the measured force (smoother) closely. Residual error is
  put down to measuring at the wrist (extra damping from cuff, wrist, synovial
  fluid and soft tissue) and to assuming the other agonists (brachioradialis,
  brachialis) share the biceps' activation while the antagonist (triceps)
  stays silent.

## Equations and numbers

Equations of motion (Eq. 9.5a, 9.5b, checked on the rendered page):

$$x_1 + x_2 = L_0 = \text{constant}$$
$$F_{ce} = M\ddot{x}_1 + B\dot{x}_1 + Kx_1 \qquad (9.5a)$$
$$F_t = Kx_1 \qquad (9.5b)$$

Tendon force appears only while the series spring is stretched beyond its
resting length.

Parameters from the twitch time T, assuming critical damping (twitch ≈
critically damped impulse response, Milner-Brown et al., 1973a):

$$T = \frac{2M}{B} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{B}{M} = \frac{2}{T}, \qquad B = 2\sqrt{MK} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{K}{B} = \frac{B}{4M} = \frac{1}{2T}$$

Only the ratios B/M and K/B enter the solution (Fig. 9.19 block diagram:
summer → B/M integrator → K/B integrator, feeding back $B\dot{x}_1$ and $Kx_1$),
plus a fitted **gain A** from EMG (µV) to $F_{ce}$ (N).

Dividing 9.5a by B gives the form actually integrated:

$$\frac{F_{ce}}{B} = \frac{M}{B}\ddot{x}_1 + \dot{x}_1 + \frac{K}{B}x_1 = \frac{T}{2}\ddot{x}_1 + \dot{x}_1 + \frac{1}{2T}x_1$$

whose impulse response is proportional to $t\,e^{-t/T}$ — Eq. 9.1's twitch.

## Relevance to migera

This is a ready recipe for **activation dynamics** on the active ragdoll,
which migera does not have: today `RagdollStrength` in
`src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs` scales the torque ceiling instantly.

- Replace "EMG" with the **commanded strength** (or commanded torque) and
  pass it through this critically damped filter with T ≈ 50–100 ms (§9.0.5
  values). The output is the effective strength. With migera's critical
  spring in `src/math/spring.rs`, the same response is a
  spring with halflife = T·ln 2 (its decay rate is ln 2/halflife).
- A step command then rises as $1 - (1 + t/T)e^{-t/T}$, ~90% at 3.9 T: T ≈ 50
  ms reproduces the ~200 ms turn-on of
  [9.0.6](../9.0-motor-units-and-twitches/9.0.6-shape-graded-contractions.md).
- The filter is linear, so it is testable against closed forms: impulse →
  peak at T with value $F_0/e$; step → the expression above. Integrate with
  exact exponentials, not the rational approximation (see Related).
- The **series spring K** here is the same series elastic element as §9.1.3; a
  ragdoll version would be compliance between PD output and body, which this
  note's own validation suggests is not needed to get realistic force *timing*.
