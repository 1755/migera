# 9.2.3 Combination of length and velocity versus force

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.2.3, pp. 239–240 ·
> [open PDF at p. 239](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=252) ·
> Up: [9.2 Force-velocity characteristics](./INDEX.md)

Tendon force depends on length **and** velocity at once, so the full
characteristic is a **3-D surface** (Fig. 9.13), not a curve. The force-length
curve is the slice at zero velocity; each force-velocity curve is a slice at
one length. Each activation level needs its own surface.

## Key ideas

- Fig. 9.13 plots contractile-element tension over (length, velocity) at
  **maximum activation**, lengthening and shortening, with the **parallel
  elastic element excluded**.
- Everyday contractions are submaximal, so surfaces at e.g. 75, 50 and 25%
  would be needed too.
- The book gives the surface only as a figure, with no formula.

## Equations and numbers

A common way to build the surface (standard Hill-type modeling practice, **not
stated in Winter**) is a product of the two slices, plus the passive term:

$$F(l, v, a) = a \cdot F_0 \cdot f_L(l) \cdot f_V(v) + F_p(l)$$

with $f_L$ the normalized force-length curve (§9.1.1), $f_V$ the normalized
force-velocity curve (§9.2.1–9.2.2), and $F_p$ the parallel element (§9.1.2),
which Fig. 9.13 leaves out.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 239 (PDF 252): text and Fig. 9.13 surface.
- p. 240 (PDF 253): note on per-activation surfaces.

## Relevance to migera

If migera ever adds both an angle-dependent (9.1) and a velocity-dependent
(9.2.1–9.2.2) factor to the ragdoll's torque ceiling, the natural composition is
multiplicative, with `RagdollStrength` playing the part of activation $a$ and
the passive limit torque added **outside** the product so it survives at
strength 0:

`ceiling = max_torque · strength · f_angle(θ) · f_vel(ω·τ̂)` and
`τ_total = clamp(τ_pd, ceiling) + τ_passive(θ)`.

This matches how `RagdollStrength::scale` already multiplies `max_torque`
(`src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`). Not modeled today.
