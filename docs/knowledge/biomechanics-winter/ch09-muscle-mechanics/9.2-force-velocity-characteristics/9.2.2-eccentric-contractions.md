# 9.2.2 Eccentric contractions

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.2.2, pp. 238–239 ·
> [open PDF at p. 238](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=251) ·
> Up: [9.2 Force-velocity characteristics](./INDEX.md)

When an active muscle is forced to lengthen, it resists with **more** force
than it can hold isometrically, rising with lengthening speed to a plateau of
**1.1–1.8 × Fmax**. The curve continues the concentric one across zero
velocity, but no neat equation like Hill's describes it.

## Key ideas

- Much less is known than for concentric contraction, although ordinary
  movement has as much eccentric as concentric activity: level walking
  (ignoring air and ground friction) has **equal positive and negative work**,
  and **downhill** walking is dominated by negative work.
- **Shape depends on protocol** (Fig. 9.12): isotonic eccentric follows the
  solid curves (Winters, 1990) rising to a plateau; **isovelocity** eccentric
  follows the dashed curves (Zahalak, 1990; Sutarno & McGill, 1995), which
  can **fall** at higher stretch speeds — as predicted by Zahalak & Ma's (1990)
  distributed-moment model of Huxley-type cross-bridges.
- **Why force rises:** breaking a cross-bridge link takes more force than
  holding it isometrically; at higher speeds the links "give way", producing
  the plateau. Viscous friction in the surrounding fluid adds resistance.
- **Why data are scarce:** an external motor must out-pull the muscle; safe on
  isolated muscle, dangerous in vivo (could drive a joint past its safe range),
  so foolproof safety stops are needed.

## Equations and numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Eccentric plateau (isotonic) | 1.1 Fmax to 1.8 Fmax (Winters, 1990) |
| Positive vs. negative work, level walking | equal (friction neglected) |

No closed-form eccentric equation is given.

## Relevance to migera

A muscle is **asymmetric**: it can resist being stretched (braking,
landing, being shoved) harder than it can push. migera's PD clamp in
`src/math/pd.rs` is symmetric: the same `max_torque` whether the
joint is being driven toward its target or pushed away from it. Consequences
for the active ragdoll:

- A Hill-style ceiling (see [9.2.1](./9.2.1-concentric-contractions.md)) should
  be extended on the eccentric side: when joint velocity opposes the commanded
  torque ($\omega\cdot\hat\tau < 0$), let the ceiling rise smoothly to
  ~1.1–1.8× — e.g. 1.5× — instead of staying at 1×. A shoved character then
  "gives" but brakes firmly, and landings absorb energy rather than
  collapsing.
- Walking is half negative work; in migera's gait (`src/character/anim/gait.rs`)
  the ragdoll mostly tracks, so this matters chiefly when it's pushed or
  lands.

Not modeled today.
