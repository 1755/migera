# 9.0.3 Size principle

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.0.3, pp. 226–228 ·
> [open PDF at p. 226](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=239) ·
> Up: [9.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Motor units are recruited in order of size (Henneman, 1974a): the smallest
first, each larger one at a higher tension threshold, and released in the
reverse order. The effect is fine force resolution at low force and coarse,
large steps only when high force is needed.

## Key ideas

- **Order:** a newly recruited unit's size grows with the tension at which it
  is recruited. Low-tension tasks are graded in fine steps; high-force tasks
  that do not need precision recruit the big units.
- **Per-unit trajectory** (Fig. 9.3): a unit starts at a base rate and speeds
  up to its maximum; its maximum tension is reached only after the next unit
  has joined. At MVC every unit fires at its maximum rate.
- **Release:** rates fall and the largest units drop out first (DeLuca et al.,
  1982).
- **Action-potential size** grows with unit size (larger motoneuron, larger
  depolarization, more fiber mass), but distance to the electrode also shrinks
  it, so one recording site can never tell a unit's size from its amplitude.

## Equations and numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Firing rate at recruitment (MU 1) | about 5–13 Hz |
| Maximum firing rate | 15–60 Hz |
| Rapid ballistic movements | up to ~120 Hz (Desmedt & Godaux, 1978) |

## Where to read in the book

- p. 227 (PDF 240): size principle statement; Fig. 9.3 staircase of MU 1–4
  thresholds and maximum tensions.
- p. 228 (PDF 241): release order, ballistic rates, action-potential size vs.

## Relevance to migera

The size principle means muscle force resolution is **relative**: small
forces are controlled precisely, large ones coarsely. A PD torque ceiling in
`src/math/pd.rs` has uniform resolution, which is fine for a
simulation with no neural noise. The idea becomes relevant only if migera ever
adds actuator noise or tremor for realism: the book's picture implies noise
proportional to force, not constant.
