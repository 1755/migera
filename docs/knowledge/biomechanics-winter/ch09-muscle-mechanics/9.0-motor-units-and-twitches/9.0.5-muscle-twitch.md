# 9.0.5 The muscle twitch

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.0.5, pp. 228–230 ·
> [open PDF at p. 228](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=241) ·
> Up: [9.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

A single motor-unit twitch has the shape of the **impulse response of a
critically damped second-order system**: the action potential is a
near-instant impulse, and tension rises to a peak at the contraction time T and
decays over about 4T. Every motor unit has this same shape; only T and the
amplitude F0 differ.

## Key ideas

- The shape is the same for all units (Milner-Brown et al., 1973a); what
  varies is **T** (larger for slow-twitch) and **F0** (larger for large
  fast-twitch units).
- The input (motor unit action potential) is short enough to treat as an
  impulse; the output (tension) is a much longer event.
- Contraction time varies widely with the muscle, the person, the stimulation
  technique (voluntary, submaximal or supramaximal) — and **temperature**:
  cooling slows it (slower metabolism, higher viscosity).
- Upper-limb muscles generally have shorter T than leg muscles.

## Equations and numbers

Twitch (Eq. 9.1, checked against the rendered page):

$$F(t) = F_0\,\frac{t}{T}\,e^{-t/T}$$

- $T$ — contraction (twitch) time, the time to peak tension; $F_0$ — a
  constant per motor unit.
- Peak at $t = T$: $F(T) = F_0/e \approx 0.368\,F_0$ (Fig. 9.5 labels the
  axis 0.368 F0).
- Tail (computed from Eq. 9.1): $F(2T) \approx 0.74$, $F(3T) \approx 0.41$,
  $F(4T) \approx 0.20$ of peak; the book calls tension "effective" until about
  **4T**. Fig. 9.5 caption: T ranges from about 20 to 100 ms by unit type.

Contraction times, Buchthal & Schmalbruch (1970), submaximal stimulation, mean
(range):

| Muscle | T |
|---|---|
| Triceps brachii | 44.5 ms (16–68) |
| Biceps brachii | 52.0 ms (16–85) |
| Tibialis anterior | 58.0 ms (38–80) |
| Soleus | 74.0 ms (52–100) |
| Medial gastrocnemius | 79.0 ms (40–110) |

Other reports:

- Biceps: 54 ms at 37 °C → **124 ms at 23 °C** (cooling).
- First dorsal interosseus: 55 ± 12 ms voluntary (Milner-Brown et al., 1973b);
  60 ms supramaximal (Desmedt & Godaux, 1978).
- Desmedt & Godaux (1978), supramaximal: tibialis anterior 84 ms, soleus 100 ms.
- Bellemare et al. (1983), supramaximal: soleus 116 ± 9 ms, biceps 66 ± 9 ms.

The mass–spring–damper that produces this shape, and how T fixes its ratios
(B/M = 2/T, K/B = 1/2T), is in
[9.3.1](../9.3-muscle-modeling/9.3.1-emg-driven-model-example.md).

## Relevance to migera

This is the physiological basis for **activation dynamics**: force cannot
follow a command instantly; it is the command passed through a critically
damped second-order low-pass filter with time-to-peak T.

- **What migera does now:** `Ragdoll::set_strength` and the stun code in
  `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs` change the PD torque ceiling instantly (a
  stun recovers linearly over `recover_secs`). There is no activation filter.
- **Mapping to existing code:** migera's critical springs
  (`src/math/spring.rs`) decay at rate $y = \ln 2/\text{halflife}$,
  and a critically damped impulse response $t\,e^{-yt}$ peaks at $t = 1/y$.
  So a spring with **halflife = T·ln 2** has exactly the twitch shape: T = 50 ms
  ↔ halflife ≈ 35 ms; T = 100 ms ↔ ≈ 69 ms. Passing the commanded strength
  through such a spring would be a one-line activation model.
- **PD comparison:** a critically damped PD at frequency f has
  $\omega = 2\pi f$ and its disturbance impulse response peaks at $1/\omega$;
  migera's 8 Hz joints peak at ≈ 20 ms, the fast-twitch end (see
  [9.0.4](./9.0.4-fast-and-slow-twitch-motor-units.md)). This is an analogy of
  shape, not a claim that the PD loop models muscle.
- **Test oracle:** Eq. 9.1 is closed-form, so any activation filter can be
  tested for peak time T and peak value F0/e against it.
