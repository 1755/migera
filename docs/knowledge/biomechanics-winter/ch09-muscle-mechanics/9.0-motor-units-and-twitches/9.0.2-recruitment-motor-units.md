# 9.0.2 Recruitment of motor units

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.0.2, pp. 225–226 ·
> [open PDF at p. 225](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=238) ·
> Up: [9.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Each motor unit fires all-or-nothing, so a muscle grades its force in only two
ways: fire the active units faster (**rate coding**) or switch on more units
(**recruitment**). Both happen together, and the process runs in reverse when
force drops.

## Key ideas

- One excitation → one **motor unit action potential** (electrical) and one
  **twitch** of tension (mechanical).
- **Rising force** (Fig. 9.2, needle EMG): the first unit fires and speeds up;
  a second, then a third, are recruited. A unit reaches its maximum rate well
  *after* the next unit has been recruited (Erim et al., 1996), so rate coding
  and recruitment overlap.
- **Falling force:** all recruited units slow; the last-recruited drops out
  first, at a rate usually **lower than the rate at which it was recruited**
  (a hysteresis); the rest drop out in reverse recruitment order.
- Firing rate rises **monotonically but nonlinearly** with force from 0 to 100%
  maximum voluntary contraction (MVC).
- Recruitment models cited: Wani & Guha (1975), Milner-Brown & Stein (1975),
  Fuglevand et al. (1993), Erim et al. (1996).

## Relevance to migera

The force a muscle produces is a nonlinear, history-dependent function of the
neural command; there is no single "strength" knob inside the body. migera's
`RagdollStrength` (`src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`) is exactly such a knob: it
scales the PD torque ceiling linearly and instantly. That is a fine
abstraction for games, but note what it leaves out: the dropout hysteresis
(force falls later and lower than it rose) is one reason real muscles turn off
more slowly than they turn on — see
[9.0.6](./9.0.6-shape-graded-contractions.md) for the measured 200 ms on / 300
ms off, which a strength filter could imitate.
