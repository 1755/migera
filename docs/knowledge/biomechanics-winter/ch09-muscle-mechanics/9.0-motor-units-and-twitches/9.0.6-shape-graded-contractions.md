# 9.0.6 Shape of graded contractions

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.0.6, pp. 230–231 ·
> [open PDF at p. 230](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=243) ·
> Up: [9.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

A voluntary contraction's force curve is the sum of twitches under
size-principle recruitment, so even a maximal, rapid effort needs **about 200
ms to turn on and about 300 ms to turn off**. A muscle is slower to relax than
to contract.

## Key ideas

- **Floor on rise time:** even if every unit fired at once at its maximum
  rate, maximum tension could not be reached faster than the muscle's average
  contraction time T ([9.0.5](./9.0.5-muscle-twitch.md)).
- **Recruitment adds delay:** small slow units come first; the large units
  join only after tension has built up in the small ones, so maximum tension
  takes several hundred ms.
- **Relaxation is slower still:** it follows the long trailing edge of the
  twitch (the decay after T lasts to about 4T), plus de-recruitment of units in
  reverse order.
- **Fig. 9.6** (rectus femoris and vastus lateralis EMG with knee-extension
  force): force persists for **about 150 ms after the EMG stops**.

## Equations and numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Time to peak tension, rapid MVC | 200 ms or longer |
| Typical turn-on | ~200 ms |
| Typical turn-off | ~300 ms |
| Tension after EMG ceases | ~150 ms |

A derived rule of thumb (not in the book): the step response of a critically
damped second-order system with time constant T is
$1 - (1 + t/T)\,e^{-t/T}$, which reaches 90% at $t \approx 3.9\,T$. A 200 ms
turn-on thus corresponds to T ≈ 51 ms and a 300 ms turn-off to T ≈ 77 ms —
consistent with the measured contraction times in 9.0.5.

## Relevance to migera

The most directly usable number in §9.0 for the active ragdoll:

- **Strength changes should be rate-limited.** In
  `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`, `set_strength` takes effect on the next
  physics step. A body can't gain full strength in under ~200 ms or lose it in
  under ~300 ms, and force lingers ~150 ms after the command stops. A
  critically damped filter on the effective strength, with a faster rise T
  (~50 ms) than fall (~75 ms), would reproduce this asymmetry.
- **Stun timing sanity check:** `StunResponse::default()` holds a hit's loss
  for 0.25 s, then recovers over 1.2 s. Physiologically, regaining force is
  possible in ~0.2 s, so the 1.2 s recovery is a stylistic (reflex/control)
  choice, not a muscle limit. That's fine, but it should not be justified as
  "muscle rise time".
- migera does not model this lag at all today.
