# 9.0.1 The motor unit

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.0.1, pp. 224–225 ·
> [open PDF at p. 224](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=237) ·
> Up: [9.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

The motor unit — one motor axon and all the muscle fibers it innervates — is
the smallest piece of muscle the nervous system can switch on. Inside it,
force comes from actin–myosin cross-bridges in sarcomeres packed massively in
series and in parallel, wrapped in connective tissue (fascia) that is partly in
series and partly in parallel with that active tissue.

## Key ideas

- **Motor unit** = synaptic junction in the spinal cord's ventral root + motor
  axon + motor end plate(s) on the fibers. Size ranges from **3 fibers to about
  2000** (Feinstein et al., 1955): few short fibers where fine control matters
  (fingers, face, eyes), many long fibers in the large leg muscles.
- **Hierarchy of sizes:** fiber ≈ 100 µm diameter → fibrils ≈ 1 µm → filaments
  ≈ 100 Å.
- **Contractile element (CE):** the actin/myosin cross-bridge structure (Fig.
  9.1: Z lines; A, H and I bands) that generates tension and shortens or
  lengthens as positive or negative work is done.
- **Sarcomere length** (Z line to Z line): about **1.5 µm fully shortened, 2.5
  µm at resting length, 4.0 µm fully lengthened**. These three numbers are the
  anchors of the force-length curve in
  [9.1.1](../9.1-force-length-characteristics/9.1.1-contractile-element-force-length-curve.md).
- **Connective tissue (fascia)** sheaths muscles into layers and groups and
  ends in the tendons. Some is in series with the CE, some in parallel; both are
  modeled as springs and viscous dampers (§9.3).

## Equations and numbers

Worked packing example (a motor unit of 0.1 cm² cross-section, 10 cm resting
length):

- sarcomeres in series: 10 cm / 2.5 µm = **40,000**
- filaments in parallel (each 10⁻⁸ cm²): 0.1 / 10⁻⁸ = **10⁷**
- contractile elements in the unit: **4 × 10¹¹**
