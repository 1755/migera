# 9.0.4 Types of motor units—fast- and slow-twitch classification

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §9.0.4, p. 228 ·
> [open PDF at p. 228](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=241) ·
> Up: [9.0 Introduction](./INDEX.md)

Motor units are classically split into small slow-twitch and large fast-twitch
types that differ in metabolism, peak tension and time to peak — but the
biophysical evidence says motor units form a **continuous spectrum** of size
and excitability rather than two bins.

## Key ideas

| | Slow-twitch | Fast-twitch |
|---|---|---|
| Other names | tonic units, type I | phasic units, type II |
| Size | smaller | larger |
| Metabolism | rich in mitochondria, highly capillarized, aerobic | fewer mitochondria, poorly capillarized, anaerobic |
| Twitch peak tension | low | high |
| Time to peak | **60–120 ms** | **10–50 ms** |
| ATPase stain (Fig. 9.4) | dark | light |
| Action potential at a nearby electrode | smaller | larger |

- Classification criteria differ by discipline: biochemists stain or measure
  metabolism, biomechanists measure twitch force (Milner-Brown et al., 1973b),
  electrophysiologists use EMG.
- Continuum, not classes (Milner-Brown et al., 1973b).

## Where to read in the book

- p. 228 (PDF 241): the full classification text.
- p. 229 (PDF 242): Fig. 9.4, ATPase stain of vastus lateralis showing mixed
  fiber types.

## Relevance to migera

Gives a physiological range for **actuator response time**: 10–120 ms from
command to peak force. A critically damped 8 Hz PD controller (migera's
default in `src/character/anim/ragdoll.rs`) has an impulse response that
peaks at 1/ω ≈ 20 ms, at the fast-twitch end of this range. Slower, postural
joints (spine, neck) could justifiably respond on the slow-twitch 60–120 ms
scale if activation dynamics were ever added; see
[9.0.5](./9.0.5-muscle-twitch.md) for the mapping.
