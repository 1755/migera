# 7.1.4 Dot and cross products

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.1.4, pp. 179–180 ·
> [open PDF at p. 179](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=192) ·
> Up: [7.1 Axes systems](./INDEX.md)

The dot product gives a scalar (only parallel components contribute — e.g.
mechanical power of a force on a moving point); the cross product gives a
vector normal to the plane of its two inputs, which is how the chapter builds
every orthogonal axis from marker positions.

## Equations and numbers

Dot (scalar) product, e.g. power of a force F acting at velocity V:

$$P = \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf V = |F||V|\cos\theta = F_xV_x + F_yV_y + F_zV_z$$

Cross (vector) product, right-hand rule, magnitude |A||B| sin θ:

$$
\mathbf C=\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=
\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf i&\mathbf j&\mathbf k\\A_x&A_y&A_z\\B_x&B_y&B_z\end{vmatrix}
=(A_yB_z-A_zB_y)\,\mathbf i-(A_xB_z-A_zB_x)\,\mathbf j+(A_xB_y-A_yB_x)\,\mathbf k
\quad(7.6)
$$

## Key ideas

- The recipe used in 7.2: two non-parallel vectors a, b in a plane → normal
  n = a × b → third axis n × a (or a × n, chosen for right-handedness). The
  result is orthogonal even if a and b were not; normalise at the end.
- The book's text says dot product was introduced in "Section 6.08"; that is
  §6.0.8, work on an external load.

## Relevance to migera

Nothing new mathematically (glam's `dot`/`cross`), but the frame-from-points
recipe is what a migera test does to get a bone's anatomical frame from FK
joint positions (e.g. knee plane = thigh × shin) — and the ordering of the
cross product fixes the *sign* of the resulting axis, which is exactly what
unsigned measurements miss
([unsigned measurements cannot see direction](../../../engineering-practice/testing/unsigned-measurements-cannot-see-direction.md)).
