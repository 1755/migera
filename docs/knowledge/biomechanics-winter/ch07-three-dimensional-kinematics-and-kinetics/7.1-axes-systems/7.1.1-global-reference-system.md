# 7.1.1 Global reference system

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.1.1, p. 177 ·
> [open PDF at p. 177](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=190) ·
> Up: [7.1 Axes systems](./INDEX.md)

The global reference system (GRS) is fixed in the lab: **X forward/backward,
Y vertical, Z medial/lateral**, so XZ is the horizontal plane. Its axes are
made to coincide with the force plate's by calibrating on the plate.

## Key ideas

- A rigid calibration object (cube or 3D axis frame) carrying markers is set on
  a force plate aligned with the plate's X and Z axes; the markers' positions
  relative to the plate origin are entered, which defines the GRS.
- Extra force plates are located by X/Z offsets from the primary plate, plus
  a Y offset if at a different height (stairs, ramps).
- Fixed-camera clinical labs calibrate once; research labs that move cameras
  must recalibrate, and cameras must not be bumped afterwards.
- Handedness is implied, not stated: the kinematic example
  ([7.2.1](../7.2-marker-and-anatomical-axes/7.2.1-kinematic-data-set-example.md))
  has the subject facing +X, and the *left* leg's lateral malleolus at a
  smaller Z than its medial malleolus — so +Z points to the subject's
  **right**, which makes (X, Y, Z) = (forward, up, right) right-handed.

## Where to read in the book

- p. 177 (PDF 190): the whole subsection (one paragraph); Fig. 3.12 in
  [Chapter 3](../../ch03-kinematics/INDEX.md) shows a fixed clinical camera set-up.

## Relevance to migera

Low on its own (calibration practice), but the axis convention is needed to
use any number in Chapter 7. Bevy is Y-up and `puppet_base.gltf` faces +Z,
so its right is −X. Book → rig: X → +Z, Y → +Y, Z → −X (a 90° yaw, det +1).
A book moment about Z (sagittal, e.g. knee flexor/extensor) is a rig moment
about −X; never copy the sign without the map
([KNEE_AXIS positive swings forward](../../../character-animation/rig-and-retargeting/knee-axis-positive-swings-forward.md)).
