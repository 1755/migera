# 7.1.2 Local reference systems and rotation of axes

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.1.2, pp. 177–179 ·
> [open PDF at p. 177](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=190) ·
> Up: [7.1 Axes systems](./INDEX.md)

Each segment carries a local anatomical frame (origin at its COM, y along the
long axis). The book gets from the global frame to it with three successive
rotations about the moving axes, **x first, then the new y′, then the new z″**
(the "Cardan" sequence), and multiplies the three coordinate-transform matrices
into one [G to A] matrix, Eq. 7.5. Order matters: the product does not commute.

## Key ideas

- **Two local frames per segment.** The *marker* frame (built from three
  surface markers) and the *anatomical* frame (built from bony landmarks,
  origin at the COM, principal y-axis along the long axis; for the pelvis, a
  line through landmarks such as ASIS/PSIS). Getting from global to anatomical
  takes two transformations: global → marker → anatomical
  ([7.2](../7.2-marker-and-anatomical-axes/INDEX.md)).
- **Intrinsic (moving-axis) sequence.** θ₁ about x gives x′y′z′ (x′ = x);
  θ₂ about the *new* y′ gives x″y″z″ (y″ = y′); θ₃ about the *new* z″ gives
  x‴y‴z‴, the target frame (Fig. 7.1).
- **The matrices are passive.** Each [Φ] re-expresses the *same point's
  coordinates* in the rotated axes (a coordinate transform), not a rotation
  of the point. That is why the sines' signs look "transposed" compared with
  the active rotation matrices a game engine uses.
- **Non-commutative.** [Φ₁][Φ₂] ≠ [Φ₂][Φ₁]; Φ₁ must be applied first, so it
  sits rightmost in Eq. 7.4.

## Equations and numbers

Shorthand cᵢ = cos θᵢ, sᵢ = sin θᵢ. A point (x₀, y₀, z₀) in the original axes
has coordinates (x₁, y₁, z₁) in x′y′z′, and so on:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\y_1\\z_1\end{bmatrix}=
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&c_1&s_1\\0&-s_1&c_1\end{bmatrix}}_{[\Phi_1]}
\begin{bmatrix}x_0\\y_0\\z_0\end{bmatrix}\quad(7.1)
\qquad
[\Phi_2]=\begin{bmatrix}c_2&0&-s_2\\0&1&0\\s_2&0&c_2\end{bmatrix}\ (7.2)
\qquad
[\Phi_3]=\begin{bmatrix}c_3&s_3&0\\-s_3&c_3&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\ (7.3)
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_3\\y_3\\z_3\end{bmatrix}=[\Phi_3][\Phi_2][\Phi_1]\begin{bmatrix}x_0\\y_0\\z_0\end{bmatrix}\quad(7.4)
$$

$$
[\Phi_3][\Phi_2][\Phi_1]=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_2c_3 & s_3c_1+s_1s_2c_3 & s_1s_3-c_1s_2c_3\\
-c_2s_3 & c_1c_3-s_1s_2s_3 & s_1c_3+c_1s_2s_3\\
s_2 & -s_1c_2 & c_1c_2
\end{bmatrix}\quad(7.5)
$$

Transcribed from the rendered page (PDF p. 192) and checked numerically.

**Rows of Eq. 7.5 are the new (anatomical) unit axes written in global
coordinates**, so its transpose is the segment's orientation matrix (local →
global), the thing a `Transform.rotation` stores.

### Mapping to engine / library conventions (verified 2026-09-28)

| Book | Equivalent |
|---|---|
| Eq. 7.5 [G to A] | `R.T` where `R = scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation.from_euler('XYZ', [θ1, θ2, θ3]).as_matrix()` (uppercase = intrinsic). Checked equal on 100 random angle triples. |
| Segment orientation (A → G) | `Rotation.from_euler('XYZ', …)`; in Bevy/glam 0.32 `Quat::from_euler(EulerRot::XYZ, θ1, θ2, θ3)` = `from_rotation_x(θ1) * from_rotation_y(θ2) * from_rotation_z(θ3)` (glam's `EulerRot::XYZ` is intrinsic; `XYZEx` is extrinsic). |
| Recover angles | `Rotation.from_matrix(GtoA.T).as_euler('XYZ')` — reproduces the book's frame-6 example to within its 4-digit rounding (−8.89°, −2.71°, −53.26° vs. −8.92°, −2.71°, −53.27°). |

The closed-form inverse used in [7.2.1](../7.2-marker-and-anatomical-axes/7.2.1-kinematic-data-set-example.md):
θ₂ = asin(m₃₁); θ₁ from −s₁c₂ = m₃₂ and c₁c₂ = m₃₃; θ₃ from c₂c₃ = m₁₁ and
−c₂s₃ = m₂₁ — use `atan2`, not `acos` plus sign-checking as the book does by
hand.

### Gimbal lock

Everything divides by c₂: θ₁ and θ₃ are undefined when θ₂ = ±90°, and the
angular-velocity matrix of Eq. 7.7b has determinant exactly c₂
([7.3](../7.3-segment-angular-velocity-and-acceleration.md)). In the book's
global frame (Y vertical) the middle axis y′ is the segment's long axis,
nearly vertical for a leg — so θ₂ is roughly the segment's **heading/axial
rotation relative to the lab**. A walker who turns 90° away from the lab X
axis drives a segment-vs-global decomposition straight into the singularity.
Gait labs avoid it because subjects walk along X.

## Relevance to migera

- **Representation.** migera stores one quaternion per bone
  (`src/character/anim/rig.rs`) and never needs Euler angles to animate, so
  gimbal lock cannot corrupt stored poses. It bites only when a rotation is
  *decomposed*: joint-limit checks, studio sliders, logs, or comparing against
  published gait-lab angle curves. Decompose the **joint** (child relative to
  parent) rotation, not the segment-vs-world one, so the heading singularity
  above never appears while the character turns.
- **Axis mapping.** Winter's lab frame is X forward, Y up, Z to the subject's
  right. `puppet_base.gltf` faces +Z with Y up, so its right is −X. The map
  book→rig is X→+Z, Y→+Y, Z→−X (a proper 90° yaw, det +1). Under it the
  book's x–y′–z″ sequence becomes the rig's **Z–Y′–X″ with θ₃ negated**:
  `M·R_book·Mᵀ == from_euler('ZYX', [θ1, θ2, −θ3])` (checked in scipy). Book
  θ₃ (about the mediolateral axis) is the sagittal flexion angle — rig X is
  `stance::KNEE_AXIS`, and the sign flip is exactly the facing trap in
  [KNEE_AXIS positive swings forward](../../../character-animation/rig-and-retargeting/knee-axis-positive-swings-forward.md).
- **Frame naming.** A migera `LocalPose` delta names a *world* (T-pose)
  axis and is conjugated into the bone's bind frame
  ([a pose delta names a world axis](../../../character-animation/rig-and-retargeting/a-pose-delta-names-a-world-axis.md),
  [conjugate pose deltas by the bind rotation](../../../character-animation/rig-and-retargeting/conjugate-pose-deltas-by-the-bind-rotation.md)).
  Winter's θᵢ are about *moving* segment axes. Before comparing a migera delta
  with a book angle, state which frame each is in; conjugating by the bind is
  the same "passive vs active / which frame" bookkeeping as Eq. 7.5's
  transpose.
- **Test oracle.** For any decomposition or composition code, compare against
  `scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation` rather than a re-derivation of Eq. 7.5
  ([same function both sides is a vacuous test](../../../engineering-practice/testing/same-function-both-sides-is-a-vacuous-test.md)),
  and use deltas whose axes are *not* parallel to the bind so order errors
  show.
