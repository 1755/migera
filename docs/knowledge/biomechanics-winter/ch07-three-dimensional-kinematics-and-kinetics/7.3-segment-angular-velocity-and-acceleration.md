# 7.3 Determination of segment angular velocities and accelerations

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.3, pp. 187–188 ·
> [open PDF at p. 187](../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=200) ·
> Up: [Chapter 7 — Three-dimensional kinematics and kinetics](./INDEX.md)

The rates θ̇₁, θ̇₂, θ̇₃ are rotation rates about three *different, non-orthogonal*
axes (x, y′, z″), so they are not the angular-velocity components. Each rate
is carried through the later rotations into the anatomical frame and summed,
giving Eq. 7.7b. Angular accelerations are then finite differences of those ω
components.

## Equations and numbers

$$\boldsymbol\omega = \dot\theta_1\,\mathbf e_x + \dot\theta_2\,\mathbf e_{y'} + \dot\theta_3\,\mathbf e_{z''}\quad(7.7a)$$

Build-up (each stage adds a new rate, then rotates the running sum with the
next [Φ]):

$$
\boldsymbol\omega'=\begin{bmatrix}\dot\theta_1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad
\boldsymbol\omega''=\begin{bmatrix}0\\\dot\theta_2\\0\end{bmatrix}+[\Phi_2]\boldsymbol\omega'=\begin{bmatrix}c_2\dot\theta_1\\\dot\theta_2\\s_2\dot\theta_1\end{bmatrix},\quad
\boldsymbol\omega'''=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\\dot\theta_3\end{bmatrix}+[\Phi_3]\boldsymbol\omega''=
\begin{bmatrix}c_3c_2\dot\theta_1+s_3\dot\theta_2\\-s_3c_2\dot\theta_1+c_3\dot\theta_2\\s_2\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_3\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol\omega=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_x\\\omega_y\\\omega_z\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}c_2c_3&s_3&0\\-c_2s_3&c_3&0\\s_2&0&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\theta_1\\\dot\theta_2\\\dot\theta_3\end{bmatrix}\quad(7.7b)
$$

Components are in the **anatomical (body) frame** — what Euler's equations
([7.4.2](./7.4-kinetic-analysis-reactions-and-moments/7.4.2-euler-3d-equations-of-motion.md))
need. Checked 2026-09-28: for the book's frame 6 (θ = −0.14781, −0.02501,
−0.46053 rad; θ̇ = −0.0911, −0.1048, −2.909 rad/s) Eq. 7.7b gives
(−0.0350, −0.1344, −2.9067) rad/s, identical to scipy's body-frame
`(R(θ)⁻¹·R(θ+θ̇h)).as_rotvec()/h`.

- **Singularity:** det of the 7.7b matrix = c₂. At θ₂ = ±90° angle rates
  cannot be recovered from ω (gimbal lock).
- θ̇ by first central difference (Eq. 3.15); α by central difference of ω
  (Eq. 3.17) or of angles (Eq. 3.18c). The book's worked α
  ([7.4.3](./7.4-kinetic-analysis-reactions-and-moments/7.4.3-kinetic-data-set-example.md))
  differences ω, not θ̇ — the correct choice, since α ≠ E·θ̈ (E changes with θ).

## Relevance to migera

- migera never differentiates Euler angles: it works in the quaternion
  tangent space (`quat_log`/`rotation_delta` in
  `src/math/quat_ext.rs`), whose finite difference *is* ω.
  Eq. 7.7b is the reason that is the right design: angle rates would need a
  sequence-specific, singular Jacobian.
- If gait-lab joint-angle curves are ever imported as targets, convert them
  to quaternions first and differentiate those; do not feed θ̇ to a spring or
  PD controller as if it were ω.
- **Frame check for the ragdoll.** Eq. 7.7b is a *body-frame* ω; avian and
  `math/pd.rs` use *world-space* angular velocity. ω_world = R·ω_body.
  Mixing the two is a silent error that vanishes whenever the body is near
  identity — the same class as
  [a pose delta names a world axis](../../character-animation/rig-and-retargeting/a-pose-delta-names-a-world-axis.md).
- Oracle: the scipy `as_rotvec` difference above is an independent check for
  any angular-velocity helper.
