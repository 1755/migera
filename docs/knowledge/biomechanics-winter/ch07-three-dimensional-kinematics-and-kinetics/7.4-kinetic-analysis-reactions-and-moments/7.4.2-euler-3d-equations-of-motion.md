# 7.4.2 Euler's three-dimensional equations of motion for a segment

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.4.2, pp. 189–191 ·
> [open PDF at p. 189](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=202) ·
> Up: [7.4 Kinetic analysis of reaction forces and moments](./INDEX.md)

Written about the segment's principal axes through its COM, the rotational
equations stay diagonal: each axis has the 2D form Iα = ΣM plus one coupling
term (I_a − I_b)ω_aω_b from spin about the other two axes. The unknowns are
the three proximal moments.

## Equations and numbers

Transcribed from the rendered page (PDF p. 203), x–y–z = anatomical axes,
y = long axis:

$$I_x\alpha_x + (I_z-I_y)\,\omega_y\omega_z = \textstyle\sum M_x = R_{zd}\,l_d + R_{zp}\,l_p + M_{xp} - M_{xd}\qquad(7.9a)$$
$$I_y\alpha_y + (I_x-I_z)\,\omega_x\omega_z = \textstyle\sum M_y = M_{yp} - M_{yd}\qquad(7.9b)$$
$$I_z\alpha_z + (I_y-I_x)\,\omega_x\omega_y = \textstyle\sum M_z = -R_{xd}\,l_d - R_{xp}\,l_p + M_{zp} - M_{zd}\qquad(7.9c)$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I_x, I_y, I_z | principal moments of inertia about x, y, z (kg·m²) |
| ω, α | body-frame angular velocity / acceleration ([Eq. 7.7b](../7.3-segment-angular-velocity-and-acceleration.md)) |
| R_{·d}, R_{·p} | distal/proximal reaction forces, already rotated to anatomical axes |
| M_{·d} | distal moments, rotated to anatomical axes |
| l_p, l_d | COM → proximal / distal joint distances along y |

- Reaction forces have **zero moment arm about the long axis** (they act
  through joint centres on y), so 7.9b contains only moments.
- The book says Eqs 7.9 have the form of the "2D Equation (4.3)"; that
  cross-reference is wrong in the book (Eq. 4.3 is the multisegment COM). The
  2D moment equation is in [§5.1](../../ch05-kinetics-forces-and-moments/5.1-link-segment-equations-free-body-diagram.md).
- Standard form of the coupling (general Euler equations) is
  I₁α₁ − (I₂ − I₃)ω₂ω₃ = M₁ — identical to 7.9a with the sign moved.
- **Size of the coupling in walking** (book's frame-6 shank, I_x = I_z =
  0.0138, I_y = 0.0024 kg·m², ω = (−0.035, −0.134, −2.91) rad/s):
  (I_z − I_y)ω_yω_z = 0.0045 N·m versus M_xp = 42.35 N·m — about 0.01 %.
  For a symmetric segment (I_x = I_z) the axial coupling in 7.9b vanishes
  exactly. The transverse terms scale with ω_axial·ω_transverse: with
  20 rad/s of twist about the long axis while swinging at 20 rad/s, the same
  shank sees (0.0138 − 0.0024)·20·20 ≈ 4.6 N·m.

## Relevance to migera

- **Ragdoll gyroscopic behaviour.** The (I_a − I_b)ωω terms are what make a
  tumbling limb precess. Capsule bodies are axisymmetric (I_x = I_z ≫ I_y),
  so the *long-axis* equation has no coupling, but a limb that twists about
  its bone axis while swinging (a thrown, spinning ragdoll) gets transverse
  gyroscopic torque (I_t − I_axial)·ω_axial·ω_transverse. At walking rates it is negligible (0.01 % above), so a PD-driven
  walking ragdoll that misbehaves is not suffering from missing gyroscopic
  terms — look at gains, frames and limits first
  ([PD damping bound](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/pd-damping-explicit-integration-bound.md)).
- **Why PD outputs acceleration.** migera's `math/pd.rs` and
  `ragdoll.rs` emit angular *acceleration* and avian applies it via
  `apply_angular_acceleration`, i.e. they prescribe α directly, bypassing
  the inertia tensor
  ([avian: apply_angular_acceleration, not apply_torque](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/avian-apply-angular-acceleration-not-torque.md)).
  Eq. 7.9 shows what that shortcut drops: the coupling term and the
  anisotropy of I. Both are small for the gains and speeds in use.
- **Torque plausibility oracle.** Feeding a segment's recorded ω, α and
  joint forces into 7.9 gives the joint moment a real human would need;
  comparing ragdoll joint torques against human-scale magnitudes
  ([7.4.5](./7.4.5-sample-moment-and-power-curves.md)) is a sanity bound for
  `max_torque` limits.
- Principal inertias per segment come from anthropometry (Chapter 4 radius of
  gyration); the shank values here (3.22 kg, 0.0138/0.0024 kg·m²) are one
  realistic set.
