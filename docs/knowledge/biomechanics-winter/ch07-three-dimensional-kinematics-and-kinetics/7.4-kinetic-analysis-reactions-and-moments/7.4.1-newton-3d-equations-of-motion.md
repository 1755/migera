# 7.4.1 Newtonian three-dimensional equations of motion for a segment

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.4.1, p. 189 ·
> [open PDF at p. 189](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=202) ·
> Up: [7.4 Kinetic analysis of reaction forces and moments](./INDEX.md)

Forces are solved in the **global** frame (gravity, force-plate data and COM
accelerations all live there); moments are solved in the **anatomical** frame
(where the inertia tensor is diagonal). So the Newton step is the 2D
free-body equation with a Z row added, followed by rotating the results into
the segment frame.

## Equations and numbers

Distal reactions R_{·D} (known: force plate or the segment below) act opposite
to the proximal ones R_{·P}, the same sign convention as 2D §5.1. a = COM
acceleration in the GRS.

$$\textstyle\sum F_X = ma_X:\quad R_{XP}-R_{XD}=ma_X\qquad(7.8a)$$
$$\textstyle\sum F_Y = ma_Y:\quad R_{YP}-R_{YD}-mg=ma_Y\qquad(7.8b)$$
$$\textstyle\sum F_Z = ma_Z:\quad R_{ZP}-R_{ZD}=ma_Z\qquad(7.8c)$$

- **Step 1:** solve 7.8 for R_XP, R_YP, R_ZP (global).
- **Step 2:** rotate proximal and distal reaction forces into anatomical axes:
  R_xp… = [G to A]·R_XP… (Eq. 7.5 with this frame's θ₁, θ₂, θ₃).
- **Step 3:** rotate the distal moments, previously in global, the same way:
  M_xd, M_yd, M_zd. Then Euler's equations give the proximal moments
  ([7.4.2](./7.4.2-euler-3d-equations-of-motion.md)).

## Relevance to migera

- The migera ragdoll is a *forward* simulation (avian integrates these same
  equations), so this is not code to write — but it is the oracle for
  checking it: record a body's COM acceleration and the joint forces avian
  reports, and ΣF = ma per body must close. A body whose joint forces do not
  balance its weight in quiet stance is mis-massed or mis-anchored
  ([ragdoll body and anchor frames](../../../character-animation/ragdoll-and-physics/ragdoll-body-and-anchor-frames.md)).
- "Forces in world, moments in the body frame" is the right split for any
  per-bone dynamics helper migera adds.
