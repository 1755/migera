# 7.4.5 Sample moment and power curves

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.4.5, pp. 195–198 ·
> [open PDF at p. 195](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=208) ·
> Up: [7.4 Kinetic analysis of reaction forces and moments](./INDEX.md)

Averaged 3D joint moments and powers for normal walking (heel contact 0 %,
toe-off 60 % of stride), normalised by body mass. Sagittal-plane ankle
plantarflexion is by far the largest moment and power; the hip abductors
(frontal) and hip rotators (transverse) are the key out-of-plane actors.

## Key ideas

Sign conventions: sagittal **extensor** +, transverse **external rotation** +,
frontal **evertor/abductor** +; power generation +, absorption −. Burst labels:
joint letter (A/K/H), number, plane (S/F/T).

1. **Ankle, sagittal** — small dorsiflexor moment right after HC lowers the
   foot; then a rising plantarflexor moment peaking near 50 % stride drives
   push-off: **A2-S**, the largest power burst in the stride.
2. **Knee, sagittal** — extensors 8–25 % control flexion under load
   (**K1-S** absorption); moment turns flexor (a by-product of gastrocnemius
   adding to plantarflexion); small extensor around TO limits knee flexion
   (**K3-S** absorption); flexor burst before HC decelerates the swinging leg
   (**K4-S** absorption).
3. **Hip, sagittal** — extensor in first half of stance (stops the trunk
   pitching forward, helps the knee, "push from behind": **H1-S**
   generation); flexor in second half (stops the trunk pitching back, then
   "pull-off" of the thigh into swing at 50–75 %: **H3-S** generation).
4. **Hip, transverse** — stance-limb external rotators brake pelvis/trunk
   yaw over the stance limb (**H1-T** absorption); internal rotators then
   stabilise the forward rotation of pelvis and swing limb.
5. **Hip, frontal** — strong abductor pattern: body weight acts ~10 cm
   medial to the stance hip. **H1-F** absorption as the pelvis drops, then
   **H2-F/H3-F** generation lifting pelvis, trunk and swing limb for toe
   clearance. The **knee** shows a similar abductor moment, but it is passive:
   medial condyle loading / lateral unloading, not muscle — ligaments and
   bone shape can supply joint moments.

## Equations and numbers

Approximate peaks **read off Figs 7.4–7.5 by eye** (±20 %; the book prints no
table):

| Quantity | Peak | When |
|---|---|---|
| Ankle plantarflexor moment | ≈ 1.6 N·m/kg | ~45–50 % |
| Hip extensor / flexor moment | ≈ +1.0 / −0.9 N·m/kg | ~5–10 % / ~50 % |
| Knee extensor moment | ≈ 0.5 N·m/kg | ~12 % |
| Hip abductor moment | ≈ 1 N·m/kg, two humps | ~12 % and ~45 % |
| Knee abductor moment | ≈ 0.6 N·m/kg, two humps | ~12 % and ~45 % |
| Hip external rotation moment | ≈ 0.2 N·m/kg | early stance |
| A2-S ankle generation | ≈ 3.5–4 W/kg | ~50 % |
| H1-S / H3-S hip generation | ≈ 0.7–0.9 W/kg | early stance / ~55 % |
| H1-F hip absorption | ≈ −0.7 W/kg | ~10 % |

Axis limits printed on the plots (useful as upper bounds): ankle sagittal
moment 2.00 N·m/kg, hip sagittal 2.0, hip frontal 2.00, knee frontal 1.50;
ankle sagittal power 6.0 W/kg, hip sagittal 2.5.

## Relevance to migera

- **Gait timing targets** for `gait.rs`/`locomotion.rs`: push-off power at
  ~50 % stride, thigh pull-off at 50–75 %, swing-leg braking just before HC.
  A procedural walk whose pelvis and leg motion ignore these phases looks
  weightless.
- **Pelvis frontal/transverse behaviour** for `pelvis.rs`: pelvic drop on the
  swing side in early stance, braked by hip abductors (H1-F), then lift for
  toe clearance; pelvic yaw braked by stance hip rotators.
- **Ragdoll torque budgets:** per-kg peaks × body mass give human-scale
  joint torques (e.g. ankle ≈ 1.6 × 70 ≈ 110 N·m) to sanity-check ragdoll
  `max_torque` limits (which migera expresses as angular acceleration — convert
  via the body's inertia before comparing).
- Passive knee abductor moment is a reminder that a ragdoll joint's limits
  (not just its motor) carry load.
