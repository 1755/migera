# 7.2.1 Example of a kinematic data set

> **Source:** Winter (2009) §7.2.1, pp. 183–187 ·
> [open PDF at p. 183](../../../../books/bmcoh/BIOMECHANICS_AND_MOTOR_CONTROL_OF_HUMAN.pdf#page=196) ·
> Up: [7.2 Marker and anatomical axes systems](./INDEX.md)

A complete numeric walk-through for the left leg (shank): build the
anatomical frame from a standing calibration, express the tracking markers in
it, derive the constant marker→anatomical matrix, then for a swing-phase frame
build the global→marker matrix, multiply to global→anatomical, and solve
Eq. 7.5 for the three Cardan angles and the COM position. I re-ran every step
in numpy/scipy; the book's results hold to its 3–4 digit rounding, with the
typos listed below.

## Key ideas

1. **Calibration (7.2.1.1).** Subject faces +X, left leg. Ankle joint =
   midpoint of the malleoli (mT2, mC1); knee = midpoint of fibular head and
   medial condyle (mT3, mC2); COM = 0.567·knee + 0.433·ankle (so the COM is
   0.433 L from the knee, 0.567 L from the ankle).
2. **Anatomical axes by cross products.** y = knee − ankle; interim z =
   medial − lateral malleolus; x = y × z; corrected z = x × y (now exactly ⟂);
   normalise. Columns (x, y, z) form [LA to G]; its transpose is [LG to A].
3. **Local coordinates.** Move the origin to the COM; rotate each landmark's
   (global − COM) vector by [LG to A]. Ankle → (0, −18.15, 0) cm, knee →
   (0, 13.86, 0) cm: both on the long axis, as they must be — a built-in check.
4. **Marker frame in anatomical coordinates.** Origin mT2; z_m = mT3 − mT2;
   interim a = mT1 − mT2; y_m = z_m × a; x_m = y_m × z_m. This gives the
   constant [LM to A] (calibration is then discarded; markers stay).
5. **Per frame (7.2.1.2).** Same construction from the global marker
   positions gives [G to M] (rows = marker axes in global coordinates).
6. **(7.2.1.3)** [G to A] = [M to A]·[G to M]; solve Eq. 7.5 for θ₁, θ₂, θ₃;
   COM: c (anatomical) = −(anatomical mT2), c_global = [A to G]·c, and
   R_c = R_m + c with R_m = global mT2.

## Equations and numbers

**Table 7.1 — calibration markers, GRS (cm).**

| Marker | Location | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mT1 | mid-leg (tibia anterior) | 9.39 | 30.02 | 21.90 |
| mT2 | lateral malleolus | 2.92 | 10.10 | 18.85 |
| mT3 | fibular head | 5.05 | 41.90 | 15.41 |
| mC1 | medial malleolus | 2.71 | 10.22 | 26.52 |
| mC2 | medial condyle | 8.29 | 41.88 | 26.52 |

Derived: ankle (2.815, 10.16, 22.685); knee (6.67, 41.89, 20.965);
COM (5.001, 28.151, 21.710).

$$
[LG\to A]=\begin{bmatrix}0.9925&-0.1190&0.0290\\0.1204&0.9913&-0.0537\\-0.0225&0.0568&0.9981\end{bmatrix}
$$

Anatomical (COM-origin, cm): mT1 (4.139, 2.371, 0.197); mT2 (0.000, −17.991,
−3.833); mT3 (−1.770, 13.974, −5.508).

$$
[LM\to A]=\begin{bmatrix}0.7164&0.6954&-0.0552\\0.0760&0.0008&0.9971\\0.6935&-0.7186&-0.0522\end{bmatrix}
$$

Frame 6:

$$
[G\to M]=\begin{bmatrix}0.4561&-0.4117&0.7889\\0.4501&-0.6580&-0.6037\\0.7677&0.6305&-0.1148\end{bmatrix},\quad
[G\to A]=\begin{bmatrix}0.5974&-0.7873&0.1515\\0.8000&0.5969&-0.0550\\-0.0472&0.1544&0.9868\end{bmatrix}
$$

Solve with the bottom row s₂ = −0.0472, −s₁c₂ = 0.1544, c₁c₂ = 0.9868, then
column 1: c₂c₃ = 0.5974, −c₂s₃ = 0.8000.

| Frame | θ₁ | θ₂ | θ₃ | R_c (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | −8.97° | −1.31° | −56.02° | (16.120, 36.325, 34.697) |
| 6 | −8.92° | −2.71° | −53.27° | (20.812, 36.646, 34.653) |
| 7 | −8.56° | −4.08° | −49.89° | (25.429, 36.818, 34.623) |

scipy (`Rotation.from_matrix(GA.T).as_euler('XYZ', degrees=True)`) gives
frame 5 (−8.97, −1.31, −56.01), frame 6 (−8.89, −2.71, −53.26), frame 7
(−8.54, −4.07, −49.88); R_c matches to ≤0.01 cm. θ₃ (sagittal) is large
because the shank is swinging.

**Typos in the printed example (confirmed by recomputation):**
- p. 183: interim z_an y-component printed 0.21; it is 0.12 (10.22 − 10.10).
- p. 184: unit x_an z-component printed "0.290"; it is 0.0290.
- p. 186: in the [M to A]·[G to M] product the first factor shows −0.9971 and
  0.7186 (should be +0.9971, −0.7186) and the second shows 0.7887 and +0.6037
  (should be 0.7889, −0.6037). The printed *result* is correct.
- §7.2 text says R_m is marker mT1, but the example's marker origin (and the
  arithmetic) uses mT2.

## Relevance to migera

- **Known-answer fixture.** Table 7.1/7.2 in, angles and R_c out: a ready
  test for any frame-from-points or quaternion→Euler helper, independent of
  migera's own math. Use the scipy numbers above as the oracle (they are
  unrounded), not the book's hand-rounded ones.
- **Recipe for measuring a bone frame on the real rig.** The same steps
  (long axis from two joint centres, a second landmark for the plane, cross
  products, re-orthogonalise) give a signed anatomical frame from FK joint
  positions, e.g. to check knee-plane direction in tests
  ([unsigned measurements cannot see direction](../../../engineering-practice/testing/unsigned-measurements-cannot-see-direction.md)).
- The "joints must land on the long axis with ≈0 x/z" check is the
  structural-invariant style of test CLAUDE.md asks for before screenshots.
